# The Fazit-Stiftung — How a Consortium of German Corporations Created a Foundation to Own a Newspaper and Shield It from the Market

---

## 1949 — The Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung Is Born

On November 1, 1949, a group of journalists, academics, and businessmen published the first issue of the *Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung* (FAZ). Germany had been a Federal Republic for only six months. The FAZ was not the refoundation of anything, though it drew on traditions of the old *Frankfurter Zeitung* (1856–1943), shut down by the Nazis. The design was deliberately austere: for decades no photographs on the front page, Gothic typography for headlines. The bet was clear from day one: a quality newspaper for the elites.

---

## 1950 — The Gesellschaft zur Förderung der FAZ and the Founding Consortium

In 1950, the *Gesellschaft zur Förderung der Frankfurter Allgemeinen Zeitung* (Society for the Promotion of the FAZ) was constituted, the embryo of what nine years later would be the definitive foundation. Two things distinguished this arrangement: First, the financiers were not a family or an individual magnate, but a **consortium of corporations**. Second, the structure was designed from the start to separate financial ownership from editorial control.

---

## 1959 — The Fazit-Stiftung Is Founded

The *Fazit-Stiftung gemeinnützige Verlagsgesellschaft mbH* was formally constituted in Frankfurt am Main in 1959. The name "Fazit" was an acronym for *Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung* + *Stiftung*, but also a play on words: *Fazit* means "balance" or "bottom line" — the same term that closes a financial analysis or a well-argued editorial.

The founding entities were: the **Frankfurt Chamber of Commerce and Industry (IHK Frankfurt)**, the **Bundesverband deutscher Banken** (Association of German Banks), the **Verband der Chemischen Industrie** (Federation of the Chemical Industry), and a **consortium of businessmen** that included the largest corporations of Germany's *Wirtschaftswunder*: **Allianz**, **Deutsche Bank**, **Hoechst AG**, **Siemens**, **Daimler-Benz**. The founding capital was approximately 2 million Deutschmarks.

The shares of *Frankfurter Allgemeine Zeitung GmbH* remained in the hands of the foundation. They are not listed on the stock exchange. They cannot be sold to third parties. Profits are reinvested in the newspaper or used for charitable purposes. There are no dividends to distribute to shareholders, because there are no shareholders.

The Fazit-Stiftung inaugurated in 1959 the legal form that the Krupp-Stiftung (1968) and the Bertelsmann Stiftung (1977) would later replicate: a foundation that converts personal, family, or corporate power into institutional perpetuity.

---

## The Five Herausgeber: Editorial Government by Consensus

The FAZ has no editor-in-chief. It has *Herausgeber* (publisher-editors). For most of its history there have been five, who make all editorial decisions by consensus. Each Herausgeber has veto power. The Herausgeber are not elected by newsroom journalists or readers. They are appointed by the existing Herausgeber with the approval of the foundation's Stiftungsrat.

---

## The Stiftungsrat: Who Commands in the Shadows

The Stiftungsrat (Foundation Council) is the supreme body of the Fazit-Stiftung. Its members are not elected. They are appointed by the council itself — co-optation — or designated by the founding institutions. Historically, the Stiftungsrat has been composed of a who's who of the German establishment: former presidents of the Bundesbank, former federal finance ministers, presidents of the BDI (Federation of German Industries), directors of Deutsche Bank, and lawyers from the major Frankfurt firms.

**Sabine Lautenschläger**, former member of the ECB Executive Board and the Bundesbank, is the most visible example of this constellation of interests. She simultaneously sits on the Board of the Alfried Krupp von Bohlen und Halbach-Stiftung, which controls 20.93% of ThyssenKrupp.

---

## Frankfurt: The Capital Without a Capital

The FAZ is headquartered in Frankfurt am Main. Frankfurt is Germany's financial capital: home to the ECB, the Bundesbank, the Deutsche Börse, Deutsche Bank, and Commerzbank. It is also the capital of Hesse, governed for eleven years (1999–2010) by **Roland Koch**, the CDU politician who in 2009 led the veto against ZDF editor-in-chief Nikolaus Brender in the public television's Verwaltungsrat.

---

## How Independence Works — and How It Doesn't

The FAZ is undoubtedly one of Europe's most independent newspapers. No government can call its editors. No advertiser can threaten. No magnate can buy headlines and change editorial direction. But that independence is of a very specific type: independence from the market, dependence on the establishment that founded it.

The FAZ is not independent of the consensus of the German corporate elite because it does not need to be: it shares its premises. It defends the market economy, fiscal discipline, the independence of the Bundesbank, NATO, European integration. These positions are not imposed by the Stiftungsrat: they are the genuine convictions of its Herausgeber and newsroom.

---

## HA Hessen: The FAZ as a Private News Agency

A rarely discussed dimension of the FAZ's power is its function as a content provider for dozens of regional newspapers through the *HA Hessen* (*Redaktionsgemeinschaft Hessen*). The FAZ produces reports, analyses, and complete supplements published in local newspapers in Hesse. In practice, the FAZ is not just a quality national newspaper: it is also a private news agency feeding its editorial line into an ecosystem of local media.

---

## The Institutional Paradox

The Fazit-Stiftung is simultaneously the most virtuous newspaper ownership model in Germany and one of the most opaque. It is virtuous because it protects the newsroom from commercial and political pressures. It is opaque because the mechanisms that guarantee that freedom — co-opted Stiftungsrat, self-referential Herausgeber — are exactly the same ones that guarantee the editorial line never deviates from the establishment consensus.

---

## The Foundation as Meta-Institution

The Fazit-Stiftung belongs to a recurring pattern in German institutional history: the use of the foundation not to escape power, but to perpetuate it under an intangible legal form. The **Wittelsbacher Ausgleichsfonds** (1923) was the pioneer. The **Fazit-Stiftung** (1959) adapted the model to corporate power. The **Krupp-Stiftung** (1968) took it to industrial power. The **Bertelsmann Stiftung** (1977) applied it to media and family power.

Four foundations, four assets of radically different natures — palaces, a newspaper, a steelworks with submarines, a media empire — and yet the same legal architecture, the same mechanism of self-referential governance, and the same result: power perpetuates itself without passing through the market, without passing through the ballot box.

The FAZ is a unique link in this chain because it is precisely the medium that should investigate and expose this web. The ultimate paradox of the Fazit-Stiftung is not that a consortium of corporations controls Germany's most influential newspaper. It is that Germany's most influential newspaper is itself the living proof of the model it should scrutinize.
