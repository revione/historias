## 1. The name that isn't the original one

Benjamin was not born Netanyahu. The surname is an adoption.

The family's original name was **Mileikowsky**. His great-grandfather, **Nathan Mileikowsky**, was a rabbi and Zionist activist born in the Russian Empire, in territory that is now Belarus. In the late nineteenth century he began actively campaigning for Zionism, traveling through Russia speaking Hebrew instead of Yiddish — unusual for the time — and participating in the early Zionist Congresses, where he opposed the Uganda Plan, which proposed establishing a Jewish state in central Africa rather than in Palestine.

In 1908, Nathan moved to Poland. In 1920, after World War I, he emigrated with his family to Palestine, then under the British Mandate. There he began signing his articles with the pen name **Netanyahu**, which in Hebrew means "God gave." The name change was part of a broader phenomenon among Zionist immigrants: Hebraizing surnames as an act of identity and rupture with the European exile.

His son, **Benzion Mileikowsky**, also adopted the Netanyahu surname upon arriving in Palestine. Benzion is Benjamin's father.

## 2. The great-grandfather: from Siberia to Warsaw, from Warsaw to Jerusalem

**Nathan Mileikowsky** (1879–1935) is the founding figure of this family story.

He was an ordained rabbi, but never served as a sedentary parish priest. While still at the Volozhin Yeshiva he began giving public speeches, and by age 20 he was already preaching Zionism in Siberia — in remote communities far from the imperial center — sent by Zionist leader Yechiel Chlenov. Zionism at the time was not the dominant movement among Jews: most rabbis rejected it, the Bundists (Jewish socialists) fought it, and many simply didn't believe it was realistic. Nathan was one of those who traveled from community to community trying to convince.

There is an episode that Benjamin has recounted repeatedly in international speeches, including before the UN:

> "One cold day in the late nineteenth century, my grandfather Nathan and his younger brother were standing at a train station in the heart of Europe. A group of antisemites ran toward them screaming 'death to the Jews.' My grandfather understood at that moment that Jews had no future in Europe."

### Why they moved to Poland (1908)

Nathan moved to Warsaw because he was offered a specific position: director of the **Hebrew Gymnasium of Mordechai Yaakov Krinsky**, a Jewish educational institution in the Polish capital. It was not exile or flight: it was a professional and strategic move.

Warsaw was the epicenter of Jewish intellectual life in Eastern Europe. It concentrated Hebrew and Yiddish newspapers, political movements of all tendencies, and was where the future of European Judaism was being contested. For Nathan, who combined a rabbinic career with Zionist activism, it was where he could have the most impact.

The Hebrew Gymnasium was a deliberate project: teaching in Hebrew at a time when Polish Jews spoke Yiddish at home and Polish or Russian outside. Hebrew, for the Zionists, was not just a religious language but the language of the future state. Teaching in Hebrew was a political act. His son Benzion was born in Warsaw in 1910, when the family had already been there two years.

In 1912 they moved to Lodz, where Nathan served as Maggid — a preacher — at the Zionist synagogue Beth Jacob. In 1914 he was appointed rabbi of Rivne (in present-day Ukraine), but World War I prevented the move.

### What Nathan wrote

Nathan published articles in the Hebrew press — primarily in Ha-Tsefirah and other Zionist organs — about the return to Palestine, the critique of the Bund, and the defense of Hebrew as a living language.

In 1928 he published **"Am u-Medinah"** (Nation and State), a collection of his speeches: Jews are a nation, and a nation needs a state. The book reflects the right-wing Revisionist Zionism he belonged to: not the gradual compromises of Labor Zionism, but a direct claim to sovereignty. That ideological line is what Benzion intensified and Benjamin turned into government policy.

### What he left in Palestine

In 1920 he arrived with his wife and nine children. They first settled in Rosh Pina (Galilee). In 1922 they moved to the Beit HaKerem neighborhood of Jerusalem. In 1924 he traveled to London to raise funds for the Jewish National Fund; the success of that tour led him to the United States, where between 1925 and 1928 he gave, according to a Yiddish newspaper, 700 lectures in nine months.

He died of diphtheria in Jerusalem on February 4, 1935, at age 55. Chief Rabbi Abraham Isaac Kook — the intellectual father of religious Zionism — called him a "divine speaker" in his eulogy. Today **Nathan Mileikowsky Square** exists in Jerusalem, next to the National Cemetery at Mount Herzl.

## 3. The father: the historian who defined the family ideology

**Benzion Netanyahu** (1910–2012) is the figure who most profoundly shaped Benjamin.

He was born in Warsaw. He arrived in Palestine at age 10, studied at the Hebrew University of Jerusalem, and became one of the most recognized historians of Judaism in the twentieth century, specializing in the history of the Jews in Spain and the Spanish Inquisition.

### The academic

His most important work, **"The Origins of the Inquisition in Fifteenth Century Spain"**, is considered by some a monumental contribution to the field. His central thesis: that antisemitism in medieval Spain was not primarily religious — a reaction against conversos suspected of secretly continuing to practice Judaism — but racial. The inquisitors were persecuting people for their blood, not their beliefs. That reading was and remains controversial among historians like Yitzhak Baer.

What is significant about the thesis is what it reveals about his broader interpretive framework: Benzion tended to read Jewish history as a **"history of holocausts"** — a continuous sequence of persecutions whose cause is the structural antisemitism of the societies where Jews have lived, regardless of Jewish behavior itself. That vision had direct political consequences for the way Benjamin understands the world.

### The failed politician and the successful ideologue

Benzion could not achieve the political career he wanted. He was a follower of **Ze'ev Jabotinsky**, founder of **Revisionist Zionism**: a current that rejected compromise with the Arab world and held that Israel should exist on both sides of the Jordan. Benzion was Jabotinsky's personal secretary in New York from 1940. When Jabotinsky died that year, he took over as executive director of the New Zionist Organization of America until 1948.

When Israel was founded, he returned. But his positions were too extreme even for the Zionist establishment: he opposed the partition into two states and openly supported the forced deportation of all Arabs from Palestine. That left him on the margins of the dominant Israeli politics of the time.

What he could not do politically, he transmitted ideologically to his son. Benjamin has described his father as the central influence on his life. Benzion died in 2012 at age 102.

### Where "the prince of a royal family" comes from

The former head of the Shin Bet (Israeli domestic intelligence), **Yuval Diskin**, formulated Benjamin's psychology this way:

> "Inside Netanyahu there is a deep sense of being the prince of a 'royal family' from the Jerusalem elite, alongside insecurity and a deep fear of taking responsibility."

The phrase comes from real history. The Mileikowsky/Netanyahu family was neither aristocratic nor wealthy, but it was part of the political and intellectual elite of the Zionist movement from before Israel existed. The great-grandfather was a celebrated orator of the Zionist movement. The father was personal secretary to the ideologue who founded the current that eventually took power in Israel. There are three generations of continuity in the hard core of Revisionist Zionism. Benjamin grew up with that history as daily context: the family was not merely observing the birth of Israel — it had helped forge it ideologically. That generates a sense of belonging and mission that is not arbitrary arrogance, but something built on a real political genealogy.

## 4. Ottoman Palestine vs. Mandatory Palestine: what was there before?

If the text refers to "Ottoman Palestine," does that mean the territory was already Jewish before Israel? No. The confusion comes from the names.

**Ottoman Palestine** is the name historians use for the period when the territory that is now Israel, the Palestinian territories, and parts of Jordan and Lebanon were part of the **Ottoman Empire** (from the sixteenth century until 1918). The Ottoman Empire was Turkish and Muslim, with its capital in Constantinople. The territory's population was predominantly Arab, with a Jewish minority that had never entirely disappeared, and a Christian minority.

In 1918, the Ottoman Empire collapsed after being defeated in World War I. The British took control and the **League of Nations** gave them the **British Mandate over Palestine** in 1922: colonial administration with a vague commitment to prepare the territory for some form of self-government. That period is called **Mandatory Palestine** (1920-1948).

When Benjamin's mother, Tzila Segal, was born in Petah Tikva in 1912, she was born in **Ottoman** Palestine — not in Israel or in any Jewish political entity. Petah Tikva was one of the first Jewish agricultural colonies founded in the region from 1878 onward, but it was a private settlement within the Ottoman Empire, not a state institution. When Nathan arrived with his family in 1920, he arrived in **Mandatory** Palestine under British control, where approximately 700,000 Arabs and about 60,000 Jewish people lived. Israel as a state would not exist until 1948.

## 5. Benjamin: where he lived and grew up

**Benjamin Netanyahu** was born on October 21, 1949 in Tel Aviv. He was the second of three sons of Benzion and Tzila.

### Life between Israel and the United States

Benzion taught at Israeli and American universities alternately for years. Benjamin spent significant portions of his childhood and adolescence in the United States.

The most concrete period: when Benzion secured a position at **Cornell University** in Ithaca, New York, the family settled in the US. Benjamin attended secondary school at **Cheltenham High School**, in the suburbs of Philadelphia, in Montgomery County, Pennsylvania — a middle-class residential area north of the city — between approximately 1963 and 1967.

The years in the US were formative in ways that went beyond schooling. Benjamin learned English with near-native fluency, internalized the codes of American politics and media, and developed a capacity to speak to Western audiences that few Israeli leaders have matched. That would become his most powerful political tool.

He returned to Israel in 1967 for military service. After the army he went back to the US to study: at **MIT** in Cambridge, Massachusetts, where he completed a bachelor's degree in architecture and a master's in management at the Sloan School of Management. He then worked at **Boston Consulting Group** in Boston before returning definitively to Israel in 1978.

## 6. The mother and the maternal branch

**Tzila Segal** (1912-2000), Benjamin's mother, was born in **Petah Tikva**, in what was then Ottoman Palestine. She studied law at Gray's Inn in London and married Benzion in 1944 when he was in New York. It was she who managed family life during the long periods when Benzion was teaching abroad. Her maternal lineage — the Markus/Dworsky family from Lithuania, who stopped in Minneapolis before settling in Palestine — is documented in section 12.

## 7. Genetic identity: Sephardic by DNA, Ashkenazi by history

In 2016, Benjamin revealed that his brother Ido had conducted a family DNA test. The result: part of the family tree connects to **Sephardic Jews** — those expelled from Spain in 1492 — which is consistent with Benzion's academic specialization, having spent decades studying precisely that community.

Benjamin used it to make a political point to the Likud audience, the party that has historically been the political home of Sephardic and Mizrahi Jews (of Arab and North African origin), who for decades felt marginalized by the Ashkenazi elite of the Labor establishment. What the DNA does not resolve is the claim of "direct descent from the biblical Israelites," which Benjamin has made in international forums and which geneticists and historians do not consider verifiable.

## 8. Brother Yonatan: the martyr who structured everything else

On July 4, 1976, Israeli commandos rescued 102 hostages held in Uganda in **Operation Entebbe**. The only Israeli soldier killed was the commander of the operation: **Yonatan Netanyahu**, Benjamin's older brother, aged 30.

Yonatan died a national hero. Benjamin, who had served in the same elite unit (Sayeret Matkal), soon afterward founded the **Jonathan Institute for the Study of Terrorism** in his brother's memory. Yonatan's death is the emotional and narrative core around which Benjamin has built much of his public identity and his stance on terrorism.

## 9. The political career: who appointed him and what is Likud

### Who appointed him ambassador to the UN

In 1984, Prime Minister **Yitzhak Shamir** appointed Benjamin as Israel's ambassador to the UN, a position he held until 1988.

**Who was Shamir?** Yitzhak Shamir (1915-2012) is one of the hardest figures in Israeli history. Born in Poland, he arrived in Palestine in 1935 and joined first the Irgun — Menachem Begin's revisionist paramilitary group — and then the Lehi, known as the "Stern Gang," a more extreme group that explored alliances with fascist Italy during World War II as an anti-British tactic. He was arrested by the British twice; the second time deported to Eritrea, from which he escaped. After 1948 he worked for the Mossad for a decade. He entered the Knesset in 1973, served as Speaker, as Begin's foreign minister, and assumed the prime ministership in 1983 when Begin unexpectedly resigned. He was prime minister in two periods: 1983-1984 and 1986-1992, always resisting any territorial concession. He chose Benjamin for the UN because he saw in him the ability to communicate Israel's position to Western audiences with an effectiveness that few Israelis had.

### What is Likud

**Likud** (Hebrew for "consolidation" or "unity") is the right-wing political party dominant in Israel since 1977.

Its roots lie in **Ze'ev Jabotinsky** (1880-1940), the intellectual who founded **Revisionist Zionism** in the 1920s. Jabotinsky held that Jews had the historical and moral right to establish their state in all of historical Palestine — including what is now Jordan — and that this right could not be subordinated to negotiations with the Arabs. His vision was explicitly nationalist, without the socialist concessions of Ben Gurion's Labor Zionism.

Likud was formally founded in **1973** as a coalition of right-wing parties headed by **Menachem Begin**. Begin had commanded the Irgun, including the bombing of the King David Hotel in Jerusalem in 1946 (91 dead). He was the direct ideological heir of Jabotinsky.

The party won for the first time in **1977**, breaking 30 years of Labor dominance — a historic result Israelis call "the Upheaval." Its **1977 platform** declared that "only the Jewish people has the eternal and indisputable right to the Land of Israel" between the Mediterranean Sea and the Jordan River, recognizing no Palestinian right to sovereignty.

Likud leaders since then: Begin (1977-1983), Shamir (1983-1993), Benjamin (1993-1999, 2005-present). Benjamin was elected Likud leader in 1993 and became prime minister for the first time in 1996.

## 10. The three criminal cases: who opened them, what happened and where they stand

Benjamin is the **only sitting prime minister in Israel's history to face a criminal trial**.

### How it started: the police investigation (2016)

The investigations began in December **2016**, when the Israeli Police opened formal inquiries. The unit that investigated Case 4000 was the **National Fraud Investigation Unit** (Lahav 433).

A detail Benjamin himself repeatedly points out: both the police chief who investigated the cases and the attorney general who eventually indicted him were **appointed by him**. Attorney General **Avichai Mandelblit** had been Benjamin's cabinet secretary before being promoted to attorney general in 2016. Benjamin expected him to be an ally; he ended up being the man who indicted him.

On **November 21, 2019**, Mandelblit formally filed the indictment: the first time in Israel's history that a sitting prime minister faced criminal charges. The trial began in **May 2020**.

---

### Case 1000 — "The gifts affair"

**The actors**: Benjamin and his wife Sara; **Arnon Milchan**, an Israeli film producer and former Mossad agent who produced _Pretty Woman_ and _12 Years a Slave_; and **James Packer**, an Australian billionaire.

**What is alleged**: Over approximately 20 years, Milchan and Packer delivered gifts to Benjamin and Sara — cigars and champagne valued at over 700,000 shekels (around $200,000). In 2017, Packer told Australian police the Netanyahu family "demanded" the gifts. Milchan testified in court in 2023 that he provided them regularly. In exchange, Benjamin allegedly managed the renewal of Milchan's US visa and promoted a tax exemption for Israeli expatriates that directly benefited Milchan, who lived in Los Angeles.

**Charges**: fraud and breach of trust. Maximum penalty: three years.

Benjamin, Sara and Milchan deny everything.

---

### Case 2000 — "The media coverage deal"

**The actors**: Benjamin and **Arnon Mozes**, owner of major daily _Yedioth Ahronoth_.

**What is alleged**: Between 2008 and 2014, Benjamin and Mozes allegedly negotiated an exchange: favorable coverage from _Yedioth_ in exchange for legislation harming rival _Israel Hayom_, a free tabloid funded by Sheldon Adelson that was cutting into _Yedioth_'s revenue. The deal was never completed, but the prosecution argues the negotiation itself constitutes the offense.

**Charges**: fraud and breach of trust. (Mozes was charged with attempted bribery.)

Benjamin denies everything.

---

### Case 4000 — "The Bezeq-Walla affair" (the most serious)

**The actors**: Benjamin; **Shaul Elovitch**, controlling shareholder of **Bezeq**, Israel's largest telecom company; and the news website **Walla!**, owned by Elovitch.

**What is alleged**: Between 2012 and 2017, while Benjamin also served as Minister of Communications, he allegedly intervened in regulatory decisions worth billions of shekels to Bezeq — generating benefits for Elovitch of approximately **1.8 billion shekels** (around $500 million). In exchange, Walla! published positive coverage of Benjamin and his family and suppressed negative information about him.

The prosecution's key witness is **Shlomo Filber**, former director general of the Ministry of Communications appointed by Benjamin, who became a state witness. Filber testified he received direct instructions from Benjamin on how to handle regulatory matters in favor of Bezeq.

**Charges**: bribery, fraud and breach of trust. Bribery carries up to **10 years in prison**.

Benjamin denies everything absolutely. He calls Filber a fabricated witness.

---

### The trial's development: a chronicle that does not end

**May 2020**: Trial begins before three judges at the Jerusalem District Court.

**2020-2023**: Extremely slow progress. Over 300 prosecution witnesses. Dozens of defense motions to delay or dismiss. COVID-19 and the October 7, 2023 attacks interrupted sessions.

**2023**: Benjamin announces the **judicial reform**, which would have drastically reduced the Supreme Court's review power. Massive protests followed for months. The reform was partially blocked by the Supreme Court in January 2024.

**December 2024**: Benjamin **began testifying** — the first sitting prime minister in Israel to take the stand as a criminal defendant.

**November 2025**: Benjamin filed a formal **presidential pardon** request with President Isaac Herzog — unprecedented in Israeli history. Former US President **Donald Trump** publicly called for Israel to pardon Benjamin during his Knesset address in October 2025.

**April 2026**: Trial continues. The war with Iran (which began in February 2026) caused a brief interruption. General elections are scheduled for October 2026.

### The political knot: why didn't he resign?

Israeli law does not require an indicted prime minister to resign — only a convicted one with a final sentence. While the trial continues, Benjamin remains in office, generating one of Israel's sharpest political debates: can someone govern a country while being tried for corruption? The legal answer is yes.

## 11. The ICC: the international arrest warrant

In November 2024, the **International Criminal Court** issued an arrest warrant against Benjamin — the first ever issued against the sitting leader of a Western-backed democratic country.

Charges for crimes in Gaza between October 8, 2023 and May 20, 2024:

- **The war crime of starvation as a method of warfare**: deliberate deprivation of food, water, electricity, fuel and medical supplies to the civilian population of Gaza.
- **The crime against humanity of murder** in relation to civilian deaths from those deprivations.
- **The war crime of intentionally directing attacks against the civilian population**.

All 125 Rome Statute member states are legally obligated to arrest Benjamin if he enters their territory. Israel — like the US — is not an ICC member and rejected the charges as "absurd." Benjamin called them a "disgrace." His freedom of movement is now severely restricted.

## 12. The maternal lineage and the question of Jewish recognition

A question that naturally arises: would Benjamin be recognized as Jewish under Orthodox law?

The short answer is yes, with high probability. What matters is not the father's line but the unbroken maternal chain.

### The documented chain

Cross-referenced records from WikiTree, Find a Grave, Geni and other sources reconstruct four generations of Benjamin's maternal line without any documented break:

**Tzila Segal** (1912-2000) — his mother. Born in Petah Tikva, Ottoman Palestine. Her family was originally from Lithuania, emigrated to Minneapolis sometime in the 1870s, and returned to Palestine in 1911.

**Chana Malka Markus** (1870-1953) — his maternal grandmother. Born in Lazdijai, Lithuania. Died in Tel Aviv in 1953.

**Chaia Sorah Dworsky** (ca. 1843-1927) — his maternal great-grandmother. Born in Suwalki, then Poland. Jewish woman from a Lithuanian/Polish Ashkenazi community.

**Avraham Markus** — his maternal great-grandfather. A Lithuanian Jew who emigrated first to the United States and arrived in Palestine around age 50, settling in **Rishon Letzion** as one of its first farmers. Benjamin mentioned him publicly: he had a mule named Fanny who followed him everywhere, even to the synagogue. The other congregants complained. Avraham's solution was to donate a plot of land to the local congregation of Turkish Jews from Urfa so they could build their own synagogue.

The entire verifiable matrilineal chain is uninterruptedly Jewish. No public source indicates any break.

### An additional detail: family across two continents

Benjamin's maternal grandfather, Binyamin Segal, died in Minneapolis on June 22, 1923 — indicating part of the family remained in the United States while another branch returned to Palestine in 1911. Benjamin, who spent part of his adolescence in Philadelphia, had family roots on both sides of the Atlantic in the same generation.

### The irony that does exist, though not about him

Benjamin does not fall outside the Orthodox matrilineal criterion. But the system he has politically supported — the Orthodox rabbinate as the exclusive arbiter of Jewish identity in Israel — creates exactly that problem for hundreds of thousands of Israelis: former Soviet Union immigrants who arrived under the Law of Return (which requires a Jewish grandparent, not matrilineality), people with Jewish fathers but non-Jewish mothers, and people with non-Orthodox conversions.

The Law of Return and rabbinic law use different criteria to define who is Jewish. Benjamin navigates both without difficulty. Many other Israelis cannot. That asymmetry — which he institutionally defends — is the structural irony.

## 13. The family arc: three generations of an ideological project

What distinguishes this family is not just the duration in power, but a transgenerational ideological project.

Nathan was a Zionist when Zionism was a minority. Benzion was a revisionist when revisionism was a minority within Zionism. Yonatan was the soldier who embodied the ideal of sacrifice. Benjamin was the one who converted all that family history into sustained political power.

The irony historians note: the Zionism founded by Herzl was radically secular, almost anti-religious. The Zionism Benjamin represents today — allied with Smotrich and Ben Gvir, who see the territory as a divine mandate — would have been unrecognizable to the movement's founder. The Mileikowsky/Netanyahu family is, in a certain sense, the story of that journey.
