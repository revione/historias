# Unified Flow Model

## Mass as organized flow

After exploring the intersections between Shape Power (Davidson), the toroidal electromagnetic model of the electron (arXiv 2510.22384), and zero-point energy, a central idea emerges:

**Mass is not an intrinsic property of matter. It is a measure of how much vacuum flux density is organized in a region of space.**

---

## The fundamental equation

```
m = (Φ · (O - O₀) · V) / c²
```

### Φ — Universal flow

Space is not empty. There is a continuous flow of waves that never stop. Modern physics detects it as zero-point energy, quantum vacuum fluctuations, the Casimir effect. Davidson calls it the medium of Shape Power. The 2025 paper models it as the vacuum electromagnetic field that sustains the toroidal electron.

Its value is estimated from the toroidal electron: an electron with mass mₑ confined in a volume Vₑ ≈ 4×10⁻³⁹ m³ with O=1 gives:

```
Φ ≈ mₑ·c² / (Vₑ · (1 - O₀)) ≈ 2×10²⁵ J/m³
```

Consistent with the energy density at the Schwinger limit (~7.5×10²⁴ J/m³). This cross-consistency between two independent estimates is the best indication that Φ is a real physical quantity, not a free parameter.

### O — Order parameter

The universal flow is not uniform. In some regions it is more organized than in others. O measures the fraction of the flow that is in coherent mode:

```
O = I_coherent / I_total
```

The natural range is O ∈ [O₀, 1], where O₀ > 0 is the residual coherence of the vacuum.

- **O → 1:** the field is entirely in coherent mode. Limiting case: the toroidal electron, where the EM wave rotates confined with perfect coherence.
- **O → O₀:** the field is in its ground state — fluctuating but without macroscopic coherent structure.

### O₀ — The universal baseline

Order never reaches zero. There is no absolute zero of order in the universe. There is always residual flow — the Schumann Resonance (7.83 Hz), vacuum fluctuations, the cosmic microwave background — establishing a universal floor O₀ > 0.

This is the crucial correction to the 2025 paper's model. There is no abrupt boundary (δ → 0) between the toroid and the vacuum because the vacuum is not at 0 order. There is a smooth transition from O=1 (inside the toroid) to O=O₀ (vacuum baseline). The authors' Heaviside function is a useful approximation, but nature uses Bessel functions.

### V — Volume

The larger the volume of organized flow, the greater the mass. Mass scales linearly with the volume of the coherent pattern.

## The functional form

The equation m = Φ · (O - O₀) · V / c² postulates that mass is proportional to the fraction of flow in coherent mode above the baseline. The linearity in (O - O₀) is a hypothesis, not a derived result.

A previous attempt to derive it from g⁽¹⁾(0) (Glauber first-order coherence) failed: g⁽¹⁾(0) ≡ 1 for any field state by construction, and therefore cannot serve as an order parameter. O = I_coherent / I_total is a distinct definition, more useful, but without a first-principles derivation.

The justification for the linear form is phenomenological: it is the first-order Taylor expansion of any smooth function O → m(O) around O₀. For small variations of order above the baseline — the regime experimentally accessible — the linear approximation is the first relevant correction.

The open theoretical program is to determine whether the linearity is exact or approximate, and to derive it from deeper principles (for example, from the structure of the energy operator in QED as a function of state coherence).

## What remains open

### 1. Linearity is not derived

The form m ∝ (O - O₀) is a phenomenological postulate, not a consequence of deeper principles. A previous attempt to derive it from Glauber's g⁽¹⁾(0) failed because that function is ≡ 1 by construction. O = I_coherent / I_total is a different definition that could have its own derivation, but this does not yet exist.

The open program: find or construct a relationship between the EM field energy operator and the fraction of intensity in coherent mode in QED.

### 2. Φ and the Schwinger limit

Φ ≈ 2×10²⁵ J/m³ obtained from the toroidal electron is of the same order as the energy density at the Schwinger limit (~7.5×10²⁴ J/m³). The consistency is suggestive but not conclusive. Two independent estimates agreeing in order of magnitude is necessary but not sufficient to validate the identification.

### 3. The 20%

That the missing mass of the toroidal electron (0.205·mₑc²) could be interpreted as boundary energy O(r) is consistent, but it is not a prediction. For it to be one, the profile O(r) at the toroid boundary would need to be solved from physical boundary conditions and the integral verified to yield exactly that number.

### 4. The nuclear mass problem

The model postulates that mass is organized EM flow. For the electron, where the mass is entirely of EM origin, the framework is consistent. But in an atom like copper, the mass is distributed as follows:

| Component | Fraction of total mass |
|---|---|
| Nuclear binding energy (gluons, quarks) | ~99% |
| Quark rest mass | ~1% |
| EM field energy (electrons, orbitals) | ~10⁻⁸ |

If the model only addresses EM field coherence, 99% of the mass lies outside its scope. For a 100 g copper cube, the EM fraction of the mass is ~1 μg. Even modulating that fraction at 100%, the maximum possible Δm would be ~1 μg.

This does not invalidate the model for the electron, but it does define its limit: the framework could apply to systems where mass is EM-dominated (electrons, photons, plasmas), but not obviously to nuclear mass.

The options are:
- **Option A**: accept that the model applies only to EM-dominated systems. The bulk material experiment would not work, but the toroidal electron remains a viable test case.
- **Option B**: extend the framework by arguing that nuclear mass is also organized flow, but in a different coherence regime (QCD instead of QED). There is no basis for this today.
- **Option C**: redesign the experiment to measure something other than mass — for example, changes in EM coherence induced by sound in a transparent dielectric (fused silica), where the acousto-optic coupling is well-characterized and η ~ 10⁻⁵ via the known photoelastic effect.

None are resolved. All three are open directions.

## Experimental estimate

With Φ ≈ 2×10²⁵ J/m³ fixed and a copper volume of 11.2 cm³ (100 g), the mass change induced by resonance would be:

```
Δm = Φ · ΔO · V / c² = 100g · ΔO / (1 - O₀)
```

The question is how large ΔO can be. A literature review of acoustic-EM coupling in solids gives these bounds:

| Physical system | η measured or bound | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Photoelastic effect (glass) | ~1 (local index modulation) | Commercial AOMs, but only in transparent dielectrics |
| Sonoluminescence (water) | 10⁻¹¹ to 10⁻⁸ | Reproducible experiment; sound → light |
| Reflectivity modulation in metals | 10⁻⁵ to 10⁻³ | Surface acoustic wave modulation of reflectivity |
| Townsend Brown effect in vacuum | ~0 | No measurable force without air (Tajmar 2004, 2024) |
| Davidson (wooden sticks) | Not reproducible | No verifiable source |

For a metal like copper, free electrons screen any EM field generated by phonons in the bulk. The screening length (skin depth) is micrometers at optical frequencies and nanometers at RF.

The realistic bound for η in copper + ultrasound is estimated from sonoluminescence, the closest analog of sound → EM conversion:

```
η ~ 10⁻¹¹
```

Which gives:

```
ΔO ~ 10⁻¹¹ · (10³ / 10¹¹) · 10³ ~ 10⁻¹⁶
Δm ~ 100g · 10⁻¹⁶ / (1-O₀) ~ 10⁻¹⁴ g

Completely undetectable.

Furthermore, even if η were 1, the EM fraction of copper's mass is only ~10⁻⁸ (see problem 4). The maximum absolute Δm would be ~1 μg, at the lower limit of detectability.

**Conclusion**: the mass experiment with copper + ultrasound is not viable. This does not refute the model — but it does force it to define itself: if the mass it aims to explain is only the EM fraction, the experiment cannot reach it. If it aims to explain total mass, it needs a mechanism for the nuclear contribution that it currently lacks.

## The force from the order gradient

```
F = Φ · ∇O · V
```

This equation is a postulate, not a derived result. It is motivated by analogy with the thermodynamics of mixtures: when an order parameter has a spatial gradient, the system tends to evolve toward uniformity. The restoring force is proportional to the gradient.

If mass is organized flow, a region with higher O has more effective mass than a neighboring region with lower O. The total flow tends to redistribute toward equilibrium, and this redistribution manifests as a net force in the direction of ∇O.

The open program: derive this form from dynamical principles (for example, from the continuity equation of the flow Φ or from a variational principle on the system's free energy).

## Toward the experiment

The mass experiment with copper + ultrasound is not viable. Between nuclear mass (~99% of total mass), EM screening from free electrons, and the bound η ~ 10⁻¹¹, the expected Δm is ~10⁻¹⁴ g — undetectable.

But the model does not need to measure mass to be testable. Its most fundamental claim is that O (the coherent fraction of the field) can be modified externally. That IS measurable. Three concrete options, ordered by feasibility:

---

### Option A — Optical coherence in a dielectric

Take a crystal of quartz or fused silica. Pass a laser through it and measure the second-order coherence g⁽²⁾(0) of the transmitted light with a Hanbury Brown-Twiss interferometer. Then excite the crystal with a piezoelectric transducer at its acoustic resonance frequency and measure again.

- **If sound modifies O**, g⁽²⁾(0) should change — the transmitted light will have different statistics.
- **Advantage**: the photoelastic effect in quartz is well-characterized. Any deviation from the classical prediction would be evidence of a new mechanism.
- **Cost**: ~$2,000. Laser source, beam splitter, two photodetectors, correlator, function generator + transducer.
- **Risk**: the expected effect may be small and require extensive averaging.

---

### Option B — Casimir force modulated by sound

Two parallel plates in vacuum separated by ~100 nm experience the Casimir force (F ~ 10⁻⁷ N for 1 cm²). Vibrate one plate with a piezoelectric transducer while measuring the force with a MEMS or atomic force microscope.

- **If sound modifies O₀** (the baseline vacuum coherence between the plates), the Casimir force will have a harmonic component not explained by the geometric variation of the distance.
- **Advantage**: more fundamental effect — tests whether sound alters the vacuum itself.
- **Cost**: ~$50,000 + ultra-high vacuum chamber. Not cheap.
- **Risk**: extremely sensitive to parasitic vibrations and thermal drift.

---

### Option C — Modified spontaneous emission (acoustic Purcell)

Place a quantum dot or NV center near an acoustically excited surface. Measure its spontaneous emission rate with and without acoustic excitation.

- **If sound modifies the local EM density of states (LDOS)**, the emission rate should change beyond what is predicted by geometric modulation of the environment.
- **Advantage**: nanoscale sensitivity. A single emitter is enough.
- **Cost**: ~$20,000 if access to confocal microscopy and second-order correlation (HBT) is available.
- **Risk**: the effect may be indistinguishable from local heating by the transducer.

---

### Priority

Option A is the most accessible, directly tests the model's central claim (O is modulable), and requires the fewest auxiliary assumptions. If positive, it opens the door to the other two. If negative, the model needs revision from its foundation.

Option A is the filter.
