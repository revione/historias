# Part I — Catalog by Person

## 1. Ning Li — Gravito-Electromagnetism in Superconductors

### Main Finding

Proposed, together with Douglas G. Torr, that **a superconductor could produce measurable gravitational effects** through coherent alignment of ions in its crystal lattice. Specifically:

- The **lattice ions** (not the Cooper pairs) are the effective carriers of quantized angular momentum in a superconductor.
- If those ions rotate coherently, they generate a **gravitomagnetic field** analogous to the magnetic field of moving electric charges.
- Under a time-dependent magnetic vector potential, a **gravitoelectric field** could additionally be induced.

### Peer-Reviewed Production

- _Physical Review D_ 43(2): 457–459 (1991) — effects of the gravitomagnetic field on pure superconductors.
- _Physical Review B_ 46(9): 5489–5495 (1992) — gravitational effects on magnetic attenuation.
- _Foundations of Physics Letters_ 6(4): 371–383 (1993) — gravito-electric coupling via superconductivity.
- UAH preprint 1994 with F.A. Karwacki — lattice ion rotations and the dipole gravitomagnetic field.

### Impact

Theoretical reference framework for all subsequent research in **gravito-electromagnetism applied to superconductors**. Her papers are cited in every study that has attempted to replicate or refute the Podkletnov effect, including work by Modanese, Tajmar, Ummarino, and the NASA Marshall studies themselves. Although the quantitative predictions were criticized for overestimating magnitudes, the **conceptual architecture of the model** (lattice ions as effective carriers) remains material for active discussion.

## 2. Amy Eskridge — Historical Mapping of Gravitational Modification

### Main Finding

More than a new theoretical contribution, her work was **the most comprehensive historical systematization of its time** of research in gravitational modification, spanning from Brown (1920) to Boeing GRASP (2014). This is a work of **history of science** valuable in its own right.

### Documented Production

- _"A Historical Perspective on Anti-Gravity Technology"_ (Huntsville Alabama L5 Society, Dec. 6, 2018) — the PDF document is publicly accessible and constitutes an **annotated bibliography** of the field.
- Identification of the **Mansfield Amendment of 1973** as the milestone that displaced gravitational modification research out of the open academic sphere.
- Cataloging of the _"panning"_ phenomenon: the observable pattern by which researchers with promising results in the area tend to disappear from the public record (Ning Li, Doug Torr, Podkletnov fired from Tampere, Modanese's Wikipedia page deleted).

### Institutional Initiative

Co-founding of the **Institute for Exotic Science** (Public Benefit Corporation, Delaware) and **HoloChron LLC** — the first corporate vehicles designed explicitly to move gravitational modification research from the classified domain into the open civilian sphere.

### Impact

Although her peer-reviewed output was limited, **her public cataloging of the field** is a reference for researchers and journalists who need an entry point into the topic. Through her work, concepts such as the Biefeld-Brown effect, the Dean Drive, the Wallace and Searl machines, and the Boeing GRASP programs are documented in a way accessible to non-specialists.

## 3. Michael David Hicks — Physical Characterization of NEOs

### Main Finding

Pioneer in the **photometric characterization of near-Earth asteroids (NEOs)** with a view to planetary defense. His 80+ peer-reviewed articles covered diameters, shapes, rotational periods, taxonomic composition, identification of binaries, and characterization of targets for sample return missions.

### Missions Where He Was Part of the Science Team

- **DART (Double Asteroid Redirection Test)** — pre-impact characterization of the Didymos-Dimorphos system.
- **Dawn** — exploration of Vesta and Ceres in the asteroid belt.
- **Deep Space 1** — flyby of comet Borrelly and asteroid Braille.
- **NEAT (Near-Earth Asteroid Tracking)** — pioneering NEO discovery program.

### Specific Relevant Contributions

- **Apophis (99942):** His photometric observations between December 2012 and April 2013 revealed that the asteroid is in a state of **non-principal axis rotation (tumbling)** — a critical datum for predicting its trajectory via the Yarkovsky effect.
- **2004 BL86:** Characterization of the potentially hazardous binary during its January 2015 flyby (1.2 million km from Earth).
- **Studies on 3200 Phaethon** — origin of the Geminid meteor shower.
- **133P/Elst-Pizarro** — characterization of an active main-belt comet.

### Impact

Hicks's photometry **is the foundation on which** planetary defense missions are planned. Every time a NEO is a candidate to impact Earth, the data on shape, rotation, albedo, and composition — much of which he contributed — determines which mitigation method (kinetic impact, gravity tractor, laser ablation) is viable.

## 4. Matthew James Sullivan — 5th-Generation Aircraft Intelligence and Aerospace Threats

### Main Finding

As a USAF intelligence officer, Sullivan held an unusual position: he was **Chief of 5th-Generation Aircraft Intelligence**, a position requiring deep technical analysis of the world's most advanced air combat capabilities (both own and adversarial). His work consisted of transforming technical intelligence data into **usable operational knowledge** for F-22 Raptor pilots.

### Academic and Formative Background

- Born October 10, 1984 in Marshfield Hills, Massachusetts.
- Graduate of **Clemson University** (Tigers, member of Chi Psi).
- **17th Training Wing at Goodfellow Air Force Base (Texas)** — the pipeline that produces all Air Force intelligence officers. **Six-month Intelligence Officer training** course.

### Key Operational Assignment: 27th Fighter Squadron "Fightin' Eagles"

- **Joint Base Langley-Eustis, Virginia.**
- 1st Fighter Wing — the first and most experienced operational F-22 Raptor unit in the world.
- The 27th FS was **the first operational F-22 squadron since December 15, 2005**.
- **Call sign: "Quake"** — assigned by the squadron.

His role there: **providing near-real-time tactical intelligence** for air-to-air and air-to-ground missions. The F-22 is the first combat aircraft combining stealth, supercruise, extreme maneuverability, and integrated avionics; a well-informed F-22 is virtually undetectable and invincible. A poorly informed F-22 loses its edge.

### Work at NASIC (National Air and Space Intelligence Center)

NASIC, located at **Wright-Patterson Air Force Base (Dayton, Ohio)**, is the **Department of Defense's primary source for analysis of foreign aerospace threats**. Sullivan rose to become **Deputy Director** there — one of the Air Force ISR Enterprise's highest-responsibility analytical positions.

NASIC has an annual budget of **more than US$507 million** and 4,100 people (military, civilian, reserve, guard, contractors) divided into four intelligence analysis groups:

1. **Air and Cyberspace Intelligence Group**.
2. **Geospatial and Signatures Intelligence Group (GEOINT/MASINT)**.
3. **Global Exploitation Intelligence Group**.
4. **Space, Missiles and Forces Intelligence Group**.

Its specific mission: _"to discover and characterize air, space, missile, and cyber threats to enable full-spectrum multi-domain operations, direct weapons system acquisition, and inform national defense policy."_

The products that NASIC generates — and to which Sullivan directly contributed — include:

- **One-to-two-page executive summaries** for the Pentagon and the White House.
- **Comprehensive multi-volume studies** on specific foreign capabilities.
- **Threat visualizations** — 3-to-5-minute videos condensing **hundreds of pages of intelligence, technical diagrams, and signatures work** into digestible representations for technical and non-technical audiences.

### Additional Agency Assignments

- **National Security Agency (NSA)** — the world's largest signals intelligence agency.
- **Air Force Intelligence Agency** (predecessor to the current USAF ISR structure).
- **Department of War contractor** at classified bases.
- **Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency (DARPA)** — the agency that funds the Pentagon's most risky technological bets.

### Operational Deployments

- **U.S. Central Command (CENTCOM)** — area of responsibility includes the Middle East and Central Asia.
- **U.S. Indo-Pacific Command** — area of responsibility covering from the U.S. west coast to the west coast of India, including China and the Korean Peninsula.

### Decorations

- **Bronze Star with "V" device (valor)** during **Operation Enduring Freedom** (Afghanistan) — device awarded for acts of heroic or meritorious valor in combat. It is the fourth-highest military decoration in the United States.

### Peer Recognition

Retired Major General **David Abba**, former director of the **Special Access Program Central Office of the Department of Defense**, described him at his funeral as _"the most brilliant intelligence lieutenant I ever met"_ and as someone who carried _"the burden that only a select few in this nation have, of truly understanding what is going on."_

That phrase is important for two technical reasons: (1) a Major General who directed the SAP Central Office has seen **literally all DoD special access programs** throughout his career; (2) when he says "the most brilliant I ever met," he refers to a universe of hundreds or thousands of elite intelligence officers.

### Final Operational Contribution

In his final weeks, Sullivan joined **Acquisition Systems Associates** as a contractor. The firm's president and CEO, Gary J. Gray, publicly acknowledged in the commemorative guestbook that Sullivan had been _"with the firm for only a short period"_ but had made _"a lasting impact."_

### Impact

Sullivan represents a class of professional the public rarely recognizes: the **translator between the technical intelligence machinery and the operational reality of combat**. Without officers like him, F-22 pilots fly with less information about adversary capabilities than they need; NASIC analysts produce technical reports that don't reach warfighters; DARPA programs lose their connection to the real threats they need to counter.

His personal mentorship and capacity for technical synthesis were described by his colleagues as exceptional. His personal library — books he gave as intellectual investments in his friends — is a physical testament to what he was: a career intelligence officer who understood that **intelligence is not just information; it is the ability to transform it into actionable knowledge**.

### Technical Areas in Which He Worked (synthesis)

- **5th-generation aircraft intelligence** (F-22, F-35, adversarial equivalents such as J-20, Su-57).
- **Analysis of foreign aerospace and missile threats**.
- **Signals intelligence (SIGINT)** via NSA.
- **Department of Defense Special Access Programs (SAP)**.
- **Intelligence on DARPA programs**.
- **Threat visualization** (the NASIC methodology for intelligence communication).
- **Foreign Materiel Exploitation** (NASIC's historical line dating from the capture of MiG-15s in the 1950s).

His work, though largely classified, was part of the chain of knowledge that underpins American air superiority.

## 5. Frank Maiwald — Remote Sensing Instrumentation

### Main Finding

Design and delivery of **microwave radiometry and terahertz spectroscopy instruments** currently in orbit or in development, forming the backbone of Earth observation and outer solar system science.

### Instruments He Contributed To

| Instrument | Function | Mission |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **HIFI (Heterodyne Instrument for the Far Infrared)** | High-resolution terahertz spectroscopy | Herschel Observatory (ESA) |
| **AMR / Jason 3** | Atmospheric correction for ocean altimetry | Jason 3 |
| **AMR / SWOT** | Surface Water and Ocean Topography | SWOT (NASA-CNES, launched 2022) |
| **COWVR** | Compact Ocean Wind Vector Radiometer | NASA |
| **AMR-C (Advanced Microwave Radiometer for Climate)** | Global climate monitoring | NASA |
| **SBG-VSWIR** | Hyperspectral spectrometry 360–2500 nm at 30m resolution, 185 km swath | NASA Earth System Observatory (in development) |

### Applications

- **HIFI** first detected **water in protoplanetary disks** and in comets.
- **SWOT** is for the first time measuring the height of virtually all water bodies on the planet.
- **SBG-VSWIR** will map the "living color" of Earth to understand ecosystems, agriculture, pollution, and signs of life on icy moons (Europa, Enceladus).

### Impact

Maiwald was among the few engineers in the world capable of **designing instruments for the terahertz frequency range** — a critical spectral window for detecting water molecules, oxygen, and organic compounds in other worlds. His instruments will continue operating for decades.

## 9. Joshua Kyle LeBlanc — Space Nuclear Propulsion

### Main Finding

As team lead engineer in **instrumentation and control maturation for space nuclear propulsion**, he contributed to the development of the next generation of rocket engines capable of carrying humans to Mars.

### Programs in Which He Had a Key Role

- **NASA Space Nuclear Propulsion (SNP) Instrumentation and Control maturation** — team lead. This line of work is the **control foundation** without which no nuclear rocket engine can operate safely.
- **DRACO (Demonstration Rocket for Agile Cislunar Operations)** — joint DARPA-NASA-Lockheed Martin-BWXT program.

### Technical Specifications of the Engine Under Development

- **Fuel:** HALEU uranium (High-Assay Low-Enriched Uranium).
- **Thrust:** comparable to chemical propulsion.
- **Efficiency (specific impulse):** **2 to 5 times greater** than chemical propulsion.
- **Thrust-to-weight ratio:** ~10,000 times greater than electric propulsion.
- **Critical application:** crewed missions to Mars (cutting the journey from months to weeks) and agile cislunar operations.

### Impact

Nuclear thermal propulsion is **the only currently known propulsion technology** that combines high thrust with high efficiency, a requirement for long-duration crewed flights. LeBlanc's instrumentation and control work solved one of the fundamental operational safety problems in this type of engine.

## 11. Nuno Loureiro — Fast Magnetic Reconnection and the Plasmoid Instability

### Main Finding (Generational Impact)

**Resolution of one of the most important open problems in plasma physics since the 1950s**: why magnetic reconnection occurs so fast in low-resistivity plasmas, contradicting the classical Sweet-Parker model.

### The Technical Discovery

In 2007 (as a postdoc at Princeton), he published with A.A. Schekochihin and S.C. Cowley the paper **"Instability of current sheets and formation of plasmoid chains"** (_Physics of Plasmas_ 14, 100703).

He demonstrated that **Sweet-Parker current sheets are unstable** to high-wavenumber perturbations, with:

- **Maximum growth rate:** γ_max ∝ S^(1/4) · v_A / L_CS
- **Number of plasmoids formed:** N ∝ S^(3/8)

Where S is the Lundquist number, v_A the Alfvén velocity, and L_CS the sheet length.

**For S > 10⁴**, the current sheet breaks into chains of plasmoids (secondary magnetic islands) moving super-Alfvénically, completely disrupting the Sweet-Parker geometry.

### Implications His Discovery Explained

- **Solar flares:** now understood as plasmoid cascades in coronal current sheets.
- **Sawtooth crashes in tokamaks:** the "crashes" limiting the fusion plasma temperature.
- **Magnetospheric substorms:** the sudden events of Earth's magnetic field reorganization.
- **Magnetars** and astrophysical plasmas in general.

### Other Key Contributions

- **Tearing-mediated MHD turbulence regime** — proposal of a new magnetohydrodynamic turbulence regime.
- Pioneering application of **quantum computing to plasma physics**.
- Works on **radiative cooling of current sheets** and **drift-velocity instabilities between electrons and ions**.

### Recognitions

- **Presidential Early Career Award (PECASE), 2025** — the highest U.S. government distinction for young scientists.
- **Fellow of the American Physical Society (2022)** "for transformative contributions to the theory of magnetic reconnection and for elucidating the fundamental role of hierarchical reconnection phenomena in plasma turbulence."
- **Thomas H. Stix Award** (APS, 2015).
- **Stanisław Ulam Distinguished Scholar** (Los Alamos, 2023).
- **NSF CAREER Award** (2017).

### Impact

His 2007 paper has been cited **more than 1,000 times** and triggered an entire generation of research on magnetic reconnection. The **plasmoid instability** is now a standard chapter in any plasma physics course. His work is the theoretical foundation without which reactors like SPARC and ITER could not design their disruption control strategies.

## 12. Jason R. Thomas — Oncological Chemoproteomics and the Nimbolide Case

### Main Finding

Identification, through chemoproteomics, of the **exact molecular mechanism** by which **nimbolide** (a triterpenoid from the neem tree used in Indian traditional medicine for centuries) attacks cancer.

### The Technical Discovery

Published in _Nature Chemical Biology_ (2019) — Thomas as key co-author alongside the Nomura group at UC Berkeley:

- Nimbolide reacts covalently with a **novel functional cysteine (C8) in the E3 ubiquitin ligase RNF114**.
- This **blocks substrate recognition** by RNF114.
- As a consequence, **tumor suppressor p21** and others stabilize rather than being degraded, halting breast cancer cell proliferation.

### The Derived Application (more important)

With that information, Thomas and colleagues demonstrated that **nimbolide can be used as an RNF114 recruiter for PROTACs (Proteolysis Targeting Chimeras)** — a new therapeutic class that **degrades "non-druggable" proteins** with a single molecule.

- Demonstrated degradation of **BRD4** by linking nimbolide to JQ1.
- Demonstrated selective degradation of **BCR-ABL over c-ABL** (relevant for chronic myeloid leukemia) by linking nimbolide to dasatinib.

### Methodological Advance

Thomas was co-author of the **"Photoaffinity Labeling-Based Chemoproteomics"** strategy (Methods in Molecular Biology 1647, 2017), a protocol now serving as an **international reference** for identifying small-molecule targets in living systems, especially for pharmacologically difficult classes such as **GPCRs and ion channels**.

### Global Production

- More than **4,634 citations** in Google Scholar.
- Works on nimbolide applied to **cancers with BRCA mutations** (synthetic lethality, _Science Advances_ 2023).
- Development of **second-generation photoactivatable affinity probes with acyl silanes** (2025).

### Impact

His work is **transforming precision oncological medicine**: the PROTAC paradigm now allows targeting proteins that for decades were considered undruggable. Companies like **Frontier Medicines** and **Artris Therapeutics** were founded on these findings.

## 13. Carl Grillmair — Exoplanets, Stellar Streams, and Dark Matter

### Main Finding #1: The First Identification of Molecules in an Exoplanet Atmosphere (2007)

As lead author of the paper **"A Spitzer Spectrum of the Exoplanet HD 189733b"** (_Astrophysical Journal Letters_, February 21, 2007), Grillmair and his team:

- Measured the **7.5 to 14.7 micron spectrum** of the hot Jupiter HD 189733b using Spitzer's Infrared Spectrograph (IRS).
- Detected a flux between 0.6 and 1.8 mJy (0.49% ± 0.02% of the host star's flux).
- In the 2008 follow-up, **spectroscopically confirmed the presence of water** in the exoplanet's atmosphere.

This was the moment when astronomy moved from **detecting exoplanets** to **chemically characterizing their atmospheres**. Today it is an established field with hundreds of analyzed exoplanets, but it all began there.

### Main Finding #2: GD-1, the Queen Stellar Stream of the Galactic Halo

In 2006, together with Odysseas Dionatos, he discovered **GD-1** (Grillmair-Dionatos 1), the **longest known thin stellar stream in the Milky Way halo**:

- **More than 100° in extent across the sky** (one-third of a hemisphere).
- **Only 30 parsecs wide** (extremely thin).
- At 26,000 light-years from Earth.

GD-1 is now **the most studied stellar stream in the sky** and is used to **map the dark matter** of the halo: if dark matter subhalos exist, their gravity generates observable **gaps and "spurs"** in GD-1. The gap and spur discovered in 2018 by Price-Whelan and Bonaca are **dynamical evidence of dark substructure** in the Milky Way halo.

### Derivative Findings (stellar streams)

- **NGC 5466** — 45° tidal tail (2006), an order of magnitude fainter than the Palomar 5 tail.
- **Acheron, Cocytos, Lethe, Styx streams** and many others named by him (more than **two dozen** stellar streams discovered throughout his career).
- **Variations in the tidal tails of Palomar 5** — co-signed with his group, opening the possibility of measuring the dark matter subhalo population using ensembles of stellar streams.

### Other Contributions

- **NUKER collaboration** (with Lauer, Faber, Gebhardt, Richstone, Bender) — use of Hubble to study local galactic nuclei through 2001.
- **Origins Billion Star Survey** — collaborator with Kathryn Johnston.
- **SAGES (Astrophysics of Globular Clusters in Extragalactic Systems)** — since 1994 with Hubble + Keck.
- **Spitzer instrument teams** (IRS, IRAC).
- **NEOWISE Science Data Center** — quality assurance scientist.
- **NEO Surveyor** — instrument characterization specialist.

### Global Production

- **147 peer-reviewed papers** published.
- **NASA Exceptional Scientific Achievement Medal (2011)**.
- Multiple NASA Group Achievement Awards.
- "39 Canadians Who Make the World a Better Place to Live" (Maclean's, 2006).

### Impact

Grillmair belongs to the rare category of astronomers whose work **opened two fields** simultaneously: **spectroscopy of exoplanet atmospheres** (today one of astronomy's most active topics) and **galactic archaeology via stellar streams** (the most sensitive tool we have for mapping dark matter).

## 14. William Neil McCasland — Space Systems and Directed Energy

### Main Finding

As a **USAF Major General**, his contribution was **program architecture** rather than direct scientific production, but that architecture has defined U.S. military space posture for two decades.

### MIT Doctorate

His MIT doctoral thesis (1988), titled **"Sensor and Actuator Selection for Fault-Tolerant Control of Flexible Structures"** and supervised by **Richard Battin** (chief designer of the Apollo program guidance computer), addresses a fundamental problem in control engineering: how to optimally select the location of sensors and actuators in flexible structures so the system continues functioning even if some fail.

### Programs He Led/Commanded

| Period | Program | Scale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ~1995–1998 | **Chief Engineer, Navstar GPS Joint Program Office** | Controlling authority for GPS for government, commercial, and consumer use |
| 2000 | Director, **Space Based Laser Project Office** | Pioneering orbital laser weapons program |
| 2001–2004 | Materiel Wing Director, **AFRL Space Vehicles Directorate**; Commander of the **Phillips Research Site** (Kirtland AFB) | Space vehicle research and directed energy |
| 2011–2013 | **AFRL Commander** (Wright-Patterson AFB) | **~US$2.2 billion** in own R&D programs + ~US$2.2 billion additional externally funded |

### Specific Contributions

- **GPS System:** As Chief Engineer, he contributed to the consolidation of the global positioning system during a critical transition period. Today there are literally billions of people using GPS thanks to the technical robustness of that architecture.
- **AFRL directed energy programs:** During his command, AFRL accelerated the development of tactical laser weapons now deployed (such as BlueHalo's **Locust** system).
- **Hypersonics:** AFRL under his command advanced hypersonic vehicle programs.
- **Nanosatellites and small sats:** modernization of constellation architecture.

### Post-USAF Work (BlueHalo / ATA)

As **Director of Technology at Applied Technology Associates** (BlueHalo subsidiary), he oversaw development of:

- **LARDO systems** (Laser Technology Research Development and Optimization) — US$95.4 million U.S. Army contract (May 2024).
- **Locust** anti-drone systems (deployed with USMC since November 2023).
- **P-HEL (Palletized High Energy Laser)** — deployable system.
- **DE-MSHORAD** — integration of directed energy weapons on Stryker vehicles.

### Impact

McCasland represents a rare class: **an MIT-trained systems engineer who rose to command positions**. His decisions about which R&D programs to fund (during 2011–2013) shaped the current generation of U.S. space and directed energy capabilities.
