# Introduction

This document compiles the **14 professional profiles** mentioned in media coverage of the cluster of deceased or missing US scientists and specialists between 2021 and 2026. The goal is to focus exclusively on **who they were, where they trained, what they researched, and which projects they contributed to** before the events.

> 📌 Cases listed in chronological order (oldest to most recent).

---

## 1. Ning Li (1943 — 2021)

**Mathematical Physicist · University of Alabama in Huntsville (UAH) · AC Gravity LLC**

- **Background:** Chinese-American mathematical physicist based in Huntsville, Alabama.
- **Career:** Researcher at the University of Alabama in Huntsville since the early nineties. In the 1990s she co-authored with Douglas G. Torr several theoretical papers predicting that **superconductors could modify gravitational fields** — a pioneering research line in applied gravito-electromagnetism.
- **Own company:** In 1999 she left UAH to found **AC Gravity LLC**, a private company that continued her research with Department of Defense funding.
- **Recognition:** Considered an international reference figure in theoretical studies on gravitational modification with superconductors.
- **End of career:** In 2014 she was struck by a vehicle on the university campus, suffering severe brain damage that led to Alzheimer's. She passed away in 2021 at age 78.

## 2. Amy Eskridge (1987 — 2022)

**Chemist and Entrepreneur · Institute for Exotic Science · HoloChron LLC**

- **Background:** Chemist with experience in chemistry, entrepreneurship, and ties to aerospace research.
- **Career:** Co-founder, president, and CEO of the **Institute for Exotic Science**, a Public Benefit Corporation headquartered in Huntsville, Alabama, dedicated to research in **gravitational modification, quantum computing, metamaterial science, and advanced communications**.
- **Parallel company:** Co-founder and CEO of **HoloChron LLC**, a family company with her father Richard Eskridge (retired NASA engineer), focused on gravitational modification R&D. Active between July 2019 and December 2022.
- **Research focus:** Investigated anti-gravity propulsion and electrostatic propulsion systems. Sought to bring gravity research out of classified programs into the open civilian domain.
- **Notable conference:** December 2018, "A Historical Perspective on Anti-Gravity Technology" for the Huntsville Alabama L5 Society (HAL5), surveying from the Biefeld-Brown effect to the EM Drive.
- **Died in June 2022** in Huntsville. The death was officially ruled a suicide.

## 3. Michael David Hicks (1964 — 2023)

**Planetary Physicist · NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL)**

- **Background:** Physicist specializing in the physical properties of comets and asteroids.
- **JPL Career (1998–2022):** Began as a NASA postdoctoral researcher and later as a research scientist. Resident of Sunland, California.
- **Scientific missions:**
  - Science team of the **DART Project** (Double Asteroid Redirection Test): first mission to demonstrate asteroid deflection via kinetic impact.
  - **Near Earth Asteroid Tracking (NEAT) Project**.
  - **Dawn Mission** (study of the asteroid belt bodies Vesta and Ceres).
  - **Deep Space 1**.
- **Recognition:** Member of the Division for Planetary Sciences of the American Astronomical Society. Published numerous scientific papers.
- **Personal life:** Enthusiast of woodblock printing, oil painting, ukulele, the works of Hunter S. Thompson, and the Muppets.
- **Died July 30, 2023**, at age 59. Cause of death (per the Los Angeles County coroner, cited by reporters) was **arteriosclerotic cardiovascular disease**.

## 4. Matthew James Sullivan (1985 — 2024)

**Intelligence Officer · United States Air Force**

- **Call sign:** "Quake".
- **Military career:** Air Force intelligence officer, decorated with the **Bronze Star with "V" device** (valor) during Operation Enduring Freedom.
- **Units:** 17th Training Wing and 1st Fighter Wing's 27th Fighter Squadron (operating F-22s).
- **Intelligence assignments:**
  - **National Air and Space Intelligence Center (NASIC)**.
  - **Air Force Intelligence Agency**.
  - **National Security Agency (NSA)**.
  - Deployments to U.S. Central Command and U.S. Indo-Pacific Command.
- **Superior's assessment:** Retired Major General David Abba, former director of the Special Access Program Central Office of the Department of Defense, described him at his funeral as "the most brilliant intelligence lieutenant I ever knew."
- **Died May 12, 2024**, at age 39, at his home in Falls Church, Virginia. The Office of the Chief Medical Examiner Northern District of Virginia ruled an **accidental overdose** from a combination of alcohol, alprazolam, cyclobenzaprine, and imipramine.

## 5. Frank Werner Maiwald (1963 — 2024)

**Principal Engineer · NASA Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL)**

- **Career:** 25 years at JPL as **Principal Investigator**, managing major space instrumentation and Earth observation projects.
- **Specialty:** **Microwave radiometry** and Earth science missions. Highly specialized engineer in the design of critical instruments for space missions.
- **Recent project:** Working on the **Surface Biology and Geology (SBG)** mission, designing a **VSWIR (Visible to Shortwave Infrared) instrument** to map the "living color" of Earth.
- **Recognition:** Multiple NASA awards for his work. In 2023 led a scientific breakthrough in detecting signs of life on other worlds.
- **Died July 4, 2024** in Los Angeles, at age 61. His obituary simply states he "passed away," with no public cause of death.

## 6. Anthony "Tony" Chavez (1947 — missing since 2025)

**Retired Construction Foreman · Los Alamos National Laboratory (LANL)**

- **Career:** Long career at LANL as **construction foreman** within the national laboratory. Retired in 2017.
- **Reported specialty:** According to the New York Post, part of his career was spent at the **Dual-Axis Radiographic Hydrodynamic Test facility (DARHT)**, a key facility for nuclear weapons research.
- **Missing since May 4, 2025**, at age 78, left his home in Los Alamos, New Mexico on foot, leaving his wallet, keys, and personal belongings behind.

## 7. Monica Jacinto Reza (1965 — missing since 2025)

**Aerospace Engineer · NASA JPL · Aerojet Rocketdyne**

- **Career:** Aerospace engineer and materials processing engineer with a career spanning Aerojet Rocketdyne and NASA JPL.
- **Position at JPL:** **Director of the Materials Processing Group** at the time of her disappearance.
- **Key patent:** Co-inventor of **Mondaloy**, a patented nickel superalloy developed at Rocketdyne in the mid-nineties for rocket engines. This research has been applied in reusable rocket programs such as **New Glenn** (Blue Origin) and **Starship** (SpaceX).
- **Professional connections:** Maintained collaboration with Wright-Patterson Air Force Base.
- **Missing since June 22, 2025**, at age 60, while hiking near Mount Waterman in the Angeles National Forest. Was with a companion about 9 meters away; smiled and waved, and when the companion turned back she was gone.

## 8. Melissa Casias (1972 — missing since 2025)

**Administrative Assistant · Los Alamos National Laboratory (LANL)**

- **Career:** Long-term administrative employee at LANL with security clearance. Resident of Taos County, New Mexico (Ranchos de Taos).
- **Role:** Administrative assistant. Her niece Jazmin McMillen has clarified that she **did not hold a high-level clearance** or access to classified technical information.
- **Family life:** Was preparing to care for her mother during an upcoming surgery.
- **Missing since June 26, 2025**, at age 53. Last seen walking alone along a road in Taos County.

## 9. Joshua Kyle LeBlanc (1996 — 2025)

**Aerospace Electrical Engineer · NASA Marshall Space Flight Center**

- **Origin:** Born February 8, 1996 in New Iberia, Louisiana.
- **Education:**
  - Catholic High School (graduated 2014).
  - **Electrical Engineering** at the University of Louisiana at Lafayette.
- **NASA career:** Started at NASA headquarters in New Orleans and then transferred to the **Marshall Space Flight Center** in Huntsville, Alabama, the nerve center of US rocket programs.
- **Specialty:** **Nuclear propulsion technology** for deep space missions.
- **Key roles:**
  - **Team lead** on the NASA **Space Nuclear Propulsion (SNP) Instrumentation and Control maturation** project.
  - Participation in the **DRACO project** (Demonstration Rocket for Agile Cislunar Operations), a nuclear thermal propulsion engine for missions to Mars and beyond.
- **Personal life:** Outdoors enthusiast who camped with his dog Odin. Known for his quick wit, charisma, and extroverted personality.
- **Died July 22, 2025**, at age 29, in Walker County, Alabama. His Tesla Model 3 was found burned after crashing into a barrier and trees.

## 10. Steven Garcia (1977 — missing since 2025)

**Property Custodian · Kansas City National Security Campus (KCNSC)**

- **Career:** Government contractor with **top security clearance** at the Albuquerque facility of the KCNSC, managed by Honeywell FM&T and overseen by the National Nuclear Security Administration.
- **Role:** **Property custodian** with oversight responsibility for tens to hundreds of millions of dollars in campus assets and equipment, part of which is classified.
- **KCNSC context:** The facility produces **more than 80% of the non-nuclear components** of US nuclear weapons, and collaborates closely with Sandia National Laboratories and Los Alamos.
- **Missing since August 28, 2025**, two days before his 48th birthday. Left his home in Albuquerque on foot wearing a green camouflage shirt and shorts, carrying only a handgun.

## 11. Nuno Filipe Gomes Loureiro (1977 — 2025)

**Theoretical Physicist · MIT Plasma Science and Fusion Center**

- **Origin:** Born in Viseu, central Portugal. Attended the Alves Martins Secondary School before his scientific career.
- **Education:**
  - Master's in Physics, **Instituto Superior Técnico (IST), Lisbon** (2000).
  - **PhD in Physics, Imperial College London** (2005). Thesis on nonlinear tearing modes in magnetic reconnection.
- **Postdoctoral career:**
  - **Princeton Plasma Physics Laboratory** (2005–2007).
  - **Culham Centre for Fusion Energy**, UK (2007–2009), inaugural Fusion Research Fellow.
  - Principal researcher at the **Institute for Plasmas and Nuclear Fusion (IST Lisbon)** (2009–2015), head of the Theory and Modeling group.
- **MIT career:**
  - 2016: Assistant professor.
  - 2017: Tenure.
  - 2021: Full professor.
  - 2022: Deputy director of the Plasma Science and Fusion Center (PSFC).
  - **May 2024: Director of the PSFC** (MIT's largest laboratory).
  - Received the **Herman Feshbach (1942) Professor of Physics** chair.
- **Scientific contributions:**
  - Fundamental advance in **magnetic reconnection** (the origin of solar flares and tokamak disruptions).
  - Proposed a new regime of tearing-mediated MHD turbulence.
  - Pioneer in applying **quantum computing to plasma physics**.
- **Awards:**
  - **Presidential Early Career Award for Scientists and Engineers (PECASE)**, 2025.
  - Fellow of the American Physical Society (2022).
  - Thomas H. Stix Award (APS, 2015).
  - Stanisław Ulam Distinguished Scholar (Los Alamos, 2023).
  - NSF CAREER Award (2017).
- **Personal life:** Married, three daughters. Enthusiast of local pickup soccer. Editor of the Journal of Plasma Physics since January 2025.
- **Died December 16, 2025**, at age 47, after being shot the previous night in the lobby of his building in Brookline, Massachusetts. The perpetrator, **Cláudio Manuel Neves Valente** (a former engineering program classmate in Portugal two decades earlier), had also carried out a shooting at Brown University days before.

## 12. Jason R. Thomas (1980 — 2025/2026)

**Chemical Biologist · Novartis Institutes for BioMedical Research (NIBR)**

- **Education:** PhD in chemistry with advanced degrees in **physics, biology, and biophysics**. University of Illinois at Urbana-Champaign.
- **Position:** **Assistant Director of Chemical Biology** at Novartis.
- **Research areas:**
  - Drug discovery.
  - **Cancer treatments**.
  - Chemoproteomics.
  - Phenotypic screening.
  - Identification of therapeutic targets.
- **Team work:** Led a team integrated in biochemical and biophysical assays.
- **Personal life:** Married to Kristen Bartoli, whom he met in college. Had five dogs (Dazzle, Prosecco, Champagne, St. Germain, and Fireball). In autumn 2025 he lost both parents and was going through a difficult period of grief.
- **Character:** His obituary describes him as "deeply compassionate and generous, firmly believing that his work could make a significant difference in people's lives."
- **Missing since December 12, 2025**, at age 45/46, after going for an evening walk from his home in Wakefield, Massachusetts. His body was recovered from **Lake Quannapowitt** on March 17, 2026. Authorities did not suspect foul play.

## 13. Carl J. Grillmair (1959 — 2026)

**Astronomer · Caltech IPAC**

- **Origin:** Born in 1959 in Calgary, Alberta, Canada.
- **Education:**
  - B.Sc. (Honours) in Astrophysics, **University of Calgary** (1983).
  - M.Sc. in Astronomy, **University of Victoria** (1986).
  - **PhD in Astronomy and Astrophysics, Australian National University** (1993).
  - Received the Petrie Fellowship, University of Toronto Doctoral Fellowship, NSERC, among others.
- **Career:** Almost 30 years at **IPAC (Infrared Processing and Analysis Center)** at Caltech, since 1997.
- **Scientific missions:**
  - Instrument teams on the **Spitzer Space Telescope (IRS, IRAC)**.
  - Deputy director of the IRS team.
  - Quality assurance scientist and pipeline operator at the **NEOWISE Science Data Center** (asteroid and comet hunter).
  - **NEO Surveyor**.
- **Scientific contributions:**
  - **2007 milestone:** Lead author of the study that **first identified molecules in an exoplanet's atmosphere** (HD 189733b), capturing enough light from extrasolar planets for spectroscopic analysis.
  - Discovered **dozens of stellar streams**, remnants of ancient collisions between the Milky Way and smaller galaxies. Many were named after him.
  - Studies on dark matter, galactic structure, stellar populations.
- **Productivity:** More than 400 hours as PI and 2,700 hours as co-PI on Hubble and Spitzer. **147 peer-reviewed papers** published.
- **Awards:**
  - **NASA Exceptional Scientific Achievement Medal** (2011).
  - Multiple NASA Group Achievement Awards.
  - "39 Canadians Who Make the World a Better Place to Live" (Maclean's, 2006).
- **Personal life:** Enthusiastic pilot of light aircraft and gliders that he maintained himself. Outdoors lover, tractor enthusiast, handyman. Maintained a personal observatory at his desert home in Llano (Mojave), chosen for its dark skies. Married to Louise.
- **Died February 16, 2026**, at age 67, on his porch from a gunshot wound. Authorities arrested a 29-year-old suspect (Freddy Snyder) who **did not know the victim**.

## 14. William Neil McCasland (c. 1957 — missing since 2026)

**Retired Major General · USAF · Former AFRL Commander**

- **Origin:** Grew up in an Air Force family. His father, Lieutenant William H. McCasland, died in an aviation accident when he was a child.
- **Academic education:**
  - **B.S. Astronautical Engineering, U.S. Air Force Academy** (1979).
  - **M.S. Aeronautical Engineering, MIT** (on full Hertz Foundation fellowship).
  - **PhD, MIT**.
  - Air War College, Maxwell AFB (1995).
  - Advanced Program Manager's Course, Defense Systems Management College, Fort Belvoir.
  - 2004: US-Russia Security Program, Kennedy School of Government, Harvard.
- **Military career (over 30 years):**
  - **Payload Systems Division**, Office of Special Projects-6 and -8, Los Angeles AFB (through 1985).
  - Assistant director, Office of Special Projects-13.
  - **Chief Engineer of the Navstar GPS Joint Program Office** (controlling authority of the GPS system for government, commercial, and consumer use).
  - 2000: Director of the **Space Based Laser Project Office** at LA AFB.
  - 2001–2004: Director of the Materiel Wing of the **AFRL Space Vehicles Directorate** and commander of the **Phillips Research Site**, Kirtland AFB, New Mexico.
  - 2004: Vice commander of the Ogden Air Logistics Center, Hill AFB, Utah.
  - Vice commander of the Space and Missile Systems Center, LA AFB.
  - Close work with the **National Reconnaissance Office (NRO)**.
  - **May 2011 – October 2013: Commander of the Air Force Research Laboratory (AFRL)**, Wright-Patterson AFB, Ohio. Managed **~$2.2 billion in science and technology programs** plus $2.2 billion in externally funded R&D.
- **Programs under his AFRL command:** Directed energy, advanced satellite systems, hypersonics, next-generation sensors, aerospace materials.
- **Post-retirement career:**
  - **Director of Technology, Applied Technology Associates** (subsidiary of BlueHalo, defense conglomerate in space warfare, directed energy, missile defense, cyber, and C4ISR).
  - **Associate Fellow** of the American Institute of Aeronautics and Astronautics (since 2013).
  - Board member of **Riverside Research** (nonprofit R&D organization for the government) since June 2019.
- **Personal life:** Married to Susan McCasland Wilkerson. Experienced hiker and outdoors enthusiast. Had a second home in Pagosa Springs, Colorado.
- **Missing since February 27, 2026**, at age 68. Left his home in Albuquerque on foot, leaving behind his phone, prescription glasses, and wearable devices. Took his wallet, hiking boots, and a .38 revolver. Had reported "mental fog" and sleep/short-term memory problems before his disappearance.
