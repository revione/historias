## 1. Gravitational Modification with Superconductors

### 1.1 The Ning Li and Douglas Torr Hypothesis (UAH, 1991–1994)

Between 1991 and 1993, **Ning Li** (mathematical physicist) and **Douglas G. Torr**, both at the University of Alabama in Huntsville, published a series of articles in indexed journals proposing that a superconductor could produce measurable gravitational effects. Their main papers were:

- **N. Li & D.G. Torr (1991)**, "Effects of a gravitomagnetic field on pure superconductors", _Physical Review D_ 43(2), pp. 457–459.
- **N. Li & D.G. Torr (1992)**, "Gravitational effects on the magnetic attenuation of superconductors", _Physical Review B_ 46(9), pp. 5489–5495.
- **D.G. Torr & N. Li (1993)**, "Gravitoelectric-electric coupling via superconductivity", _Foundations of Physics Letters_ 6(4), pp. 371–383.
- **N. Li, D.G. Torr & F.A. Karwacki (1994)**, "Lattice ion rotations and the dipole gravitomagnetic field of superconductors" (UAH preprint).

### 1.2 The Theory

The central idea is that in a superconductor:

- The **crystal lattice ions** (not the Cooper pairs) are the effective carriers of quantized angular momentum.
- If those ions rotate coherently, they generate a **gravitomagnetic field** (analogous to the magnetic field of moving electric charges, predicted by general relativity as "gravito-electromagnetism").
- If a large number of ions is aligned — for example, by trapping them in a lattice structure inside a **rotating high-temperature superconductor disc** and setting it to rotate — the collective effect could produce a detectable gravitomagnetic field.
- Additionally, if a time-dependent magnetic vector potential is applied, a **gravitoelectric field** could additionally be induced.

### 1.3 The Podkletnov Experiment

In parallel, Russian physicist **Eugene Podkletnov** published in _Physica C_ (1992) a work titled "A Possibility of Gravitational Force Shielding by Bulk YBa₂Cu₃O₇₋ₓ Superconductor". He reported that above a superconducting YBCO disc approximately 30 cm in diameter, cooled, levitated over magnets, and set to rotate, a test mass showed a **weight loss of 0.05% to 2.1%**. He called the effect **"gravitational force shielding."**

### 1.4 The Replication at NASA Marshall (1996–2002)

NASA Marshall Space Flight Center, together with the University of Alabama in Huntsville, attempted to replicate the effect. The project was led by:

- **Ron Koczor** (project manager at MSFC).
- **David Noever** (theoretical physicist).

Resulting publications:

- Li, N., Noever, D., Robertson, T., Koczor, R. and Brantley, W. (1997), "Static Test for a Gravitational Force Coupled to Type II YBCO Superconductors", _Physica C_ 281, pp. 260–267 — **negative result** with a non-rotating disc.
- Noever & Koczor, "Radio-frequency Illuminated Superconductive Disks: Reverse Josephson Effects and Implications for Precise Measuring of Proposed Gravity Effects", presented at the 9th Advanced Space Propulsion Research Workshop at NASA JPL.
- Noever, Koczor & Robertson, "Superconductor-Mediated Modification of Gravity? AC Motor Experiments with Bulk YBCO Disks in Rotating Magnetic Fields" (AIAA-98-3139, 34th Joint Propulsion Conference).

In 1999 several single-layer superconducting discs were manufactured in UAH-Marshall collaboration; Podkletnov was hired as a consultant and a bilayer disc was constructed. The complete experiment could never be finished due to resource constraints.

### 1.5 The Criticisms

- Edward G. Harris (1999), "Comments on 'Gravitoelectric-Electric Coupling via Superconductivity'", _Foundations of Physics Letters_ 12, pp. 201–208 — argued that the fields calculated by Torr and Li were **too large by many orders of magnitude** due to unrealistic assumptions.
- Hathaway et al. (2003) — repeated the experiment with **50 times greater sensitivity** than Podkletnov's and found no evidence of gravitational modification.
- de Podesta & Bull (1995) — proposed an alternative (non-gravitational) explanation for the original results.

### 1.6 Boeing GRASP

Around 2002, Boeing Phantom Works initiated the **GRASP (Gravity Research for Advanced Space Propulsion)** project, examining Podkletnov's gravitational shielding and "impulse beam" variants. Later reports indicate this effort also failed to replicate the effects.

## 2. Gravitational Modification (General Concepts)

### 2.1 Definitions

- **Gravitational modification:** Broad term. Includes shielding, attenuation, amplification, or redirection of the local gravitational field. Does not require violating general relativity per se.
- **Anti-gravity:** Stricter. Implies generating a repulsive force equivalent to or canceling gravitational attraction. Would require negative mass or some mechanism not described by current physics.
- **Gravito-Electromagnetism (GEM):** Approximate framework within general relativity describing gravity in analogy with electromagnetism, predicting "gravitomagnetic" fields generated by moving masses. Experimentally verified by **Gravity Probe B** (2011) — but the effects are extraordinarily small.

### 2.2 Historical Research Lines

- **Biefeld-Brown effect** (1920s) — Thomas Townsend Brown observed drift in high-voltage capacitors. Today considered an ionic effect (ion wind), not gravitational.
- **Tesla's dynamic theory of gravity** — speculative proposal without modern formalization.
- **Searl Generator** and **Dean Drive** — "fringe" proposals without verified physical basis.
- **Woodward Effect / Mach Effect** — proposal for reactionless propulsion based on transient variations in inertial mass. The **MEGA Drive** and **MET** (Mach Effect Thruster) device even received funding from NASA's NIAC program (NASA Innovative Advanced Concepts) in 2017–2018.
- **Gravitomagnetic Li-Torr-Modanese theories** about superconductors (Section 1).
- **EM Drive** (Roger Shawyer, 2001) — propellantless propulsion via microwaves in a conical cavity. Independent tests at Dresden University (2018) attributed the "detected signals" to magnetic interaction with cables, not real thrust.

## 4. Richard Eskridge — His NASA Career

Richard Eskridge is a retired engineer from **NASA Marshall Space Flight Center (MSFC)**, specialized in plasma physics and fusion technology. His work is documented in NASA technical publications. Main areas:

### 4.1 Pulsed Plasma Accelerators

- **PEPA-0** (Pulsed Electromagnetic Plasma Accelerator) — coaxial pulsed thruster developed at MSFC. Experiments achieved **plasma jet velocities of approximately 50 km/s**.

### 4.2 Magnetized Target Fusion (MTF)

Hybrid scheme between inertial and magnetic confinement fusion. The idea is to compress a magnetized plasma through a "liner" (a converging layer). Eskridge investigated the use of **spherically distributed plasma jets that dynamically merge** to form a gaseous liner that implodes the magnetized target. He studied interfacial stability using Orr-Sommerfeld equations.

### 4.3 FRC Acceleration Space Thruster (FAST)

Research on the use of a repetitive **Field Reversed Configuration (FRC)** source as a thruster for **nuclear-electric propulsion (NEP)** systems. An FRC is a plasmoid with closed poloidal field lines.

### 4.4 Plasmoids for Propulsion

Studied plasmoids — compact plasma structures with integral magnetic field — as space propulsion agents. Plasmoids are classified by the ratio between poloidal (Bp) and toroidal (Bt) magnetic fields.

### 4.5 Other Contributions

- Fuel injectors for rocket engines using optical techniques (Raman, fluorescence, Mie scattering).
- Studies on atomization of swirling liquid sheets (formation of ligaments and droplets).

- **Total funding:** US$1.2 million (modest) over six years of operation.

## 6. Anti-Gravity Propulsion

### 6.1 Why It's Difficult

The gravitational force is **always attractive** according to classical general relativity, because the field source (mass-energy) is always positive. Producing anti-gravity would require:

- **Negative mass** (not observed).
- **Negative energy** (partially possible in quantum effects like the Casimir effect, but in negligible quantities).
- Modifying the **local cosmological constant** (theoretically proposed by Modanese, 1996).

### 6.2 Diametric Drive (Forward & Woodward)

If blocks of negative mass existed, attaching them to positive masses would accelerate the system indefinitely without propellant, because negative mass "falls toward" positive, while positive "flees from" negative. The entire system accelerates in one direction without violating conservation of momentum (mathematically). This was analyzed by Robert L. Forward and Jamie Woodward within the NASA BPP.

### 6.3 Reactionless Propulsion

Broader category that includes anti-gravity but also proposals such as the **EM Drive** and **Mach Effect Thruster**, all pending robust experimental validation.

## 7. Electrostatic Propulsion

This category is widely proven and is **mature operational technology**. It is one of the methods of "electric propulsion" in space.

### 7.1 Main Types

| Type | Mechanism | Thrust | Isp |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Gridded ion engine** (Kaufman) | Ionization + acceleration through high-voltage grids | < 0.5 N | 1,500–10,000 s |
| **Hall-effect thruster (HET)** | Electrostatic acceleration with transverse magnetic field | 0.05–0.2 N | 1,000–3,000 s |
| **Electrospray / FEEP** | Extraction of ions from conductive liquids with needles | < 0.001 N | > 1,000 s |

### 7.2 History

- **1959:** Harold R. Kaufman builds the first functional ion thruster at NASA Lewis Research Center (mercury as propellant).
- **1964:** Suborbital flight **SERT-1** (Space Electric Rocket Test) — 31-minute operation.
- **1970:** **SERT-2** — orbital test.
- **1971:** The first Hall thruster in space flies on a Soviet Meteor satellite.
- **1972–1990s:** Hall thrusters routinely operational on Soviet satellites.
- **1992:** A team from NASA JPL, Glenn Research Center, and AFRL (with BMDO support) visits Russia and brings the **SPT-100 HET** to the West.

### 7.3 Notable Missions with Electrostatic Propulsion

- **Deep Space 1** (NASA, 1998) — first interplanetary mission with ion thruster (NSTAR).
- **Hayabusa 1 and 2** (JAXA, 2003 and 2014) — asteroid sample return.
- **SMART-1** (ESA, 2003) — first European Hall thruster to the Moon.
- **Dawn** (NASA, 2007) — exploration of Vesta and Ceres with three NSTAR engines.
- **BepiColombo** (ESA/JAXA, 2018) — journey to Mercury.
- **DART** (NASA, 2021) — NEXT-C thruster as secondary propulsion.
- **Starlink** (SpaceX) — argon Hall thrusters on every operational satellite.
- **Tiangong** (China) — first Hall thruster on a crewed mission (8,240 hours of continuous operation).

## 8. Gravity Research: Classified vs. Open Civil

### 8.1 Amy Eskridge's Concern

Her public thesis was that a disparity exists between:

- **Open civil programs** (peer-reviewed, limited funding: BPP, NIAC) — which have not produced clear advances.
- **Classified programs** ("black projects") — where, according to her claims, more advanced research might exist that is not subject to normal scientific disclosure.

She argued that the **Mansfield Amendment** of 1973 (which restricted DoD funding to research with direct military application) pushed exotic research out of the open academic domain and toward private contractors with non-disclosure agreements.

### 8.2 Declared (Open) Programs

- Breakthrough Propulsion Physics Program (NASA, 1996–2002).
- NIAC (NASA, ongoing).
- BPP cancelled, without a formal successor of equal ambition.
- Isolated university programs (UAH, Imperial College, Tampere).

### 8.3 Alleged but Unconfirmed Programs

- **TR-3B** — supposed triangular anti-gravity aircraft. No verifiable documentary evidence. Mentioned in Eskridge's presentations as an example of a "black" program type.
- **"Legacy UFO program"** — alleged by Matthew Sullivan according to Liberation Times and Rep. Burlison; presented as a program for recovering unidentified objects active since the mid-twentieth century through various private contractors.

> Note: The existence of the latter has not been confirmed by official sources and is part of the set of unverified claims of the "UAP disclosure movement."

### 8.4 The Documented FBI Case on Huntsville

A 2013 article (cited in independent investigations) described Huntsville as "a primary target for foreign nationals (spies) seeking to obtain classified information" according to FBI spokesman Paul Daymond. The National Classification Management Society, the Defense Security Service, and Lockheed Martin Space Systems organized a security conference in the city for this reason.

## 9. The DART Project in Detail

### 9.1 Mission

**DART (Double Asteroid Redirection Test)** was NASA's first mission specifically dedicated to testing asteroid deflection as a **planetary defense technique**.

- **Operated by:** Johns Hopkins Applied Physics Laboratory (JHU/APL), under the direction of NASA's Planetary Defense Coordination Office.
- **Launch:** November 24, 2021 (Falcon 9, SpaceX).
- **Impact:** **September 26, 2022, 23:14 UTC**.
- **Cost:** ~US$324.5 million.

### 9.2 The Target

**Binary system (65803) Didymos**:

- **Didymos** (primary): ~780 meters in diameter.
- **Dimorphos** (satellite): ~160 meters in diameter, oblate. Was the direct impact target.

Why a binary? Because the success metric (change in Dimorphos's orbital period around Didymos) can be measured from Earth-based telescopes with precision, without depending on instruments in space.

### 9.3 The DART Spacecraft

- Mass at impact: ~570 kg.
- Impact velocity: **6.14 km/s** (relative to Dimorphos).
- Autonomous navigation system: **SMART Nav** with camera **DRACO** (Didymos Reconnaissance and Asteroid Camera for Optical navigation).
- Propulsion: **NEXT-C** ion engine (Hall/gridded ion thruster), technology demonstration.
- Companion: **LICIACube** (Italy, ASI) — CubeSat released a few days before impact that photographed the collision and ejecta.

### 9.4 Results

- **Impact point:** within 25 meters of Dimorphos's center figure.
- **Change in orbital period:** from 11.92 hours to **11.37 hours**, i.e., a reduction of **33.0 ± 1.0 minutes** (3-σ). The mission success threshold was just a change of 73 seconds.
- **Reduction in orbital velocity:** Δv = **2.70 ± 0.10 mm/s** in Dimorphos's along-track component.
- **β factor (momentum enhancement):** between 2.2 and 4.9 depending on assumed density (1,500–3,300 kg/m³). For density of 2,400 kg/m³: **β = 3.61** (+0.19/-0.25, 1-σ).
- **Implication of β > 1:** The momentum transferred to Dimorphos was **considerably greater than DART's incident momentum**, due to _ejecta recoil_.

### 9.5 Successor Mission

**Hera (ESA, launched October 7, 2024)** will arrive at the Didymos system in 2026 to characterize in detail the crater, Dimorphos's mass (today the largest uncertainty in the β calculation), and the long-term effects.

### 9.6 Key Scientific Publications

The first peer-reviewed results were published in _Nature_ in 2023:

- **Daly et al.**, "Successful Kinetic Impact into an Asteroid for Planetary Defense", _Nature_ 616, 443–447 (2023).
- **Thomas et al.**, "Orbital period change of Dimorphos due to the DART kinetic impact", _Nature_ 616, 448–451 (2023).
- **Cheng et al.**, "Momentum transfer from the DART mission kinetic impact on asteroid Dimorphos", _Nature_ 616, 457–460 (2023).
- **Li et al.**, on the ejecta tail seen by Hubble.
- **Graykowski et al.**, on the dust plume from Earth-based telescopes.

## 10. Kinetic Impact: The Physics

### 10.1 The Concept

A **kinetic impactor** deflects an asteroid by transferring momentum through a direct high-speed collision. The idea in its simple form:

$$
m_{impactor} \cdot v_{impact} = M_{asteroid} \cdot \Delta v
$$

But the reality is more complex due to **ejecta**.

### 10.2 The β Factor (Beta)

When a spacecraft hits an asteroid, in addition to the direct momentum transfer, the crater expels **material at high velocity in the direction opposite to the spacecraft**. This ejecta acts like an improvised rocket: by Newton's third law, the asteroid receives an additional push.

$$
\beta = 1 + \frac{p_{ejecta}}{p_{impactor}}
$$

If **β = 1**, there is no ejecta (perfectly inelastic collision, worst case).
If **β > 1**, the ejecta amplify the effect.

DART measured **β ≈ 3.6**, meaning the deflection was ~3.6 times more efficient than a simple collision. This has **enormous implications for planetary defense**: a smaller spacecraft can deflect a larger asteroid than conservative theory suggested.

### 10.3 Variables That Affect β

- **Asteroid porosity:** "Rubble pile" asteroids tend to have higher β.
- **Density and mechanical strength.**
- **Impact angle and velocity.**
- **Surface composition.**

This is why it is so important to first characterize candidate asteroids (hence the role of infrared telescopes like Spitzer, NEOWISE, and NEO Surveyor — the projects on which Carl Grillmair and Michael David Hicks worked).

### 10.4 Other Planetary Defense Techniques

- **Gravity tractor:** a massive spacecraft flies near the asteroid and "pulls" it gravitationally over years.
- **Laser ablation:** sustained surface vaporization that acts as a thruster.
- **Nuclear standoff detonation:** nearby explosion, no contact, to push via X-ray radiation.
- **Ion beam shepherd:** the spacecraft fires an ion beam at the asteroid.

DART is the only one tested at real scale.
