## What Is Being Studied

There are two questions here, and they require different material.

One: whether these deaths and disappearances have a common cause.

Another: how a list assembled from unrelated cases reached an FBI review and a question in the White House press room.

The first is decided with numbers. The second is decided by the trail of who published what and on what date.

This text works on the first and leaves the second noted.

---

## The Base Rates

Five figures, all from official statistical sources, all annual and referring to the U.S.

1. **More than 500,000 missing-person reports per year.** FBI, 2024 NCIC Missing and Unidentified Person Statistics.
2. **Between 1,200 and 1,600 disappearances per year inside national parks alone.**
3. **More than 900,000 deaths per year from heart disease and related diseases.** CDC.
4. **Close to 50,000 suicides per year**, of which 27,300 involved firearms. CDC and Johns Hopkins Center for Gun Violence Solutions.
5. **About 7,500 pedestrians killed per year in traffic crashes.**

*Compilation: Michael Shermer, Skeptic, April 25, 2026, with a link to each primary source. The figures are official; the selection is his.*

The magnitude proposed on the other side: eleven cases in four years, between 2022 and 2026.

Against that background, each individual case on the list falls within what the rates produce without needing an added cause. One arteriosclerosis death among 900,000. One firearm suicide among 27,300. Four disappearances among 500,000 reports.

### The Population the List Comes From

More than 700,000 people hold *top secret* clearances in the U.S. aerospace and nuclear sectors. Applying ordinary mortality rates to that population, the period covered by the list produces on the order of 250 homicides and suicides, plus several thousand natural deaths.

*Origin: estimate by Mick West, independent analyst, cited in Vanity Fair on April 22, 2026. It is not an official figure. It decides the order of magnitude, not the decimal.*

Eleven against two hundred and fifty, in the most restrictive category.

And the real population is larger still. It includes retirees, contractors, administrative personnel, and relatives who claim cases.

### The Entry Criterion Is Not Bounded

In April 2026 the list had ten or eleven names. In August 2026 journalistic counts are at thirteen.

A list that grows every time someone searches does not measure a rate. It measures search effort.

Two recent entries show this.

**Ning Li**, antigravity researcher. She died in 2021 at age 78 from Alzheimer's, after a long decline that began with a 2014 traffic crash on the University of Alabama in Huntsville campus. She entered the list as a suspicious death.

**David Wilcock**, paranormal writer and YouTuber, died by suicide on April 20, 2026. The Boulder County sheriff's office indicated that the 911 operator suspected a mental health crisis during the call. A congressman stated that it does not look like a coincidence to him.

## The Mechanism Already Has a Published Name

Three names, all three with literature behind them.

**Patternicity.** The tendency to find meaningful patterns in random noise.

**Base rate neglect.** Attending to vivid, anecdotal evidence and ignoring the statistical generalization that better explains the phenomenon. It has been measured in cognitive psychology.

**Fallacy of excluded exceptions.** This is the most useful one here, and it can be seen in a four-cell matrix.

```text
                         dies young / disappears    no
UFO/nuclear scientist           cell 1            cell 2
other scientist                 cell 3            cell 4
```

The list is all of cell 1. The other three are never counted.

How many nuclear and aerospace scientists remain alive and in their posts. How many scientists in other fields died or disappeared in the same period. Without those two cells, cell 1 says nothing.

*The three terms, with links to the literature, in Shermer, Skeptic, April 25, 2026.*

## The Cases, With What Is Established

### Cause Determined by Medical Examiner or Judicial Authority

**Michael David Hicks**, 59, JPL scientist between 1998 and 2022, comets and asteroids. Died on July 30, 2023.

The circulating version says "undisclosed cause." According to the Los Angeles County medical examiner, the cause was arteriosclerotic cardiovascular disease. His daughter told CNN he had known medical problems. His brother publicly expressed discomfort with the association to the list.

**Nuno Loureiro**, director of MIT's Plasma Science and Fusion Center, shot at his home in Brookline in December 2025. The perpetrator was a former classmate from the 1990s, who confessed to having planned it for years out of envy and resentment toward his career. On April 29, 2026, the FBI and the federal prosecutor's office in Massachusetts concluded that Claudio Manuel Neves Valente acted alone and that the crime has no nexus to terrorism.

**Amy Eskridge**, 34, president of the Institute for Exotic Science, antigravity and electrostatic propulsion. Alabama, June 2022. Suicide by gunshot wound. Her father told NewsNation that scientists also die, like everyone else.

**Jason Thomas**, 45, chemical biology researcher at Novartis. Disappeared in December 2025, found on March 17, 2026, in a lake near Wakefield, Massachusetts. He had lost his parents shortly before.

### With an Open Judicial Process

**Carl Grillmair**, 67, IPAC (Caltech) astronomer since 1977. Shot on the porch of his home in Llano on February 16, 2026. Death ruled a homicide by gunshot wound to the torso.

Freddy Snyder, 29, lived about three kilometers away. He is charged with murder, carjacking, and first-degree residential burglary, pleaded not guilty, and has bail set at $3.175 million.

What did not appear in the previous version of this post. On December 20, 2025, Snyder was reported for being on Grillmair's property. Deputies found him nearby with a loaded, unregistered rifle and arrested him for a weapons offense. A neighbor told the Los Angeles Times that Snyder broke into his house on December 28, days after being released. Less than two weeks before the shooting, prosecutors dropped the charges from that first episode.

There is a documented failure of a local judicial system there, and it stands on its own, without any hypothesis about the list.

*Unresolved: whether the two men knew each other. The sheriff's office and ABC7 said they did not. Los Angeles Magazine later published that the widow described her husband seeing Snyder on the property in December and finding him odd, and that he declined to press charges. Shermer wrote that Grillmair was the one who called the police; LA Magazine corrected that point and says it is unknown who made the call.*

### With an Explanation Provided by Someone With Access

**William Neil McCasland**, 68, retired Major General, seventh commander of the Air Force Research Laboratory. He left his Albuquerque home on foot on February 27, 2026. He is still missing.

The 911 audio from that day, released by Law&Crime Network, contains the following. His wife says she has indications that he planned not to be found. She says he turned off his phone and left it at home, something he never did. She says both of them were in medical treatment for anxiety, short-term memory loss, and lack of sleep. And she says he had been repeating that if his brain and body kept deteriorating, he did not want to live like that.

He left his phone, prescription glasses, and devices. He took his boots, wallet, and a .38 revolver with holster.

The Bernalillo County Sheriff's Office stated that there is no evidence of foul play, that McCasland had a standard retiree clearance, and that there is no recorded connection between this case and the others. His wife posted on March 6 that since his retirement, thirteen years earlier, he had held only very common clearances, and that it seems unlikely to her that he would be taken to extract secrets that old. She added that his relationship with the UFO community was limited to consulting for a work of fiction.

**Melissa Casias**, 53, administrative assistant at Los Alamos with a security clearance. She disappeared on June 26, 2025. She was seen around 2:15 p.m. walking along a road near Talpa, about five kilometers from her home in Taos.

Her daughter found the car, purse, keys, and two phones at home, one work phone and one personal phone, both factory-reset.

Her remains appeared on May 28, 2026, in the McGaffey Ridge area, Carson National Forest, with a pistol beside them.

Her husband and daughter maintain that she left of her own will, under personal and financial pressure. Her mother and sister reject that.

Cause and manner of death: undetermined by the New Mexico Office of the Medical Investigator as of the latest published information, July 2026.

### Unresolved

**Frank Maiwald**, 61, JPL engineer with 25 years of service. Died on July 4, 2024, in Los Angeles. The cause has not been made public.

**Monica Jacinto Reza**, 60, director of JPL's Materials Processing Group. Disappeared on June 22, 2025, in Angeles National Forest, near Mount Waterman. Not located.

*Unresolved, twice. One: police speak of a companion about nine meters away who turned around and she was no longer there; Shermer writes that there were two experienced hikers and that she left the trail. Two: some sources describe her as working in materials and superalloys, others in orbital communication systems.*

The area has steep slopes and dense brush, and there are disappearances there every year.

**Anthony Chavez**, 78, retired Los Alamos employee. Disappeared on foot from his home on May 4, 2025. Exhaustive searches with no result. Not located.

**Steven Garcia**, 48, contractor with a security clearance. Left his Albuquerque home on foot on August 28, 2025. He took a pistol and left his phone, keys, wallet, and car. Not located.

## What the Original Graphic Was Doing

Each person had a section that explained what they had access to and why that access was valuable. That section is the engine of the piece and accepts any entry.

Any twenty-year technical career produces that paragraph. No biography on the list would produce the opposite result, because the phrase "his access was irrelevant" does not exist when the person worked at a national laboratory. It is cell 1 of the matrix, written person by person.

Three concrete formulations and their descriptive version.

**"The institutional silence of NASA and JPL regarding the cause of death contrasts with their usual communication in cases involving high-profile personnel."**

Buried assumption: that NASA publishes its employees' causes of death. It does not. Cause of death is determined by the medical examiner and its release depends on state law and the family. In Hicks's case, moreover, the cause is recorded by the county medical examiner.

Descriptive version: NASA did not publish Maiwald's cause of death. NASA does not publish its employees' causes of death.

**"The objects he left behind versus those he took suggest a planned departure, not a spontaneous cognitive episode."**

The sentence presents as alternatives two things that are compatible. The 911 audio describes cognitive decline under medical treatment and a deliberate departure at the same time, from the person with the most access to the case.

**"The factory reset of two phones is an atypical forensic detail. Most voluntary disappearances do not include the methodical destruction of digital traces."**

The second sentence asserts a base rate with no figure behind it. No one has published what fraction of voluntary departures includes wiping devices. Without that number, the sentence informs nothing.

## Corrections to the Previous Version of This Post

**Hicks.** It said the cause of death had not been disclosed. It is recorded by the Los Angeles County medical examiner: arteriosclerotic cardiovascular disease.

**Grillmair and the detainee.** It said the suspect did not know the victim and that there was no apparent motive. Snyder lived three kilometers away and had been arrested in December 2025 on Grillmair's property with a loaded rifle. Whether they came to know each other remains unclear.

**Grillmair and the molecules.** He was credited with the first identification of molecules in an exoplanet's atmosphere. Sodium in HD 209458b was detected in 2002. What Caltech credits him with in 2007 is having been first author of the work that first captured enough light from exoplanets to obtain their spectra. The exact wording changes the milestone. *Warning: precision against the literature, with no journalistic source that records it.*

**Chavez and DARHT.** It was asserted that he supervised construction of the DARHT facility and knew its shielding and plans. Public sources describe him as a retired employee who worked as a construction foreman. The attribution to DARHT circulates in the graphic and does not appear in any verifiable source. *Unverified.*

**McCasland's position.** He was placed as director of technology at Applied Technology Associates. NewsNation reported in June 2026 that he had tried to resign from a position at Riverside Research days before disappearing and that he had left several retirees' working groups because of what he described as brain fog. *Unresolved: two employers in different sources, with no date ordering them.*

**Count.** The previous post listed seven people and spoke of eleven cases. Eskridge, Loureiro, Thomas, and Garcia were missing. August 2026 counts are at thirteen.

## What Would Close the Matter

In the direction of connection, any of these things:

1. The same perpetrator, funder, or intermediary identified in two or more cases.
2. A classification or restriction order on a police file from the list, with date and number.
3. A forensic match between two cases: same weapon, same signature, same scene pattern.
4. A measured excess mortality in the cleared population, compared with the expected rate. Measured, with all four cells counted, not manually compiled from headlines.

In the opposite direction, case by case:

1. The determination of cause and manner for Casias.
2. The location of Reza, Chavez, Garcia, and McCasland.
3. Maiwald's cause of death.

The base rates already close the version that says eleven cases in four years is, by itself, a finding. What remains open is individual, and four people are still missing.

### The Link Between Reza and McCasland

The Oversight Committee noted that they overlapped in the early 2000s in an Air Force-funded program on advanced materials for reusable space vehicles.

The field of asteroid characterization and deflection has a few hundred specialists (Fortune, April 2026), and the field of Air Force-funded aerospace materials is just as narrow. That any two names from that world overlapped twenty years ago is what would be expected.

What would turn the link into evidence: that it was recent, that it was exclusive to the two of them, and that they did not share it with dozens of colleagues who are still alive. None of the three conditions has been shown.

## What Remains Open and Does Not Depend on Any Theory

Reza, Chavez, Garcia, and McCasland have been missing for months or years. Three of the four in New Mexico.

In the Casias case, a private team hired by the family and volunteers from 4Corners K-9 Search and Rescue returned to the site of the remains almost a month after state police had deemed the scene processed. According to the family's attorney, a former federal prosecutor, they found additional bone fragments, hair, remnants of clothing, and other items. New Mexico State Police have not published a detailed reply.

In the Grillmair case, prosecutors dropped the charges against the man who is now accused of the homicide less than two weeks before the homicide.

Both are problems of resources and judgment in specific local institutions. They stand on their own.

---

## Official Status, as of August 14, 2026

The FBI stated in April that it is leading the review, with the Department of Energy, the Department of Defense, and state and local police. The House Oversight Committee sent letters to the FBI, NASA, DOE, and DoD. Committee staff recorded on April 16 that DoD was not investigating any disappearance associated with these cases. NASA stated that nothing related to the agency indicates a threat to national security.

To date, no conclusion has been published establishing a link between the cases. The review remains open.

*This section expires. Verify before citing it.*

## The Families

Hicks's daughter and brother, Grillmair's widow, Eskridge's father, McCasland's wife, and colleagues from JPL and Caltech have publicly rejected the connection. Several have described the effect of the theory on their grief. The New York Times devoted a piece to that on June 2, 2026.

That does not decide whether there is a pattern. It is a cost of publishing the list, and it goes on the ledger of what the text one writes costs.
