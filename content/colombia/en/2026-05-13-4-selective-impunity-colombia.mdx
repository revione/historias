# Selective Impunity in Colombia

In Colombia, whether corruption is punished depends on who you are and who you've harmed. No government has truly convicted its own allies. The pattern is always the same: when the accused is on the opposing side, the investigation moves fast; when they're on your side, the case is archived, delayed, or the file "gets lost."

This document compiles the most emblematic cases of impunity across all recent governments to show that the problem is neither left-wing nor right-wing: **it is structural**.

---

## Álvaro Uribe Government (2002-2010)

### The Parapolitics Scandal
More than **60 members of Congress** were convicted for ties to paramilitaries. The **Pact of Ralito** (2001) was an agreement between paramilitary leaders (Mancuso, Don Berna, Jorge 40) and politicians to "refound the fatherland." Signatories included Senators Miguel De la Espriella, Álvaro Araújo, Juan Manuel López Cabrales, among others.

**Álvaro Uribe Vélez** was investigated by the Supreme Court for procedural fraud and bribery (witness tampering case). He was a senator until 2024. As of 2026, **he has no final conviction**. His process has been riddled with delays, challenges to magistrates, and changes of judge.

**Musa Besaile** (senator, convicted for parapolitics): paid **$2 billion pesos** to the Cartel of the Robe to stop his arrest warrant. His 6-year sentence (July 2024) was one of the few that reached a conclusion.

**Jorge Noguera Cotes**: director of DAS (2002-2005), sentenced to **25 years** for parapolitics and homicide. DAS illegally wiretapped Supreme Court magistrates, journalists, and political opponents.

**María del Pilar Hurtado**: director of DAS (2007-2008), sentenced to **14 years** for conspiracy to commit a crime and illegal wiretapping. She sought asylum in Panama.

### Abelardo de la Espriella
Congressman for the Cambio Radical Party, close to the Uribista movement. Convicted for parapolitics. Served his sentence under **house arrest**. Today he remains active in politics.

## Juan Manuel Santos Government (2010-2018)

### Odebrecht in Colombia
The largest corruption case in the country's history.

**The contract**: Odebrecht (Brazil) partnered with **Corficolombiana** (of Grupo Aval) to build the **Ruta del Sol II**. Contract worth over **$2 trillion pesos**. Bribes of **US$6.5 million** were paid to Deputy Minister Gabriel García Morales to win the bid.

**Gabriel García Morales**: Deputy Minister of Transport, convicted of bribery. He confessed to receiving the bribe and implicated senior officials.

**Néstor Humberto Martínez**: served as **Grupo Aval's lawyer** before being appointed Attorney General (2016). As attorney general, he shielded the Odebrecht investigation. Auditor **Jorge Enrique Pizano** recorded him saying of the irregular payments: *"That's a bribe, asshole"* —and asking him for "discretion." Pizano died of a "heart attack" on November 7, 2018; his son Alejandro died of cyanide poisoning 3 days later while reviewing the evidence. **The investigation into both deaths was archived**. Martínez resigned in 2019. As of 2026, **he has no conviction**.

**"Official 3"**: a senior official of the 2010-2018 government who received **US$3.4 million** in March 2014 to approve the Ocaña-Gamarra addition without bidding. Unidentified. The Petro government asked the U.S. to reveal their identity in December 2025.

**Juan Manuel Santos**: his re-election campaign (2014) received money from Odebrecht via Corficolombiana. The Accusations Committee **archived** the investigation in 2021. As of 2026, **he has no conviction**.

### Paloma Valencia and Grupo Aval
Senator **Paloma Valencia** (Centro Democrático, Uribista) fiercely defended Grupo Aval during the November 2018 Odebrecht debate, calling the holding company a generator of "80,000 direct jobs" and "happiness" for the country. In 2018, **Grupo Aval donated $2.45 billion pesos** to Centro Democrático. On the same day as the debate, Valencia **deflected attention** by publishing a video of Gustavo Petro receiving money — a move that Centro Democrático sources attributed to Martínez himself through intermediaries, just as Martínez was about to be questioned in the Senate. As of 2026, Valencia **has no conviction**.

### Grupo Aval
In August 2023, Corficolombiana (Grupo Aval) signed a Deferred Prosecution Agreement with the U.S. Department of Justice. It admitted to paying **US$23 million in bribes** to Colombian officials between 2012-2015. It paid **US$80 million in fines** ($40.6M to DOJ + $40M to SEC). The agreement does not exonerate individuals. In Colombia, no Grupo Aval executive has gone to prison.

Grupo Aval owns:
- **Porvenir** (the largest pension fund manager in Colombia)
- **Banco de Bogotá**
- **Banco Popular**
- **Banco de Occidente**
- **Corficolombiana**

## Iván Duque Government (2018-2022)

### Duque's 2018 Campaign
Investigated by the CNE for alleged campaign finance violations. **Archived** in 2022.

### Francisco Barbosa
Attorney General (2020-2024), appointed by the Supreme Court from a shortlist sent by Duque. His tenure was marked by a **total confrontation with the Petro government**. He investigated Petro's campaign and Nicolás Petro, and was accused by the latter of attempting to **extort President Petro**: offering to halt the judicial process against Nicolás in exchange for Petro leaving Martha Mancera at the Prosecutor's Office. Barbosa denied it. He faced criticism for **not investigating Uribista congressmen with the same intensity**.

## Gustavo Petro Government (2022-2026)

### UNGRD — National Unit for Disaster Risk Management
**Olmedo López** and **Sneyder Pinilla** (UNGRD directors) allegedly diverted **$50 billion+ pesos** in water tanker truck contracts. Part of those funds allegedly financed bribes to congressmen to approve budget loans. As of 2026, **there are no convictions**.

### Nicolás Petro
The president's son, arrested on March 21, 2023. Charged with **money laundering** and **illicit enrichment**. He allegedly received money from Alfonso "el Turco" Hilsaca and Santander Lopesierra ("Hombre Marlboro"), individuals with criminal records, supposedly for his father's presidential campaign. He pleaded not guilty in January 2025. Trial ongoing.

### Petro Presidente 2022 Campaign — CNE Sanction
On November 27, 2025, the CNE sanctioned the campaign for exceeding spending limits by **$3.042 billion pesos** and concealing **$3.698 billion pesos** (total unreported: $6.011 billion). **Prohibited funding sources** were used: legal entities (FECODE, USO, Ingenial Media). **35,584 election witnesses** were paid **$1.108 billion pesos** without reporting.

**Ricardo Roa** (campaign manager, now president of Ecopetrol): jointly fined over **$5 billion pesos**. The CNE referred the case to the Prosecutor's Office for criminal investigation.

**Armando Benedetti**: in leaked WhatsApp audios (2023), told Laura Sarabia: *"If I talk, everyone goes to prison, because I know how they got that money."* He was referring to **$15 billion pesos** he allegedly obtained on the coast for the campaign. As of 2026, **he has no conviction**. He was appointed Minister of the Interior in 2025.

### Criticism of Selectivity
Petro is **the first president in Colombian history** formally sanctioned by the CNE for his campaign. This does not mean his campaign was clean (the findings are documented). But it is part of a pattern where the electoral system —controlled by the traditional parties— scrutinizes the newcomer with a fine-tooth comb while archiving cases against their own.

## The Common Pattern

| Case | Government | Amount | Are there convictions? |
|------|-----------|--------|----------------------|
| Parapolitics | Uribe | — | 60 congressmen convicted, Uribe without conviction |
| Cartel de la Toga | All | $2B+ in bribes | 50+ arrests (magistrates) |
| Odebrecht | Santos | US$23 million | Martínez shielded it, Santos without conviction |
| Grupo Aval | All | US$80M fine in U.S. | Zero imprisoned in Colombia |
| UNGRD | Petro | $50B+ | No one in prison |
| Petro 2022 Campaign | Petro | $6.011B concealed | Sanctioned by CNE, no criminal conviction |

The pattern is clear: **in Colombia, corruption is punished when politically convenient**. Cases against the opposing side move forward; cases against one's own side get archived. The judicial system is not independent: it is part of the political game.

Until that changes, no constituent assembly, no law, no reform will be of any use.
