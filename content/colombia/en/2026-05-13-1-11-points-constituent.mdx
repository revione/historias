# Petro's 11 Points for the Constituent Assembly

In April 2026, President Gustavo Petro proposed a National Constituent Assembly (Asamblea Nacional Constituyente) with 11 points. According to him, it is not about replacing the 1991 Constitution but rather adding these points that Congress has blocked.

This document breaks down each point with a fixed structure, but there is a thread connecting them all: **real transparency as the only shield against corruption**.

Each point is analyzed with:
1. **What it seeks to achieve** — the real intention behind the proposal
2. **How it would work** — how it would function in practice
3. **Why they won't allow it** — who is blocking it and why
4. **The smokescreens** — the false arguments used to oppose it
5. **What they are hiding** — what they are really protecting
6. **The money trail** — how those resources are stolen today
7. **Real solutions** — what actually works, with or without the constituent assembly

But the underlying solution is neither public nor private: it is **transparent**. A system where anyone can consult in real time every peso that comes in and goes out, every decision made, every result obtained. When everything is visible, corruption does not magically disappear, but it becomes so risky that almost no one dares to try it.

## 1. Health Reform (Reforma a la Salud)

> "Eliminate corrupt intermediaries and allow immediate care for the sick person, strengthen the preventive health system, fortify the public hospital network, and finance drinking water and nutrition for all Colombians as a priority"

### What it seeks to achieve
Eliminate EPS (Entidades Promotoras de Salud — Health Promoting Entities) as intermediaries between the State and hospitals. So that the money the government allocates for healthcare goes directly to those who treat patients, without a private company taking a commission along the way.

### How it would work
ADRES (Administradora de los Recursos del Sistema de Salud — Health System Resources Administrator) would pay hospitals (IPS) directly without going through EPS. The system would be 100% public: the State collects, the State administers, the State pays hospitals. The EPS figure as a private insurer would be eliminated.

### Why they won't allow it
The owners of EPS, their shareholders, the politicians who have used EPS to hire their friends and family, the congress members who receive "contributions" from these companies for their campaigns. The healthcare business in Colombia moves more than **$80 trillion (COP) annually**. Each percentage point that an EPS keeps is $800 billion that does not go to patient care.

### The smokescreens
- **"The State lacks administrative capacity"** — the State already manages everyone's taxes, already collects healthcare system funds (via ADRES), already has public hospitals, already pays the payroll. Capacity is built, not a divine gift. The argument is that private entities "administer better" when the track record shows otherwise: EPS have stolen the money and gone bankrupt, leaving thousands without care.
- **"It will be like Cuba or Venezuela"** — Colombia is neither Cuba nor Venezuela, and the current system is already terrible. Millions of Colombians wait months for an appointment or die at emergency room doors. Saying that "the risk of collapse" exists with a public system ignores that the current system **has already collapsed**.
- **"Public hospitals also steal"** — true. But it is easier to track corruption in a direct flow of resources than in a system with 20 opaque intermediaries that hide transactions. Opacity is not a defect of the current system; it is its purpose.

### What they are hiding
That the opacity of the EPS system allows billions of pesos to be diverted without anyone being able to trace them. Each layer of intermediation is a smokescreen for the money to disappear. EPS do not exist because they are efficient; they exist because they allow healthcare money to be diverted into private pockets.

### The money trail
1. The State (ADRES) transfers the **UPC** (Unidad de Pago por Capitación — Capitation Payment Unit) to the EPS for each enrollee: ~$1.5 million (COP) annually per person
2. EPS keep **up to 15%** in "administrative expenses"
3. EPS issue **promissory notes (pagarés)** to hospitals instead of cash, with 6–18 month terms
4. Hospitals need liquidity and sell those notes to **factoring** firms (often linked to the same EPS owners) at **30–40%** discounts
5. The discount is profit for the factoring firms
6. When the note matures, the EPS pays it with new UPC funds
7. **Cycle**: the money evaporates in financial commissions and discounts

**Documented cases** (only the largest):
- **Saludcoop** (Carlos Palacino): embezzlement of **$1.4 trillion (COP)** sentenced to 15 years in prison — used UPC funds for luxury real estate projects and foreign investments
- **Caprecom/Medimás**: **> $2 trillion (COP)** fiscal hole — bankrupt state EPS that left trillion-dollar debt to hospitals
- **Hemophilia Cartel (Cartel de la Hemofilia)** (Lyons, Besaile, Pérez Ardila): **$50 billion (COP)** — created fake hemophilia patients to bill for medications that were never delivered
- **Total EPS debts to IPS**: **~$20 trillion (COP)** — public hospitals are literally financing EPS by not receiving payment, while EPS use that money to invest in real estate and financial assets

### Real solutions
- **Direct payments from ADRES to hospitals**: no intermediaries. Eliminating the EPS commission saves between 10–15% of system resources
- **Public blockchain traceability**: every peso traceable in real time, any citizen can see how much the State paid for each procedure
- **Citizen oversight with open data**: anyone can monitor system flows from a public app
- **Automatic alert system**: if a hospital receives less money than it should or a patient does not receive timely care, a public alert is triggered
- **Outcome indicators**: not measuring "how many enrollees it has" (as today), but "how many patients were cured, how long it took to treat them, how much it actually cost"

## 2. Pension Reform (Reforma Pensional)

> "Approve the pension reform and guarantee the right to a pension for all people who work and meet the requirements"

### What it seeks to achieve
That all Colombians have the right to a pension when they grow old. Today, only **1 in 4 older adults** receives a pension. The private system (AFP) has left millions without a pension after decades of contributions.

### How it would work
Unify the system into a public pay-as-you-go regime (Colpensiones). All workers contribute to a common fund from which current pensions are paid. Private pension funds (AFP) are gradually eliminated.

### Why they won't allow it
The economic groups behind the AFPs: **Grupo Aval** (Luis Carlos Sarmiento Angulo — owner of Porvenir, Colfondos, Banco de Bogotá, Banco Popular, Banco de Occidente), **Grupo Sura** (owner of Protección and Bancolombia), and **Skandia**. These AFPs manage more than **$400 trillion (COP)** in savings. The commissions they charge (~2.6% on each contributed salary) are a guaranteed cash flow regardless of whether the enrollee ever receives a pension.

### The smokescreens
- **"Colpensiones is unsustainable"** — true, but the argument hides that the private system is also unsustainable for the enrollee: AFP annuities lose **15–40% of purchasing power** year after year because when recalculating the programmed withdrawal, the capital runs out before the person dies
- **"The State will steal the savings"** — as if the AFP owners were not already stealing them with disproportionate commissions
- **"Argentina collapsed"** — Argentina collapsed because they printed money without control, not because they had a public pension system. Uruguay, Costa Rica, Chile (before Pinochet's reform) had public systems that worked

### What they are hiding
That AFPs are a business designed so that **the owners always win and the enrollee always loses**. Commissions are charged on the flow of contributions (regardless of returns), risks are borne by the enrollee (not the AFP), and when the capital runs out, the State — not the AFP — has to pay the minimum pension. It is a business where profits are private and losses are socialized.

### The money trail
1. The worker contributes monthly 16% of their salary (4% from them, 12% from the employer)
2. The AFP withholds ~2.6% in commissions regardless of fund performance
3. The rest is invested in **TES** (Colombian public debt bonds), stocks of the same economic groups (Grupo Aval, Grupo Sura), and abroad
4. When the worker retires, their balance becomes an annuity calculated based on life expectancy
5. Because returns are low and the balance is recalculated each year, the annuity progressively decreases
6. If the enrollee dies early, their balance goes to "inheritance" — but the AFP already collected commissions for decades

**Key data**:
- AFPs have charged commissions of more than **$100 trillion (COP)** over 30 years
- Only **1 in 4 Colombians** retires with a pension (the other 3 ended up with nothing)
- AFP pensioners lose **15–40% of purchasing power** year after year (Apenpri report, 2026)
- **$25 trillion (COP)** is in dispute over Decree 415 of 2026, which ordered its transfer to Colpensiones — the Council of State suspended it in May 2026
- More than **118,000 enrollees** took advantage of the transfer window to Colpensiones

### Real solutions
- **Autonomous and transparent pension fund**: a fund with its own legal status, independent of the general national budget, managed with clear rules and direct citizen oversight. Its income comes from a dedicated source (not from "general taxes" that get lost in the fiscal hole). Every peso traceable in real time, public balances, investment rules that ensure real returns without commissions for intermediaries. The fund pays for life, with room for growth, without third parties enriching themselves by managing it
- **Pillar system**: a universal basic pillar (the autonomous fund), a complementary contributory pillar (those who contribute more receive more, within the same fund), and a voluntary savings pillar (those who want to save more can do so through private instruments) — but the basic pillar does not depend on general taxes, it has its own dedicated income source
- **Eliminate flow-based commissions**: AFPs (if they exist) should charge only on returns, not on contributions
- **Total transparency on commissions and returns**: each enrollee can see in real time how much they have paid in commissions and what their pension would be

## 3. Living Wage and Workday Reduction (Salario Vital y Reducción de Jornada)

> "Guarantee a living and mobile wage and the reduction of the workday in accordance with increases in productivity. Work must have stability by constitutional order"

### What it seeks to achieve
A minimum wage that truly covers living costs, a shorter workday, and job stability so that people cannot be fired overnight.

### How it would work
Establish in the Constitution that the wage must be "living and mobile" (rising with inflation and productivity), reduce the workday to 42 hours per week (already approved from 48 to 42), and establish that dismissal without just cause should not be the general rule.

### Why they won't allow it
Business associations (ANDI, Fenalco, SAC) and large employers whose business model is based on labor flexibility: they hire, fire, and rehire at no cost. Informality suits them because they do not pay benefits, payroll taxes, or full social security.

### The smokescreens
- **"It will increase unemployment"** — the same argument used against every labor improvement. It was said when the minimum wage was created, when the workday was reduced, when maternity leave was extended. In practice, countries with stronger labor protections (Uruguay, Costa Rica, Europe) do not have higher unemployment than Colombia.
- **"Companies will become more informal"** — Colombia already has **56% informality**. The fault lies not with labor protections but with **structures designed to obscure processes**: cascading outsourcing, associated work cooperatives (cooperativas de trabajo asociado), union contracts (contratos sindicales), and perpetual service provision orders (órdenes de prestación de servicios). Systems that are not market defects but **deliberate architecture** so that companies have less civic responsibility. It is not that the State does not investigate: it is that the layers of intermediation make it impossible to trace who the real employer is and who is evading. As long as labor opacity remains functional, companies will continue to exploit it.

### What they are hiding
That labor flexibility is not about "creating jobs," it is about reducing labor costs at the worker's expense. That informality is functional: it allows a available workforce without paying benefits, without stability, without unions. That large business owners benefit from 56% of the workforce having no rights.

### The money trail
It is not a typical direct theft route, but rather **corporate savings at the worker's expense**:
- Companies that outsource services to avoid having direct employees
- Associated work cooperatives (CTA) created to evade social benefits
- Union contracts where the union becomes the employer and reduces costs
- Service provision contracts (orden de prestación de servicios — OPS) that hide a real employment relationship
- Corporate savings on benefits are estimated at **$50–70 trillion (COP) annually** that should be in the pockets of formal workers

### Real solutions
- **Real formalization**: not by lowering standards (the usual "flexibilization"), but by penalizing those who evade and reducing non-wage costs that make formality expensive
- ~~Differentiated minimum wage by sector~~ — discarded. In Colombia that would open the door for powerful sectors to push lower wages for vulnerable sectors. The minimum wage must be unique and universal as a floor, not fragmented by sectors where the weakest always lose
- **Single universal contract** with proportional benefits from day one
- **Digitalization of hiring**: any job, even informal ones, can be registered digitally with proportional benefits
- **Workday reduction without salary reduction** accompanied by investment in technology to maintain productivity

> ⚠️ **Connection to point 8 (Central Bank)**: it is not true that reducing the workday requires technology investment to maintain productivity. The 2 hours being cut were not productive — they were presenteeism, exhaustion, psychological resistance. No one produces better because their time is tracked and they are pressured more. Every country that has reduced the workday has seen productivity adjust on its own: more rested, more motivated workers waste less time. The argument about "needing cheap credit to compensate" is the same old fear. That said, if credit were cheap and accessible (as it should be), companies could also modernize; but it is not a requirement, it is an additional benefit.

## 4. Justice Reform (Reforma a la Justicia)

> "End impunity; maintain the current Constitutional Court and its selection mechanism"

### What it seeks to achieve
That justice works. That crimes do not go unpunished. That trials do not last 10 years. That the powerful pay like the poor.

### How it would work
Reform the way judges and prosecutors are elected, streamline judicial processes, stiffen penalties for certain crimes, change the structure of the Attorney General's Office (Fiscalía) to make it more effective.

### Why they won't allow it
Colombia's justice system is captured by those who should be investigated. The **Cartel de la Toga** (Gown Cartel) proved that Supreme Court magistrates sold rulings: they altered, disappeared, diverted, and delayed cases in exchange for money. **50+ arrests** including 2 Supreme Court presidents (Leonidas Bustos — 10 years in prison, Gustavo Malo — 9 years in prison). Politicians paid: **Musa Besaile** paid $2 billion (COP) to halt an arrest warrant, **Alejandro Lyons** paid $500 million (COP). The Prosecutor's Office has been used politically by every government: the DAS to persecute opponents (Jorge Noguera — 25 years in prison), Néstor Humberto Martínez's Prosecutor's Office to shield Odebrecht, Barbosa's Prosecutor's Office to selectively investigate Petro while Uribismo-aligned candidates remained unpunished.

### The smokescreens
- **"It is to politicize justice"** — justice IS already politicized. The Cartel de la Toga, the DAS wiretaps, the Odebrecht cover-up, the Prosecutor's Office as a political weapon. The problem is not that it will become politicized, it is **who controls it and for what purpose**. A justice system that punishes all wrongdoers equally — regardless of party — would be an improvement.
- **"Keeping the Constitutional Court"** — point 4 explicitly states it is maintained. But critics say it is a trap: they keep it formally but strip it of power by changing the other courts.

### What they are hiding
That the judicial system is designed so that **white-collar crimes go unpunished**. 90%+ of crimes in Colombia go without conviction. Impunity is not due to a lack of laws: it is because those who should investigate and judge are the same ones who benefit from impunity or can be bought. The country ranks **#2 worldwide** in organized crime (only behind Myanmar), and organized crime costs the country **$68 trillion (COP) annually** according to UIAF — equivalent to 4 tax reforms.

### The money trail
1. A politician or businessperson commits a crime (corruption, drug trafficking, land dispossession)
2. The case reaches the Prosecutor's Office, where the assigned prosecutor receives a bribe to shelve it
3. If the case escalates, it reaches the Supreme Court, where Cartel de la Toga magistrates alter, disappear, or delay it
4. If there is a conviction (rare), the convicted person gets house arrest or administrative benefits
5. The bribe money is paid in cash or through overpriced public contracts to lawyers or companies linked to the magistrates

**Concrete example**:
- **Musa Besaile**: paid **$2 billion (COP)** to the Cartel de la Toga to halt an arrest warrant for parapolitics
- **Alejandro Lyons**: paid **$500 million (COP)** to Luis Gustavo Moreno (anti-corruption director of the Prosecutor's Office) to divert investigations
- **Gnecco Clan**: paid **$150 million (COP)** for the same service
- **Néstor Humberto Martínez**: as prosecutor, shielded the Odebrecht investigation and was heard on audio saying a contract "is a bribe (coima)"

### Real solutions
- **Merit-based judicial career**: judges and prosecutors selected by public competitive examination with citizen oversight, no political quotas
- **Prosecutor's Office independent of the Executive** with an autonomous budget, but without dependence on the same circles of power
- **Absolute transparency of every judicial decision**: every ruling published, every case closure justified, every system expense online
- **Prosecution of all those implicated in the Cartel de la Toga** and judicial corruption networks, regardless of party
- **Real protection for whistleblowers and witnesses** — the case of Jorge Enrique Pizano (Odebrecht auditor who died "poisoned") is emblematic
- **Oral and public trials** that do not last 10 years

## 5. Public Services Reform (Reforma de Servicios Públicos)

> "End tariff speculation in electricity, drinking water, and sanitation services"

### What it seeks to achieve
That public service rates are fair, that companies cannot charge whatever they want, that water is a right and not a business. Especially in the Caribbean Coast (Costa Caribe), where energy rates are up to **40% more expensive than in Bogotá**.

### How it would work
The State sets rate caps, regulatory commissions (CREG, CRA) are reformed so they are not captured by the same business interests, and the constitutional right to water is recognized.

### Why they won't allow it
The economic groups that own public service companies: **Grupo EPM** (municipality of Medellín), **Enel Colombia** (Italian), **Naturgy** (Spanish), **Grupo Argos** (Celsia), the owners of **Air-e** (Consorcio Energía de la Costa), and the owners of water and sanitation companies. The regulatory commissions (CREG, CRA) have historically been captured by these same groups.

### The smokescreens
- **"Freezing rates will bankrupt companies"** — companies have had record profits for years. In 2024, Air-e alone had revenues of **$6.11 trillion (COP)** and owed **$1.2 trillion (COP)** to thermal plants. It is not that there is no money: the money is lost in "technical losses" (29% for Air-e) that are passed on to the user.
- **"Who will pay for the water system?"** — users pay, but rates have been artificially inflated with cost overruns, unaudited declared losses, and estimated rather than actual billing.

### What they are hiding
The model is simple and perverse: **privatization of profits, socialization of losses**.

Private companies take the profits (Air-e had revenues of $6.11 trillion in 2023). The losses — "technical," "non-technical," theft, inefficiencies — are passed on to the user through rates. The State bears the risks: when Electricaribe went bankrupt, the government had to intervene and users paid the debt. When Air-e collapsed, the "tariff option" (opción tarifaria) turned the company's debt into a citizens' debt ($2.9 trillion).

And the public discourse is always "the State is the problem," "private companies are more efficient." But those who pay for inefficiency are the citizens. Those who take the profits are the private companies. And when everything collapses, the State — which according to the discourse is the inefficient one — ends up responding with everyone's money.

It is the same pattern in health (privatize profits through EPS, the State pays the debts), in pensions (AFPs charge commissions, the State pays the minimum pension when the enrollee runs out), and in public services: **profits are private, risks are public, citizens always pay**.

The **tariff option (opción tarifaria)** (2020) is the perfect example: it created a debt of **$2.9 trillion (COP)** that users owe to the companies. Users financed the companies during the pandemic and now they are charged interest. The companies never assumed the risk.

### The money trail
1. CREG sets the tariff formula based on costs reported by the companies themselves
2. Companies declare "technical and non-technical losses" (energy theft, tampered meters)
3. CREG authorizes passing those losses on to the user's rate
4. Companies charge estimated (not actual) bills without adjustments
5. The user pays for energy they never received, for losses they did not cause, for the company's inefficiency

**Key data**:
- **Electricaribe** went bankrupt with million-dollar debts and was liquidated in 2020
- **Air-e** was intervened in 2024 by Superservicios for tariff abuses
- The tariff option accumulated a debt of **$2.9 trillion (COP)** that users must pay
- Air-e's losses are **29%** — one of the highest in the country
- Costa users pay **up to 40% more** than in Bogotá for the same service

### Real solutions
- **Independent regulators**: CREG and CRA should not be captured by the same businesses they regulate — their directors should be selected through merit-based public competition with participation from users and academia
- **Transparent tariff formulas**: no cost should be passed on to the user without prior independent audit
- **End of the tariff option**: companies should bear their own losses, not users
- **Direct subsidies for the poor**: those who cannot pay should receive an explicit subsidy from the national budget, not disguised in differential rates
- **Public energy company on the Coast**: a state operator to replace Air-e if it cannot operate without abusing users

## 6. Political System Reform (Reforma del Régimen Político)

> "End corruption and clientelism, build a true electoral arbiter and a Registry Office (Registraduría) with electoral transparency"

### What it seeks to achieve
Clean elections. That votes are counted correctly. No vote-buying, electoral migration (trashumancia), or illegal campaign financing. That whoever wins is truly the winner.

### How it would work
Reform the scrutiny system (software, hardware, counting process), change how the Registry Office (Registraduría) and the National Electoral Council (CNE) are selected, eliminate preferential voting (voto preferente), implement electronic voting with a paper trail.

### Why they won't allow it
It is not that "nobody controls" the electoral system. It is that **control is shared among those who benefit from opacity**: the traditional parties that divvy up the CNE, the companies with protected contracts at the Registry Office (Indra, Thomas Greg & Sons, Idemia), the congress members whose power depends on clientelism and preferential voting. They all have an interest in keeping the system non-transparent.

### The smokescreens
- **"It is for national security"** — that is the Registry Office's argument for not opening the scrutiny software's source code. The MOE (Misión de Observación Electoral — Electoral Observation Mission) and Fundación Karisma have repeatedly requested it. The answer has always been the same: "it cannot be done for national security reasons." Translation: if the code is public, anyone can see if it is rigged.
- **"Electronic voting can be hacked"** — the current voting system is also manipulated: 1,412 polling stations altered in 2014, E-14 forms with massive cross-outs in 2018, differences of millions of votes between the preliminary count and the official scrutiny. Electronic voting with a paper trail allows every vote to be audited both manually and electronically. Those who oppose auditing have something to hide.
- **"Electoral institutions exist and function"** — the CNE is composed of magistrates elected by the same parties they are supposed to investigate. It is judge and party. The Registry Office has been captured by a small group of companies that repeat in every tender.

### What they are hiding

**The software**: The company **Indra** (Spanish, MTA: IDR) has been the historic provider of scrutiny software in Colombia. In 2021, the Registry Office contracted the purchase of proprietary software for **$27 billion (COP)** through abbreviated selection (not public tender), citing "national security." The person who coordinated this contracting was **Alejandro Campo Valero**, IT manager at the Registry Office, who previously worked at **Indra and Thomas Greg & Sons** and was Indra's project manager for the 2017 contract.

**The hardware**: There are no electronic voting machines in Colombia. Voting is manual on paper. But digitization and data transmission are in the hands of **Thomas Greg & Sons** (the Bautista Palacios brothers — with **Noemí Sanín** and **Ricardo Lozano** on its board, and former presidents **Juan Manuel Santos** and **Andrés Pastrana** having passed through it) and **Indra**. Biometrics are handled by **Idemia** (French) with contracts worth ~$50 billion (COP), with exclusivity since 1999.

**The CNE**: 9 magistrates elected by **Congress** using the distributing figure method (cifra repartidora). The parties divvy up the CNE among themselves. In 2018, Centro Democrático, Liberal, and Conservative obtained 6 of 9 magistrates. It is "paradoxical that the judges of the parties are chosen by the parties themselves" (Semana).

**The Registry Office**: **Alexander Vega** (registrar 2019–2023, elected by the 3 High Courts, former CNE magistrate for Partido de La U) was accused of asking a candidate for **$1.2 billion (COP)** to review scrutiny results and had more than **4 hours of calls** with **Otto Bula** (Odebrecht lobbyist). He said: "Those who do not feel guarantees should not run."

**The system's selectivity**: The CNE has investigated campaigns of all presidents. Duque (2018), Santos (2014, with Odebrecht money), Zuluaga (2014, also with Odebrecht). **All were archived**. Petro is **the first president in Colombia's history** to be formally sanctioned by the CNE. The cases from all sides are documented in a separate investigation.

### The money trail (technical and political)

**The technical route (how votes are manipulated)**:

1. **Tailored bidding documents (pliegos a medida)**: software and digitization contracts are designed so that only the usual companies (Indra, Thomas Greg, Idemia) can apply
2. **Revolving door (puerta giratoria)**: former employees of those companies go to the Registry Office to coordinate contracting that will benefit their former employers
3. **E-14 forms designed to fail**: In 2022, E-14 forms had a design that made it difficult to mark closed lists (Pacto Histórico lost votes because polling judges did not understand how to mark them)
4. **Preliminary count vs. scrutiny**: there are systematic differences of millions of votes between the preliminary count (digital, informational) and the official scrutiny (manual, official). In 2022 the difference was **4 million votes** (mostly from blank votes and unmarked ballots)
5. **Captured municipal scrutiny**: scrutiny commissions appointed by the CNE who are friends of local politicians "find" votes for certain candidates
6. **Closed software**: the scrutiny code is not public. No one outside Indra and the Registry Office knows exactly what it does

**The political route (clientelism)**:

1. A businessperson or economic group wants favorable legislation or a public contract
2. They "contribute" to a candidate's campaign (in cash, unreported)
3. The candidate wins and uses their bureaucratic quota (positions in the State) to repay the favor
4. Or they manage "indicative quotas (cupos indicativos)" (budget allocations) for contracts in their region
5. Votes are bought directly in the regions: groceries, scholarships, jobs, cash
6. The electoral witness (testigo electoral) (paid by the candidate) verifies that the voter complied

**Emblematic cases — the 5 you need to know**:

**1. Ape Cuello and the Ministry of Sports ($24 billion COP)**
Alfredo "Ape" Cuello Baute, a representative to the Chamber for Cesar for nearly 20 years (Conservative Party), placed the Minister of Sports in 2024. In exchange, his political group secured work contracts worth ~$24 billion (COP) in Cesar municipalities. The projects (a soccer field in San Diego for $13.958 billion, a sports complex in La Gloria for $9.951 billion, a venue in Codazzi for $8.034 billion) were awarded to friendly contractors, including **Libardo Cuello**, Ape Cuello's nephew. Cuello controls 17 of 25 mayoralties in Cesar, including Valledupar (annual budget of $1 trillion). Sports Ministry sources say: *"When you ask what's happening, they say you have to ask Doctor Ape Cuello."*

**2. Armando Benedetti and the $480 billion COP**
Benedetti, appointed Minister of the Interior in 2025 to "grease the machinery," accelerated the delivery of ~$480 billion (COP) in budget allocations to congress members from different parties **just before the Electoral Guarantees Law (Ley de Garantías Electorales)** (which prohibits the government from delivering resources in the pre-electoral period). The strategy was to buy votes for Petro's reforms (health, pension, labor) in exchange for contracts in the congress members' regions. His arrival in the cabinet prompted the resignation of 8 ministers from the left wing. Minister Susana Muhamad said: *"As a woman, I cannot sit at this cabinet table with Armando Benedetti."*

**3. Odebrecht + Grupo Aval: the multinational bribery scheme**
Odebrecht and Corficolombiana (from Grupo Aval, Luis Carlos Sarmiento Angulo) paid **US$6.5 million** to Deputy Minister Gabriel García Morales to win the Ruta del Sol II contract (~$2 trillion COP). The money was channeled through **fictitious contracts**. Auditor **Jorge Enrique Pizano** discovered the scheme, recorded then Grupo Aval lawyer **Néstor Humberto Martínez** saying *"It's a bribe, dude (Es una coima, marica)"* — and asking him for "discretion." Pizano died of a "heart attack" on November 7, 2018; his son Alejandro died of cyanide poisoning 3 days later while reviewing the evidence. The investigation into both deaths was **archived**. Martínez resigned as Prosecutor in 2019. Corficolombiana admitted in the U.S. to paying **US$28 million in bribes** between 2012–2015. Grupo Aval paid **US$80 million in fines** to the Department of Justice. "Official 3" (Funcionario 3) remains unidentified: a high-ranking 2010–2018 government official who received US$3.4 million in March 2014. The Petro government asked the U.S. to reveal their identity in December 2025.

**4. The CNE's selectivity**: No previous president was sanctioned by the CNE. Duque 2018: archived. Santos 2014 (with Odebrecht): archived. Zuluaga 2014 (with Odebrecht): archived. Uribe 2002/2006: not even investigated. Petro 2022: first time in history the CNE sanctions a presidential campaign, with a fine of $5 billion+ COP. The details of each case are documented in a separate investigation.

**5. Grupo Aval and its US$80 million fine in the U.S.**
Corficolombiana (Grupo Aval) signed a Deferred Prosecution Agreement with the DOJ in August 2023. It admitted that between 2012 and 2015 it paid more than **US$23 million in bribes** to high-ranking Colombian officials to obtain the Ocaña-Gamarra contract (Ruta del Sol II). The fine was **US$80 million** ($40.6M to DOJ + $40M to SEC). The agreement does not exonerate individuals, but Grupo Aval, owner of Porvenir AFP, Banco de Bogotá, Banco Popular, Banco de Occidente, and Corficolombiana, is officially recognized internationally as a corrupt entity. In 2017 they had denied "with absolute certainty" having made illicit payments. Grupo Aval donated **$2.45 billion (COP) to Centro Democrático** (Uribe's party) in 2018. Senator Paloma Valencia made a fierce defense of Grupo Aval during the 2018 Odebrecht debate, calling the holding a generator of "80,000 direct jobs" and "happiness" for the country.

**Other key data**:
- **2014 elections (Congress)**: Software manipulated in **1,412 polling stations** to favor the Mira movement. It took 4 years to correct
- The electoral roll had **224,000 military personnel** (who by law cannot vote) in the 2016 plebiscite — biometrics did not filter them because the Army did not send the lists

### Real solutions
- **Public and auditable source code**: the scrutiny software must be open source. Any person, any party, any organization can review it. End of the "national security" argument
- **Electronic voting with a paper trail**: the citizen votes electronically, the machine prints a receipt that the citizen verifies and deposits in a ballot box. The electronic count is cross-checked with the manual count. Any discrepancy triggers an immediate alert
- **Preferential voting with individual accountability**: no need to eliminate it (that generates rejection). But each candidate within a list must individually report their campaign expenses, with individual caps. So that 35,584 electoral witnesses are not hidden in the party's accounting. If each candidate reports separately, vote-buying becomes detectable
- **CNE elected by public competition** with civil society participation, not by party distribution. Magistrates disqualified from having ties to parties during and after their term
- **Registry Office with a fixed non-renewable term** and merit-based selection
- **Real-time citizen auditing**: anyone can follow the scrutiny from a public app with open data
- **Mixed and transparent financing**: not banning private contributions (that is not realistic), but making them **all visible in real time**. Anyone can see who donated how much to whom, before the election (not years later when the statute of limitations has expired). With clear caps, a ban on legal entities (which already exists but is not enforced), and sanctions applied before the election, not 3 years later when the candidate is already president
- **Permanently audited electoral roll**: anyone can verify they are on the roll, that there are no deceased people, that there is no electoral migration

## 7. Corruption as the Most Severely Punished Crime

> "The crime of corruption will be the most severely punished crime in Colombia, and it will be served in the country's prisons"

### What it seeks to achieve
That corrupt individuals serve real prison time, not house arrest or with benefits. That corruption ceases to be a profitable crime.

### How it would work
Toughen penalties for corruption in the Constitution, eliminate criminal benefits for corruption-related crimes, ensure sentences are served in prison.

### Why they won't allow it
Because **every government has corruption scandals**. If corruption were truly punished, half of Congress would be in prison, several former presidents would be under investigation, and the traditional parties would disappear. Colombia's political system works because corruption is not punished.

### The smokescreens
- **"Penalties are already severe"** — if penalties are already severe (up to 20 years for embezzlement), why is no one in prison? Because the problem is not the penalties, it is that **there is no investigation, no conviction, and when there is a conviction, house arrest is granted**. Toughening penalties without fixing criminal prosecution is a smokescreen.
- **"It is punitive populism"** — toughening penalties sounds good but solves nothing if there is no investigation. Colombia has penalties of up to 20 years for embezzlement but no one serves them because the judicial system is captured. Examples: **Álvaro Uribe** (investigated for procedural fraud and bribery, senator until 2024 without conviction), **Paloma Valencia** (defended Grupo Aval in the Odebrecht scandal while receiving donations from the same group), **Abelardo de la Espriella** (congressman convicted for parapolitics, served house arrest and is still active in politics today). The problem is not the penalties, it is that they are not applied to the powerful of any side.

### What they are hiding
That **no government in Colombia has truly punished corruption among its own allies**. Uribismo protected parapolitics congress members (many were acquitted by the Supreme Court thanks to the Cartel de la Toga — **Musa Besaile** paid $2 billion to halt his arrest). Santismo protected those implicated in Odebrecht (Prosecutor Martínez shielded the investigation, he was Grupo Aval's lawyer before becoming prosecutor). The "fight against corruption" is always against the opposing side, never against one's own.

### The money trail
Corruption in Colombia does not have a single route, but the main mechanisms are:

1. **Bribes for public contracts**: the businessperson pays a commission (10–20%) to the official for contract awards
2. **Indicative quotas (cupos indicativos)**: budget allocations the government assigns to congress members for projects in their regions, in exchange for legislative support
3. **Parallel payrolls (nóminas paralelas)**: people who collect a State salary without working (family, friends, front men of politicians)
4. **Overpricing**: inflated contracts where the difference is split between the contractor and the official
5. **Royalty diversion**: mining-energy royalty resources assigned to phantom projects in the regions

**Emblematic examples of impunity, from all sides**:
- **Parapolitics (parapolítica)** (Uribismo): 60 congress members convicted for ties to paramilitaries. **Musa Besaile** paid $2 billion (COP) to the Cartel de la Toga to halt his arrest warrant. **Álvaro Uribe** was a senator until 2024 under investigation for procedural fraud, without conviction.
- **Odebrecht** (Santismo): **Néstor Humberto Martínez** was Grupo Aval's lawyer before becoming prosecutor and shielded the investigation. **Juan Manuel Santos** was president during the scandal, his campaign received money from Odebrecht via Corficolombiana. No conviction.
- **Grupo Aval** (all governments): owners of Porvenir (AFP), Banco de Bogotá, admitted in the U.S. to paying US$23 million in bribes. Paid US$80 million in fines. In Colombia, no one went to prison.

### Real solutions
- **Independent Anti-Corruption Prosecutor's Office** independent of the Executive, with its own budget and the capacity to investigate any official, including the President
- **Asset forfeiture without criminal conviction**: if someone has assets they cannot justify, they lose them. Without the need for a criminal conviction (this exists in Colombia but is excessively slow and captured)
- **Real protection for whistleblowers**: an official who reports corruption should not end up fired or threatened
- **It must apply to everyone equally**: the first test of any anti-corruption law is whether it investigates one's own. If in 2 years no government ally is in prison, the law is a farce
- **Real-time citizen auditing**: anyone can track public contract execution from an app

## 8. Central Bank Reform (Reforma del Banco de la República)

> "Economic institutions will be reformed to guarantee the true independence of the Banco de la República that coordinates anti-inflationary policy in coherence with economic growth and employment"

### What it seeks to achieve
That the Central Bank (Banco de la República) is not an autonomous entity that only focuses on inflation and does not care about employment. That it coordinates its policy with economic growth. That interest rates do not strangle the real economy.

### How it would work
Reform the Bank's Board of Directors to have a dual mandate (inflation + employment, like the U.S. Federal Reserve), include representatives from productive sectors and workers, and coordinate monetary policy with fiscal policy.

### Why they won't allow it
The financial groups that benefit from the current model: **Grupo Aval** (Sarmiento), **Grupo Sura** (Bancolombia), **Davivienda** (Grupo Bolívar), foreign carry trade investors, the IMF, and credit rating agencies (Moody's, S&P, Fitch). The Central Bank, with its high rates, **transfers income from debtors to banks**.

### The smokescreens
- **"It will generate inflation like in Venezuela"** — Japan has a Central Bank coordinated with the government, debt at 250% of GDP, and has had 30 years of 0–2% inflation and ~2.5% unemployment. The U.S. Federal Reserve has had a dual mandate (employment + inflation) since 1977 and the U.S. is not Venezuela. Inflation is not generated by coordinating monetary policy with economic policy; it is generated when the Bank prints without control to finance public spending, and that can be avoided with controls and clear rules.
- **"Central Bank independence is sacred"** — Central Bank independence was designed in the 1990s Washington Consensus. It is not a natural law, it is a political decision. Many countries have independent central banks and have inflation (Argentina). Many countries with coordinated banks have low inflation (Japan). Independence guarantees nothing.
- **"The banking families will leave"** — they are not going anywhere. The business of lending money in a country with 30% credit card rates and 25% consumer rates is too profitable. If they leave, others will come.

### What they are hiding
That high interest rates are a **massive transfer from debtors to bankers**. Banks raise funds at 9–11% (CDTs) or 0.5% (savings accounts) and lend at 18–35%. The intermediation margin (spread) is 10–20 percentage points. In 2024, the profits of the 4 largest financial groups totaled **~$12 trillion (COP)** — equivalent to 1% of GDP concentrated in banking profits, transferred directly from the pockets of debtors (households and businesses) to bank owners.

### The money trail
1. The Central Bank sets the intervention rate (today ~9.25%)
2. Banks take that rate as a reference and lend at **18–35%** (consumer credit, credit cards, loans)
3. Banks collect savings at **0.5–2%** (savings accounts) or **9–11%** (CDTs)
4. The spread (difference) is bank profit
5. Foreign investors do **carry trade**: borrow in dollars at 0–2%, convert to pesos, buy TES at 9–13%, pocket the difference
6. The government pays interest on debt (TES) to the same banks that buy them — a **closed circuit where the State pays the people's taxes to the banks**

**Who wins**: Grupo Aval (Sarmiento Angulo), Grupo Sura (Bancolombia), Davivienda (Grupo Bolívar), foreign investment funds
**Who loses**: mortgage debtors, SMEs (credit at 25%), consumers with credit cards (30%+), the State (pays more interest on debt), small savers (savings accounts at ~0.5%)

### Real solutions
- **Dual mandate**: the Central Bank should target both inflation and employment (like the Fed)
- **Democratize the Board of Directors**: not only economists from the same circle (Fedesarrollo, Uniandes, former finance ministers), but representatives of workers, regions, and productive sectors
- ~~Cap the banking spread~~ — that will never happen. Instead of imposing caps that banks always find ways to evade, open the system: allow citizens to invest directly in public debt (TES) without bank intermediaries, as happens in other countries with accessible platforms. The government should issue bonds for ordinary citizens, not only for investment funds. And clear caps for everyone — banks, individuals, companies — with transparent and verifiable regulation, not regulation written by the banks themselves
- **Strong public banking**: the State should have a commercial bank that competes with private banks and offers cheap credit
- **Capital controls on carry trade**: speculative money that enters only for rate arbitrage should pay a tax or face restrictions

> ⚠️ **Connection to point 3 (Wage and workday)**: reducing the workday does not need "technological compensation" to maintain productivity — that is a myth. The hours being cut used to be unproductive (presenteeism, exhaustion, pressure). Productivity adjusts naturally. That said, cheap credit would help companies that want to modernize, but not as a requirement for reducing the workday, rather as a parallel benefit. Labor reform and financial reform are connected, but not in terms of "if there is no credit, the workday cannot be reduced."

## 9. Preschool Education (Educación Preescolar)

> "The right to preschool education and its immediate funding is established; the budget priority in social spending must be children"

### What it seeks to achieve
That all children have access to education from early childhood. Secured funding for quality public daycare centers (jardines infantiles).

### How it would work
Elevate preschool education to a constitutional right, prioritize social spending on children, build public daycare centers, improve school feeding programs.

### Why they won't allow it
Because the budget is finite and the current fiscal priority is not children. Because school feeding contracts (PAE) are one of the country's largest corruption hotspots: **$600 billion to $1 trillion (COP) annually** is lost in phantom rations, rotten food, and cost overruns.

### The smokescreens
- **"Where will the money come from?"** — it comes from reallocating the budget. Colombia spends billions on less essential things (gasoline subsidies, corporate tax exemptions, military spending). The problem is not that there is no money, it is that children are not a priority.
- **"Early childhood already receives a lot of money"** — it receives money, but the results are poor because resources are stolen in contracting (PAE, school infrastructure).

### What they are hiding
That the **School Feeding Program (Programa de Alimentación Escolar — PAE)** is one of the main sources of regional corruption. School feeding contracts are the spoils of local politicians: they are awarded directly without competitive bidding, rations arrive incomplete or rotten, children get sick, and contractors enrich themselves. Locking children's funding into the Constitution without solving corruption in contracting is useless.

### The money trail (PAE — step by step)

The PAE manages ~**$4.5 trillion (COP) annually** to serve ~5.7 million children. But the Comptroller's Office (Contraloría) has documented that the program is a **sieve**: between 2020–2025 it found **185 fiscal findings worth $56 billion (COP)** — and that is only what it managed to audit.

**The legal flow:**
```
National Budget → MinEducation → PAE Unit → 96 Territorial Entities → Tender → Contractor → Rations
```

**The diversion occurs at 3 levels:**

**Level 1 — At the municipality (the ETC):**
1. The mayor or governor receives resources from the Nation
2. Their education secretary structures the bidding documents
3. The documents are **tailored** so that only certain contractors can qualify
4. Costs per ration are inflated (in 2025, the Comptroller's Office found **$18.6 billion in cost overruns** just in the audits it managed to conduct)
5. Contracts are fragmented to avoid public tenders

**Level 2 — At the contractor:**
1. Bills for full rations but delivers fewer (phantom rations)
2. Reduces quality: buys the cheapest, expired, or spoiled food
3. Does not pay food handlers (hires them in 4-month periods to evade benefits)
4. Invoices for supplies never purchased

**Level 3 — Money laundering:**
1. The contractor invoices through shell companies (empresas de papel)
2. Transfers money through front men (testaferros) and family members
3. Invests in properties or legitimate businesses

**Documented cases by the Comptroller's Office (2020–2025):**
- **Nariño**: $23.549 billion — non-compliance with technical annex, menu cycles without approval
- **Soacha**: $3.550 billion — costs paid exceeded what was executed
- **Boyacá**: $2.010 billion — amounts paid vs. amounts actually executed
- **Montebello (Antioquia)**: 84 consecutive days without PAE in 2023 — former mayor **Virgilio Garzón** and former health secretary **Mateo Acevedo** with charges from the Attorney General's Office (Procuraduría)

**The result**: 229,000 children affected in 2024 due to non-delivery of the program. 860,000 children in 2026 started the school year without PAE because 17 municipalities had not contracted in time.

### Real solutions

**For the PAE — a verification and accounting platform:**
- **Public real-time verification platform**: all contracts, menus, quantities, prices per ration, and deliveries visible online. Anyone can check how much the State paid for each ration, what it contains, and whether it arrived. It is not to block payments (the flow must continue), it is so that any irregularity is immediately evident
- **Citizen oversight with an app**: parent associations can report in real time whether the ration arrived, in what condition, and whether the children ate. That report carries legal weight and triggers automatic auditing
- **Live auditing**: the Comptroller's Office has real-time access to PAE data with automatic anomaly alerts (prices outside market range, unreported deliveries, mismatched menus)
- **Automatic consequences**: if the audit finds non-compliance, the contractor receives fines, is barred from public contracts for a period, and the current contract is terminated. No waiting years for the justice system to decide. Penalties must be automatic and enforceable
- **Local management with central auditing**: municipalities continue managing the PAE (they know their needs), but with a national platform that consolidates all data in real time. Contracting can be local, oversight must be national and auditable by any citizen from anywhere. Small management points, a single control eye

**For school infrastructure:**
- **Visible projects on a public map**: each school under construction with its budget, contractor, physical progress, and deadlines. Any citizen can verify if construction is advancing
- **Payment against real progress**: no million-dollar advances. Progressive payments against physical milestones verified by the educational community
- **End of unjustified additions**: if a contract deviates more than 10% from the original budget, a public alert is triggered and citizen approval is required

## 10. Higher Education (Educación Superior)

> "The right to higher education in Colombia is established"

### What it seeks to achieve
Free public university for everyone. That any Colombian can study regardless of their economic situation.

### How it would work
Elevate free higher education to a constitutional right, increase the budget for public universities, eliminate ICETEX as a barrier.

### Why they won't allow it
Because universal free access without quality control is financially unsustainable. Because ICETEX is a business that has generated a delinquent portfolio of **$2.1 trillion (COP)** and has garnished wages from thousands of debtors. Because public universities are in fiscal crisis: the **Universidad Nacional** has a deficit of **$450 billion (COP)**, the **Universidad del Valle** is on the verge of closure, the **Universidad de Antioquia** cut 42 master's programs.

### The smokescreens
- **"Quality will drop"** — quality is already low in public education. Saber test scores show a gap of **53 points** between private (298) and public (245) schools. The problem is not that more people study; it is that those who study graduate poorly educated.
- **"Free education benefits those who do not need it"** — true, but that is solved with a progressive system (scholarships for the poor, full tuition for the rich), not by denying the right to everyone.

### What they are hiding
That the argument "quality will drop if too many people enter" is used in every country to block access to higher education. Germany, Norway, and Finland have free university education and are among the best education systems in the world — because they invest what is needed, not because they restrict access.

In Colombia, the problem is not that people study "too much." It is that the system is designed so that few can access it: ICETEX lends at interest rates that indebt people for life, public universities are financially starved, and those who manage to graduate from public universities (who are the majority of the country's professionals) face a labor market that does not value them because the economy is not designed to absorb professionals, but rather cheap labor.

Out of every 100 students who enter university, only 48 graduate. That is not the fault of "people who cannot cut it." It is the fault of a system that does not support them: there is no financial support for them to finish, universities are on the verge of fiscal collapse, and low-income students have to work and study at the same time. In Norway, the State pays students to study. In Colombia, the State charges them interest to go into debt.

### The money trail
- Public universities receive transfers from the State that arrive late and inadequate
- Construction and infrastructure contracts at public universities are overpriced
- ICETEX lends to students and then pursues them with wage garnishments when they cannot pay
- Resources that should go to improving quality are lost to bureaucracy, parallel payrolls, and corruption in contracting

**Key data**:
- ICETEX's budget fell **37%** ($7.3B → $4.6B) in recent years
- **332,000 students** affected by the reduction in ICETEX loans
- University dropout rate is **52%** (only 48 out of 100 graduate)
- Public universities have chronic deficits while faculty salaries and pensions consume almost the entire budget

### Real solutions
- **Guaranteed funding with total transparency**: do not condition the budget on "outcome" metrics (that leads to fudging numbers). Funding must be guaranteed by law, as a right. But every peso must be traceable: how much each university receives, what it spends it on, how much goes to faculty salaries, how much to infrastructure, how much to scholarships, how much to activities. Transparency reveals where corruption is without needing to cut the budget as punishment
- **Progressive scholarship system**: the rich pay, the poor do not, and the middle class pays according to their ability. No more ICETEX that indebts for life
- **Strengthen technical and technological education**: SENA is Colombia's most successful model but is still seen as "second class"
- **Teachers with real benefits**: dignified salaries that match their training and responsibility, job stability without eternal temporary contracts, paid continuing education, resources to research and teach well. A motivated, well-paid teacher is the foundation of any functioning education system
- **University that prepares for a complete life**: as in Germany, the university should not only be a place to get a degree and find a job, but a space with cultural, sports, artistic, research, and exchange activities. Students who look forward to their future in the labor market and also enjoy life — education that improves their life both at work and at home. Forming complete people, not just skilled labor
- **Flexible ICETEX with citizen responsibility**: a credit system with income-based payment options (you do not pay until you are working well), forgivable for social service (working in communities, rural areas, marginalized zones) or academic performance, with flexible payment plans that do not ruin the life of whoever studied. But also with responsibility: those who can pay, do pay — so the fund is sustainable and others can also study

## 11. Peasant Right to Land (Derecho Campesino a la Tierra)

> "The right of peasants to land is established"

### What it seeks to achieve
Redistribute land. That peasants have access to the land they work. Break up unproductive large estates (latifundio).

### How it would work
Expropriate unproductive land, grant titles to peasants, implement the comprehensive rural reform of the Peace Agreement, advance the multipurpose cadaster (catastro multipropósito).

### Why they won't allow it
The **large landowners (terratenientes)** and economic groups that own vast extensions (ranchers, palm oil producers, mining-energy companies). The **regional politicians** who have illegally accumulated land. The **armed groups** (paramilitaries, drug traffickers) who dispossessed land and still hold it.

Some concrete examples of political families with land:
- **Álvaro Uribe Vélez**: his farms **El Ubérrimo** (~290 ha in Montería) and **La Carolina** (Donmatías), cattle ranching in Córdoba and Antioquia. Testimony from Salvatore Mancuso and other former paramilitary leaders indicated that his farms were used for meetings with armed groups. His brother Santiago Uribe owned the El Ocaso farm.
- **Valencia family (Paloma Valencia)**: the **Ingenio San Carlos** sugar mill in northern Cauca (Caloto, Santander de Quilichao), founded by Ignacio Valencia López. The expansion of sugarcane in the region has been at the center of the conflict with the Nasa indigenous communities. 5 km from the mill, the **El Nilo Massacre** (1991) occurred, where 21 Nasa indigenous people were killed in a land dispute over sugarcane agro-industry.
- **Gnecco Cerchar Clan** (Cesar, La Guajira): the patriarch Lucas de Jesús Gnecco Navas was a smuggler. The clan has extensive cattle ranching properties in Cesar and La Guajira, originating from drug trafficking money laundering. **Pepe Gnecco** signed the Pacto de Ralito with paramilitaries. **Kiko Gómez** (former governor of La Guajira) sentenced to 55 years. **José Alfredo Gnecco**, senator linked to the Cartel de la Toga.
- **Araújo Clan** (Cesar): **Álvaro Araújo Noguera** (former senator) convicted for parapolitics — alliance with the AUC Northern Bloc (Jorge 40) to secure political control and expand cattle ranching properties in Cesar. His father **Álvaro Araújo Castro** also investigated for ties to Mancuso.
- **Abelardo de la Espriella**: according to La Silla Vacía (January 2026), he has 19 properties registered under his companies: 3 in Córdoba (including a penthouse and lots), 5 in Atlántico (including a beachfront house and a 10-ha farm), 6 apartments in Bogotá, properties in Santa Marta and Tolima, and 2 properties in Miami (a mansion for US$5.1 million). He was a defender of paramilitaries linked to parapolitics.

Colombia has a land GINI of **0.85–0.87** — one of the highest in the world — and **1% of owners concentrate more than 50% of arable land**.

### The smokescreens
- **"It is expropriation, it creates legal insecurity"** — Colombia already has legal insecurity in the countryside because land titles are a disaster: the cadaster barely exists, public lands (baldíos) have been illegally appropriated, and land has been stolen through paramilitary violence. The "legal security" defended by large landowners is the security that no one will take away what they stole.
- **"Agrarian reform has not worked in Colombia"** — it has not worked because it was never truly implemented. Each government starts a reform and the next one reverses it. The problem is not the concept, it is the lack of political will to sustain it over time.

### What they are hiding
That land in Colombia has been concentrated **through violence**. Parapolitics was not just an alliance between paramilitaries and politicians: it was a mechanism for land dispossession. Paramilitaries displaced peasants (6.5 to 8 million hectares dispossessed), and politicians legalized the appropriation with fraudulent titles. **60 congress members** were convicted for ties to paramilitaries. The **Pacto de Ralito** (2001) was an agreement between paramilitary leaders and politicians to "refound the homeland" which in practice meant carving up the territory.

### The money trail
1. Paramilitaries displace peasants from their land using violence and massacres (Chocó, Urabá, Córdoba, Cauca)
2. Large landowners and businesspeople take possession of the dispossessed land
3. Local politicians legalize the appropriation with fraudulent titles
4. The land is used for African palm, bananas, extensive cattle ranching, illegal mining
5. The State looks the other way or is complicit (the DAS persecuting land defenders)
6. The displaced peasant arrives in the city and swells the poverty belts

**Key data**:
- **6.5 to 8 million hectares** dispossessed during the conflict
- Land restitution (Law 1448 of 2011) has restored only **~800,000 hectares** — at the current pace it would take **more than a century** to complete restitution
- **More than 60% of rural territory** is in the hands of **0.4% of owners** (about 4,000 owners control more than 40 million hectares)
- The cadaster is outdated in more than **95% of municipalities** — no one knows who owns what or what it is worth
- **Between 2 and 4 million hectares** of public lands (baldíos) have been illegally appropriated

### Real solutions
- **Real multipurpose cadaster**: update the cadaster nationwide so that large landowners pay proportional property taxes. That alone would force the sale of unproductive land
- **Asset forfeiture of dispossessed land**: the SAE (Sociedad de Activos Especiales) should deliver seized land (more than 1.5 million hectares) to peasants and victims
- **Public land bank**: the State buys land at market price (with resources from collected property taxes) and redistributes it
- **Not just land**: agrarian reform does not work without soft credit, technical assistance, seeds, irrigation, and guaranteed markets (the State buys the harvest)
- **Real titling**: the peasant must be a legal owner, not a beneficiary of a program that disappears with the next government

## Conclusion: Transparency — The Only Real Guarantee

Each of these 11 points touches a real problem. Each has its money trail, its smokescreens, its beneficiaries of opacity. And in all of them, the same pattern repeats: **the lack of transparency is not a defect of the system, it is its purpose**.

Healthcare money passes through 20 intermediaries so that no one knows where it gets lost.
Pension savings are invested in closed funds so that no one questions the commissions.
Utility rates are set with formulas no one understands so that losses are paid by the user.
Voting software is secret so that no one can audit whether the count is real.
Central Bank decisions are made in meetings where the same bankers set the rules.
School feeding contracts are awarded in municipalities without oversight so that the money disappears before reaching the children.
Land ownership is a mystery so that dispossession continues without anyone knowing who owns what.

It is not that the system is imperfect. It is that opacity **works** for those who benefit from it.

The solution is neither public nor private. It is not nationalize nor privatize. It is not more State nor less State. It is that **everything be visible**.

Not because transparency makes corruption impossible — there will always be those who try to steal. But because when anyone can consult every peso, every decision, every contract, every vote, every hectare in real time, corruption becomes an enormous risk. It can be done, but it is so easy to detect that almost no one dares.

The change is not technical, it is political. The smokescreens we analyzed here do not exist because someone made a mistake designing the system. They exist because those who today benefit from opacity — politicians, contractors, economic groups — have fought for decades to maintain them. And they will not let them go voluntarily.

This document is not intended to defend Petro's constituent assembly nor to attack it. It is intended to expose the reality of each point and to show that any real solution passes through a single principle: **that everything be visible, auditable, consultable by anyone, at any time**.

The question that remains is not technical. It is political: are we ready to demand that opacity cease to be functional? Because when the thief knows they can be discovered instantly, stealing ceases to be a profitable business.

And that is the only real guarantee that exists.
