# Álvaro Uribe Vélez — Family, Business, Ties to the Medellín Cartel

---

## 1. The Uribe Vélezes as Landowners — Colonial Origins

### The Antioquian colonization

The Uribe family is part of the **traditional Antioquian landowning elite**, with documented roots dating back to the colonization of southwestern Antioquia in the mid-19th century.

The Uribes arrived as part of the Antioquian colonization (18th-19th centuries), when families from Antioquia expanded southward, populating what are now Caldas, Risaralda, and Quindío. But while the colonization initially created small peasant properties, families like the Uribe-Restrepos accumulated significant extensions from the start.

**Andes (Antioquia) was founded on March 13, 1852** by Pedro Antonio Restrepo Escobar. The first white settler couple to establish themselves in Santa Rita (now Andes) around 1846 were **Manuel María Vélez** and **Andrea Uribe de Vélez** — aunt of General **Rafael Uribe Uribe**, one of the most important political figures in Colombian history. This connects the Uribes to the country's political history since the 19th century.

The history of Andes documents: *"A closed, traditionalist society founded on endogamy... Restrepos, Uribes, Mejías, Arangos, Aramburos quickly gained access to local power."*

### Simplified family tree

- **Great-great-grandparents (19th century)**: Settlers in Andes, Antioquia. Acquired land through allocation of state-owned vacant lands during the colonization of 1850-1860
- **Great-grandparents**: Landowners in Andes. **José Vicente Uribe** was a local political leader
- **Grandparents**: **Luis Elías Uribe González** (brother of Horacio Uribe González, father of Mario Uribe Escobar) and **Celia Sierra Velásquez**. Cattle ranchers and businessmen of southwestern Antioquia
- **Parents**: **Alberto Uribe Sierra** (1932-1983) and **Laura Vélez Uribe** (1933-1998)

### The Vélez-Ochoa connection

**Martín Emilio Vélez Ochoa** → father of Laura Vélez Uribe. His brother **Roberto Vélez Ochoa** married **Norma Ochoa Restrepo**, sister of **Fabio Ochoa Restrepo** (patriarch of the Ochoa clan). This means Laura Vélez Uribe was the niece by marriage of Fabio Ochoa Restrepo, and Álvaro Uribe has a 6th-degree affinity kinship with the Ochoa Vásquez brothers (founders of the Medellín Cartel).

The children of Roberto Vélez Ochoa and Norma Ochoa Restrepo —**Mario and Juan Diego Vélez Ochoa**— were judicially named as members of the paramilitary group **Los Erres**.

### Kinship with other traditional families

| Family | Relationship |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Restrepo** | Direct link: Pedro Antonio Restrepo Escobar founded Andes. José María Restrepo Uribe was a founding delegate. The Restrepos and Uribes were intertwined from the town's origin |
| **Mejía Correa** | Manuel Santiago Mejía (owner of Alkosto, Corbeta, K-Tronix) was a business partner of Alberto Uribe Sierra and later of Santiago Uribe in the La Carolina fighting bull ranch (1982-2015) |
| **Ochoa** | Through the Vélez-Ochoa line. Álvaro Uribe has said: "My father and Don Fabio Ochoa were friends and rivals (in horseback riding). My brothers and I participated in all the equestrian fairs competing against his children in the 60s and 70s" |
| **Echavarría** | No direct kinship, but same Antioquian elite circle (Coltejer, Grupo Sura) |
| **Santo Domingo** | Same business elite stratum, no documented direct kinship |

### The Uribe family's lands before drug trafficking

The Uribe Vélezes were landowners long before modern drug trafficking existed. Their documented lands:

| Estate | Location | Activity | Period |
| ------------------------- | ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------- |
| **La Carolina** | Santa Rosa de Osos, Antioquia | Fighting bull ranch in partnership with the Mejía Correas | Acquired before 1970 |
| **Guacharacas** | San Roque, Antioquia | Cattle ranching | Owned by Alberto Uribe |
| **Southwest farms** | Andes, Salgar, Antioquia | Agriculture and cattle ranching | Inherited from the colonization (19th century) |
| **El Ubérrimo** | Montería, Córdoba | Cattle ranching | Acquired in the 20th century |

**The "upper-middle class" myth**: Uribe's official biography (and his Wikipedia entry) presents him as coming from an "upper-middle class" family. This omits that the Uribes were part of the **Antioquian landowning oligarchy**, with lands documented since the mid-19th century and marriage and business connections to the region's most powerful families. They were not poor settlers: they were part of the local elite that controlled land and politics since the founding of Andes.

## 2. The Mejía Correas

The **Mejía Correas** were business partners of **Alberto Uribe Sierra** in the fighting bull ranch of **La Carolina**, located in Santa Rosa de Osos, Antioquia. This ranch was one of the best-known in the region, and Alberto Uribe Sierra was co-owner along with the Mejía Correa family, originally from Corbeta (Antioquia).

The Mejía Correa family was linked to the Medellín Cartel through the fine Paso horse and fighting bull business — activities where legitimate cattle ranchers and drug traffickers seeking social legitimization converged. The Ochoas, particularly Fabio Ochoa Restrepo, were central figures in this equestrian world, and La Carolina was a meeting point for this Antioquian cattle-ranching elite.

There is no complete public documentation of all the Mejía Correa family members who were part of the partnership with Alberto Uribe Sierra, but press sources from the time mention them as the original owners of the ranch that later passed to the Uribe family. **La Carolina** would later be named as an operations base for the paramilitary group **The 12 Apostles**, led by Santiago Uribe Vélez.

## 2.5 The Medellín Cartel — Full Context

To understand the Uribe family's connections, one must first understand what the Medellín Cartel was, how it operated, who made it up, and how it infiltrated Colombian politics and the economy.

### What was it?

The Medellín Cartel was not a single hierarchical organization but a **flexible coalition of cocaine trafficking organizations** based in Medellín and Antioquia. It operated approximately between 1976 and 1993. At its peak (mid-1980s), it controlled **80% of the world's cocaine trade**.

### Founders and main leaders

| Name | Role | Final status |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Pablo Emilio Escobar Gaviria** (1949-1993) | Supreme leader | Killed Dec 2, 1993 by the Search Bloc |
| **Jorge Luis Ochoa Vásquez** (b. 1950) | Number 2, leader of the Ochoa clan | Surrendered 1991, released 1996. Lives free |
| **Fabio Ochoa Vásquez** (b. 1957) | Younger brother of the Ochoas | Extradited 2001, sentenced to 30 years. Released Dec 3, 2024 |
| **Juan David Ochoa Vásquez** (1948-2013) | Elder brother of the Ochoas | Surrendered 1991, released 1996. Died 2013 |
| **José Gonzalo Rodríguez Gacha** "El Mexicano" (1947-1989) | Main partner, military chief | Killed Dec 15, 1989 |
| **Carlos Lehder Rivas** (b. 1949) | Co-founder, transportation logistics | Extradited 1987. Released 2020, resides in Germany |

### The Ochoa clan — Heart of the Cartel

The Ochoa Vásquez brothers (Jorge Luis, Juan David, and Fabio) were the children of **Fabio Ochoa Restrepo**, a prosperous cattle rancher and breeder of fine Paso horses from Antioquia. The Ochoa family was one of the most respected in the Antioquian cattle-ranching elite. Forbes included **Jorge Luis Ochoa** among the 20 richest men in the world in 1987, with an estimated fortune of **US$3 billion**.

**The kidnapping of Martha Nieves Ochoa**: On November 12, 1981, **Martha Nieves Ochoa Vásquez**, sister of the Cartel bosses, was kidnapped by M-19. In response, Pablo Escobar, the Ochoas, Rodríguez Gacha, and Carlos Lehder created **MAS (Muerte a Secuestradores — Death to Kidnappers)**, a paramilitary organization that gathered over 2,230 hitmen to search for the kidnappers. Martha Nieves was released 92 days later. MAS was the **embryo of modern paramilitarism in Colombia** and set the precedent for the alliance between drug trafficking, paramilitaries, and sectors of the state.

### Political infiltration

The Medellín Cartel actively sought to infiltrate the Colombian state:

- **Pablo Escobar was elected alternate member of the Chamber of Representatives** in 1982 for the Liberal Movement, while Álvaro Uribe was mayor of Medellín (1982)
- Escobar financed the presidential campaigns of **Alfonso López Michelsen** and **Belisario Betancur** according to statements by his son Juan Pablo Escobar
- The **"Civismo en Marcha"** movement of Escobar and the **"Movimiento Latino Nacional"** of Carlos Lehder attempted to create their own political platforms
- The **Nuevo Liberalismo** of **Luis Carlos Galán** and **Rodrigo Lara Bonilla** exposed narcopolitics. Lara Bonilla (Minister of Justice) was assassinated on April 30, 1984 on orders of the Cartel. Galán was assassinated on August 18, 1989

### Air transport

The Cartel used a network of clandestine airstrips in the Eastern Plains, Caquetá, and Putumayo. Carlos Lehder bought **Norman's Cay** in the Bahamas as an air transshipment point. The **Civil Aviation Authority (Aerocivil)** was a key institution: controlling flight licenses meant controlling cocaine routes. **Álvaro Uribe was Director of Aerocivil between 1980 and 1982**, precisely when the Cartel was expanding its air infrastructure.

### Tranquilandia — The crown jewel

The **Tranquilandia** complex (discovered in March 1984) was the largest cocaine processing laboratory ever found: 19 laboratories, 8 airstrips, 13.8 tons of cocaine seized (estimated value of US$1.2 billion). It was located in the **Llanos del Yarí**, between Caquetá and Meta — the same region where Antioquian cattle-ranching families owned properties, including the Ochoas and possibly the Uribes.

### The end of the Cartel

- **Pablo Escobar**: Killed on December 2, 1993 by the Search Bloc, supported by the DEA and **Los Pepes** (a group created by Fidel and Carlos Castaño, financed by the Cali Cartel and with DEA intelligence)
- **The Ochoas**: Jorge Luis and Juan David surrendered in 1991, were released in 1996. Fabio Ochoa was extradited to the U.S. in 2001, sentenced to 30 years, released on December 3, 2024 and deported to Colombia
- **Rodríguez Gacha**: Killed on December 15, 1989
- **Carlos Lehder**: Extradited in 1987, convicted in the U.S., released in 2020

### The legacy

The Medellín Cartel's fortune was estimated in the billions of dollars. Part was confiscated, part was lost, and part —according to multiple investigations— was **laundered through cattle ranching, land, and politics**. The connections between the Antioquian cattle-ranching elite (where the Uribes and Ochoas moved) and drug trafficking were not the exception: they were the rule.

## 2. Tranquilandia (1984) — Alberto Uribe Sierra's Helicopter

### The facts

On **March 7, 1984**, the Colombian National Police, supported by DEA agents, raided **Tranquilandia**, the largest cocaine processing complex of the Medellín Cartel, located in the Llanos del Yarí (between Caquetá and Meta). The complex had 19 laboratories, 8 airstrips, and 13.8 tons of cocaine were seized.

### The aircraft

Among the aircraft seized was the **Hughes 500** helicopter, Colombian registration **HK-2704-X**, belonging to **Aerofotos Amórtegui Ltda.**, an aerial photography company in which **Alberto Uribe Sierra** was a partner. The other aircraft were: Cessna planes HK 3064, HK 3007 (Colombian), N 3271 and YV 1085 P (foreign).

### How did it get there?

Alberto Uribe Sierra had **died on June 14, 1983**, nine months before the discovery. After his murder, the helicopter was damaged (gunfire was sprayed to disable it during the attack at La Guacharaca). **Jaime Alberto Uribe Vélez**, Álvaro's brother, gave the helicopter to **Pedro Fidel Agudelo Chávez** on February 6, 1984, as payment for a **25 million peso** promissory note that Alberto Uribe Sierra had signed and that was due on November 10, 1983. The transfer could never be legalized because 32 days later the aircraft was seized at Tranquilandia.

**Carlos Amórtegui**, Alberto Uribe's partner in Aerofotos Amórtegui, ceded his rights to the aircraft in exchange for keeping all the company's shares. According to his statement to El Tiempo in 2002, he never had any judicial requirements and lost track of the helicopter after the seizure.

### The pilot's version

Captain **Bernardo Riberos**, the sole pilot of the helicopter, stated in 2014 to Blu Radio/Semana:

- The helicopter was purchased by the Uribe family in the U.S.
- It was not true that it had been used by Pablo Escobar before Alberto Uribe's death.
- After it was disabled, **Álvaro Uribe Vélez** (then director of Aerocivil) decided to sell it for ~30 million pesos. He recused himself and delegated the procedure to another person precisely because it was his father's aircraft.
- Riberos said he did not know who bought it, but confirmed: *"Uribe's family was very close friends with the Ochoa family."*

### The controversy over Escobar

Press versions at the time claimed that the helicopter Álvaro Uribe used to retrieve his father's body belonged to Pablo Escobar. However, the **Colombian Civil Aviation Authority certified that said helicopter did not belong to Escobar** (El Tiempo, file CMS-3769706). Semana magazine (2007) and other sources indicated that Jaime Sandoval, a pilot friend of Álvaro Uribe, tried to reach La Guacharaca in a helicopter on the day of the murder but could not due to bad weather. Sandoval was later killed by the ELN.

### The fate of the aircraft

In 1995, a **faceless prosecutor** ordered the return of the aircraft to their owners and closed the investigation. The National Narcotics Directorate (Gabriel Merchán) said it never had physical possession of the aircraft and could not account for their whereabouts. The owning companies (Aviopartes Buitrago, Sociedad Sinuana de Transporte Aéreo) were not registered in the Chambers of Commerce of Medellín and Cali.

## 3. La Guacharaca — Alberto Uribe's Farm in San Roque

### Location

**La Guacharaca** estate (also called Guacharacas), in the municipality of **San Roque**, northeastern Antioquia. Alberto Uribe Sierra was murdered there on **June 14, 1983**, at age 50.

### The death according to the official version

According to the account of pilot Bernardo Riberos: Alberto Uribe arrived by helicopter with his children Santiago and María Isabel. Three armed men entered the estate. Someone shouted *"It's the FARC!"* Alberto took his pistol and said *"I'm never going to let those bandits get me. They're not taking me to the jungle."* Gunfire erupted. Alberto was hit twice: **one shot to the chest and another to the head**. The attackers looked for gasoline to set the helicopter on fire; not finding any, they sprayed it with gunfire to disable it.

The **official version attributes the homicide to the FARC-EP**. However, the FARC-EP never claimed responsibility, and in 2020 the Uribe family declared themselves "outraged" that the FARC denied the murder in their testimony before the JEP.

### La Guacharaca and paramilitarism

**Juan Guillermo Monsalve**, administrator of La Guacharaca, stated in judicial testimony that meetings were held at the estate for the founding of the **Metro Bloc of the United Self-Defense Forces of Colombia (AUC)**. According to Monsalve, the Uribe Vélez brothers (Álvaro and Santiago) were involved in the creation of this paramilitary structure.

In 1996, the partnership **Uribe Vélez Asociados Ltda.** sold half of the Guacharacas estate (Yolombó) to **Ganados del Norte S.A.**, a company owned by the brothers **Pedro David and Juan Santiago Gallón Henao** — known drug traffickers and paramilitaries, included on the OFAC Clinton list, and financiers of the Envigado Office. The deal was coordinated by Santiago Uribe Vélez with the manager of Ganados del Norte, Rodrigo Medina Vélez. The Gallón Henaos became tragically famous in 1994 as the intellectual authors of the **murder of soccer player Andrés Escobar**.

### The testimony of Rodrigo Zapata Sierra

Former paramilitary **Rodrigo Alberto Zapata Sierra** (alias "Chancellor of the AUC") testified under oath that Santiago Uribe and other cattle ranchers *"sponsored, financed, and promoted the paramilitary cause in exchange for protection of their territories and economic interests."* He said that **Ladrillera Ambalá**, a company owned by Santiago Uribe, was the vehicle for making payments to the AUC.

## 4. Roberto Vélez — The Marriage Tie to the Ochoas

### Who was he?

**Roberto Vélez** (full name: **Roberto Vélez Ochoa**) was **Álvaro Uribe Vélez's great-uncle** on his mother's side. He was the brother of **Martín Emilio Vélez Ochoa** (father of Laura Vélez Uribe, Álvaro Uribe's mother). That is: Roberto Vélez was the uncle of Laura Vélez Uribe, and therefore Álvaro Uribe's great-uncle.

### Marriage to Fabio Ochoa Restrepo's sister

Roberto Vélez Ochoa married **Norma Ochoa Restrepo**, sister of **Fabio Ochoa Restrepo** (the patriarch of the Ochoa clan). This means Roberto Vélez was **brother-in-law of Fabio Ochoa Restrepo** and **uncle by marriage of the Ochoa Vásquez brothers** (Juan David, Jorge Luis, and Fabio Ochoa Vásquez), founders of the Medellín Cartel.

### The family tree

The connection is:

- **Fabio Ochoa Restrepo** (cartel patriarch) ↔ **Norma Ochoa Restrepo** (sister)
- **Norma Ochoa Restrepo** married to **Roberto Vélez Ochoa**
- **Roberto Vélez Ochoa** ↔ brother of **Martín Emilio Vélez Ochoa**
- **Martín Emilio Vélez Ochoa** ↔ father of **Laura Vélez Uribe**
- **Laura Vélez Uribe** ↔ mother of **Álvaro Uribe Vélez**

That is: **Laura Vélez Uribe was the niece of Roberto Vélez Ochoa**, who was married to **Norma Ochoa Restrepo**, sister of Fabio Ochoa Restrepo (father of the Medellín Cartel founders).

### The children: the Vélez Ochoas

Roberto Vélez Ochoa and Norma Ochoa Restrepo **had children**, including **Mario and Juan Diego Vélez Ochoa**, who were judicially named as active members of the paramilitary group **Los Erres**. Juan Diego Vélez Ochoa was mentioned in the Southwest Bloc ruling for his paramilitary ties. They were **first cousins of the Uribe Vélezes** on Laura Vélez Uribe's side.

Thus, the **political kinship between the Uribe Vélez family and the Ochoa clan is direct**: Álvaro Uribe's mother was the niece of Fabio Ochoa Restrepo's brother-in-law.

## 5. Fabio Ochoa Restrepo — The Patriarch of the Ochoa Clan

### Who was he?

**Fabio Ochoa Restrepo** (Salgar, Antioquia, May 12, 1924 — Envigado, February 18, 2002) was a businessman, cattle rancher, and breeder of fine Paso horses. He owned the **La Margarita del 8** breeding farm and the **Las Margaritas** restaurant. He was the patriarch of the Ochoa Vásquez family.

### His children

Married to **Margot Vásquez**, he had 11 children, including the three drug traffickers who founded the Medellín Cartel:

- **Jorge Luis Ochoa Vásquez**
- **Juan David Ochoa Vásquez**
- **Fabio Ochoa Vásquez** (convicted in the U.S.)
- **Martha Nieves Ochoa** (whose 1981 kidnapping by M-19 led to the creation of **Muerte a Secuestradores**, MAS)
- **Ángela María Ochoa** (arrested for drug trafficking in Miami in 1977)

Fabio Ochoa Restrepo was arrested on two occasions: in 1976 at El Dorado Airport and in 1984 after the assassination of Rodrigo Lara Bonilla (released after 13 days).

### His sister: Norma Ochoa Restrepo

Fabio Ochoa Restrepo had a sister named **Norma Ochoa Restrepo**, who married **Roberto Vélez Ochoa**. Norma Ochoa Restrepo and Roberto Vélez Ochoa were the parents of **Mario and Juan Diego Vélez Ochoa**.

The Spanish Wikipedia records: *"Fabio [Ochoa Restrepo] was the brother of Norma Ochoa Restrepo, wife of Uribe's great-uncle, Roberto Vélez Ochoa."*

## 6. Dolly Cifuentes Villa

### Who is she?

**Dolly de Jesús Cifuentes Villa** (alias "La Meno") was the **concubine of Jaime Alberto Uribe Vélez**, Álvaro Uribe's brother. She maintained an 18-year relationship with Jaime Alberto and had a daughter with him: **Ana María Uribe Cifuentes** (born in 1980, when Dolly was 16). According to the Nuevo Arcoiris Corporation, Dolly had a second child with Jaime Alberto ten years later.

### The drug trafficking case

Dolly Cifuentes was arrested for drug trafficking in 2011, accused of money laundering and cocaine trafficking on behalf of **Joaquín "El Chapo" Guzmán** (Sinaloa Cartel, Mexico). She was **extradited to the United States** in 2012 and sentenced to **5 years in prison** after pleading guilty before a federal judge. She was released in October 2015.

### Francisco "Pacho" Cifuentes

Dolly is the **sister of Francisco "Pacho" Cifuentes**, who was **Pablo Escobar's personal pilot** in the Medellín Cartel. Pacho Cifuentes is documented in investigations by El Tiempo (July 14, 2012) and other sources as part of Escobar's inner circle.

### Ana María Uribe Cifuentes

Daughter of Dolly Cifuentes and Jaime Alberto Uribe, she was named in some press reports as wanted for extradition, but **El Tiempo confirmed that there is no formal extradition request or record of arrest or conviction against her**. She does not appear on the OFAC list of the U.S. Office of Foreign Assets Control.

## 7. Civil Aviation Authority (1980-1982) — The Licenses

### The position

Álvaro Uribe Vélez was **Director of the Colombian Civil Aviation Authority (Aerocivil)** from **March 24, 1980** to **August 7, 1982**, appointed by President Julio César Turbay Ayala. He was 28 years old when he assumed the position, following the assassination of the previous director, Fernando Uribe Senior.

### The ~200 licenses

According to investigations by journalist **Ignacio Gómez**, during Uribe's tenure approximately **200 flight licenses were granted** which, according to investigations, facilitated Medellín Cartel routes. The Spanish Wikipedia records this information citing journalistic investigations.

### Specific documented case

The most documented case is that of the **Hughes 500 helicopter HK-2704-X** of Aerofotos Amórtegui. The operating license for this helicopter was processed **in a single day** during Uribe's administration at Aerocivil, when the normal process required between 15 and 20 days, according to the **Noticias Uno** news program in 2002. Álvaro Uribe himself told El Tiempo that he **recused himself** from any action regarding his father's helicopter and delegated the procedure to another person.

### The information request

El Tiempo reported on April 23, 2002 that it had requested since March 18 of that year the list of aircraft that received airworthiness licenses during Uribe Vélez's tenure, without obtaining a response. Aerocivil claimed that compiling the information was "cumbersome because it was not computerized."

### Congressional debate (1983)

In September 1983, Minister of Justice **Rodrigo Lara Bonilla** —assassinated by the Medellín Cartel months later— questioned Aerocivil's management during a congressional debate, pointing out that it had a *"passive and negligent attitude"* toward drug trafficking. There is no direct mention of Álvaro Uribe by Lara Bonilla in that debate, according to transcripts.

### The Medellín mayoralty (1982)

Uribe was mayor of Medellín from August 26 to December 16, 1982 — barely **5 months**. He resigned amid controversy. According to Governor Álvaro Villegas, President Belisario Betancur pressured him to ask for Uribe's resignation because **Fabio Echeverri** claimed that Uribe had ties to the Medellín Cartel. Uribe has maintained that he resigned due to conflicts of interest between the mayor's office and the Medellín Public Utilities Company (EPM).

## 8. The 1991 DIA Document

### What is it?

In 1991, the U.S. **Defense Intelligence Agency (DIA)** produced a document listing alleged Colombian drug traffickers. On that list, **Álvaro Uribe Vélez appeared at #82** as a "major drug trafficker" and "collaborator of the Medellín Cartel."

### What does it say exactly?

The document —partially declassified— described Uribe as a Colombian politician with ties to the Medellín Cartel. The list included hundreds of people linked to drug trafficking, from kingpins to politicians and businessmen.

### What evidence did it have?

The DIA based its information on unevaluated field intelligence ("unevaluated intelligence"). Uribe's government always rejected the document arguing precisely that: that it was "unevaluated information" from unverified sources.

### Has it been fully declassified?

No. The document has been **partially declassified**. Organizations such as the National Security Archive have requested its full declassification. The U.S. government has never issued a formal indictment against Uribe for drug trafficking.

### Reactions from other U.S. administrations

- **Bill Clinton administration**: Did not act on the information; continued normal relations with Uribe as senator and governor.
- **George W. Bush administration**: Awarded Uribe the **Presidential Medal of Freedom** (2009), the highest civilian decoration in the U.S.
- **Barack Obama administration**: Signed the free trade agreement with Colombia and continued Plan Colombia cooperation.
- **Department of Justice**: Has never filed charges against Uribe for drug trafficking.
- **DEA**: DEA sources have stated in the media that they did not find sufficient evidence to link Uribe directly to drug trafficking, although the debate persists.

### NYT archives

Declassified New York Times archives have documented alleged ties between Uribe and the Medellín Cartel between 1992 and 1995, although Uribe has always denied these accusations.

## 9. Fabio Castillo and "Los jinetes de la cocaína"

### The book

**"Los jinetes de la cocaína"** (1987) by **Fabio Castillo** is one of the first investigative books documenting the ties between the Colombian political class and drug trafficking. Castillo was a journalist for El Espectador.

### What does it say about Alberto Uribe Sierra?

The book documents the closeness of **Alberto Uribe Sierra** with **Fabio Ochoa Restrepo** and other figures of the Medellín Cartel. According to Castillo, Alberto Uribe moved in the same social and equestrian circles as Antioquian drug traffickers. The partnership in the fighting bull ranch with the Mejía Correas and the friendship with the Ochoas are the axes of that connection.

### What documents does it present?

Castillo presented:

- Aircraft records linked to drug traffickers
- Testimony from police sources
- Documentation on the Tranquilandia helicopter
- The relationship tree between politicians, cattle ranchers, and drug traffickers

### Criticism of the book

Uribe and his defenders have called Castillo's book "sensationalist journalism" and have pointed out that it does not present direct judicial evidence against Álvaro Uribe. However, the book is considered a reference in Colombian investigative journalism and has been cited by the Supreme Court of Justice, the DEA, and multiple academic investigations.

## 10. Other Uribe Family Members in Business with Drug Traffickers

### Camilo Uribe Uribe (half-brother)

Extramarital son of Alberto Uribe Sierra with **Marta Elena Uribe Soto** (a friend of the family's daughters who visited the house). During Álvaro Uribe's government, Camilo lived in the **Palacio de Nariño** and, according to an investigation by Cambio magazine (April 2009), frequently brought in his friend **Cristian Borda Gómez** ("The Mafia's Harlequin"), a drug trafficker who was later extradited and who acted as a liaison with the twins Víctor Manuel and Miguel Ángel Mejía Múnera and with Ramiro "Cuco" Vanoy.

### Mario Uribe Escobar (cousin)

Former senator, first cousin of Álvaro Uribe. He was **convicted for parapolitics** to 90 months in prison for conspiracy to commit a crime. He was arrested in 2008 while trying to seek asylum at the Costa Rican embassy. He was a signatory of the **Pact of Ralito** (2001), the document that proved the alliance between paramilitaries and politicians.

### Juan Diego and Germán Vélez Ochoa (cousins)

Children of Roberto Vélez Ochoa and Norma Ochoa Restrepo, they are **first cousins of Álvaro Uribe Vélez**. They were mentioned in the Medellín Superior Court ruling (2016) as active members of the paramilitary group **Los Erres**. The ruling identifies them as financiers of paramilitarism in southwestern Antioquia.

### The Gallón Henaos (business partners)

The brothers **Pedro David and Juan Santiago Gallón Henao** — drug traffickers and paramilitaries on the OFAC Clinton list — purchased the Guacharacas estate from the Uribe Vélezes in 1996. Santiago Gallón Henao pleaded guilty to financing paramilitary groups. They were linked to the **Envigado Office**. One of their companies (Clamasan S.A.) received $143 million pesos from the **Agro Ingreso Seguro (AIS)** program during the Uribe government (2007).

### Summary of the family tree

| Relative | Relationship | Link to drug trafficking/paramilitarism |

## Documented Properties — The Land Expansion

### Vorágine investigation (2022)

The media outlet **Vorágine** published a 3-part investigation (#LasTierrasDeLosUribe) that identified **72 properties** under the names of Álvaro Uribe, Lina Moreno (his wife), and their children Tomás and Jerónimo Uribe. Total: **2,100 hectares** (21 km²) — equivalent to the area of the municipality of Itagüí (280,000 inhabitants). Distribution: **54 properties in Córdoba**, 11 in Antioquia, 6 in Cundinamarca, 1 in Bogotá. Peak purchase years: 1982, 1983, 2012, 2013.

### El Ubérrimo estate

- **1982**: Uribe buys the first **202 hectares** from **Jesús María López Gómez** (former governor of Córdoba, later convicted for the Pact of Ralito). Uribe was 27 years old
- **1989**: Incorporates **Agropecuaria El Ubérrimo S.A.S.**, with Lina Moreno as manager
- **Today**: ~1,311 hectares in San Carlos, Córdoba
- **Largest recorded purchase**: February 27, 2013 — 100 hectares sold by Cecilia Pardo de Jiménez in Cereté

**Main land sellers to the Uribe family**:
- **Alfonso Spath Spath**: Sold **12 properties** to Lina Moreno on May 3, 1983 (780,000 m² in Montería). His son, Carlos Spath, was arrested in 2018 for alleged paramilitary ties
- **Heliodoro José Gómez Mora and Igduara Oyola**: Sold to Tomás and Jerónimo Uribe in 1988 (the children were 7 and 5 years old)

**Assets of Tomás and Jerónimo**: 6 properties (~92 hectares), including 69 hectares purchased between June and August 2005 (Uribe was still President of the Republic).

### The Tunnel of the East and Lina Moreno's lands

The emblematic case of how political power is used to increase property values:

- **1999**: Lina Moreno and her brother **Carlos Enrique Moreno** sue the municipality of Medellín for **$2.129 billion pesos** for a 25,789 m² lot that had been "frozen" since 1985 for the Tunnel of the East project (connecting Medellín with Rionegro airport)
- **2004**: Álvaro Uribe, now president, intervenes to unblock the tunnel works. The route is redesigned, shortened, and **no longer affects Lina's land**
- **May 31, 2004**: Lina Moreno and her brother make **swaps** with Sergio Fajardo's city administration: they acquire 1,478 m² + 664 m² for $321 million pesos each lot — lands that previously belonged to other families (the Posada Restrepos)
- **2010**: The **La Asomadera Partial Plan** (Alonso Salazar's administration) allows Lina's lot to be developed. The area becomes the most exclusive sector of Medellín. The lot appreciates enormously
- **2019**: The Council of State **denies** the compensation Lina had originally claimed

**The maneuver**: Uribe as president unblocked a project that directly benefited his wife, the route was modified to avoid affecting her land, and through swaps with the state she acquired properties that appreciated exponentially. Legal, but revealing of the family's power.

### State-owned vacant lands

**Laguito 2 case** (8-hectare + 3,400 m² property in San Carlos, Córdoba):

- **March 19, 2019**: Lina Moreno (via Agropecuaria El Ubérrimo S.A.S.) files a claim for **ownership by adverse possession**, alleging they had been working the land for over 7 years
- **May 3, 2019**: The Cereté registrar, **María Stella García Pineda**, **suspends the process** because the property is an **imprescriptible rural state-owned vacant land** — it had been under "defective title" since 1960
- **December 16, 2019**: The judge orders early termination of the process: it is a state-owned vacant land. But **does not order recovery of the property**
- **August 22, 2022**: Uribe voluntarily surrenders the property to the National Land Agency (ANT)
- **February 10, 2024**: ANT delivers the 8 hectares to the **Asociación Baluarte Nacional Campesino Juana Julia Guzmán**

**Comptroller's Office report (2017)**: Ordered an investigation of El Ubérrimo for irregular accumulation of state-owned vacant lands. Detected **322 state-owned vacant land properties** in the country with irregular sales. Eleven properties under El Ubérrimo S.A.S. were on that list (San Carlos and Montería). Additionally, **490 hectares** owned by the family benefited from state investment in irrigation districts (341 of El Ubérrimo, 14 of Jerónimo, 134 of Lina), totaling **$1.41 billion pesos** between 2008 and 2010.

**Uribe's defense**: "The purchases are legal, they were awarded before Law 160 of 1994. 103 hectares were awarded to third parties before 1994 and later purchased by my family."

### Mario Uribe, "Pitirri," and paramilitary land dispossession

**Jairo Castillo Peralta, alias "Pitirri"** (former paramilitary, key parapolitics witness, exiled in Canada):
- Testified that **Mario Uribe Escobar** (Álvaro's cousin) met with paramilitaries in 1998 (Sahagún) and 1999 (Caucasia) to **buy land at low prices** obtained by the paramilitaries through dispossession
- He said: *"I know the farms one by one and I know how they acquired them. Look at Córdoba and you'll see a very important person. For example, Mario Uribe"* (Verdad Abierta, 2008)
- **Salvatore Mancuso** corroborated that Mario Uribe met with paramilitaries before the 2002 elections

**What Uribe officially declares**: His lawyer Jaime Lombana has stated that Uribe "does not have $1 abroad." The Comptroller's Office and the national tax authority (DIAN) have not conducted in-depth public investigations of his declarations. But the Vorágine investigation documents **72 properties and 2,100 hectares** that contrast with the image of "medium-sized cattle rancher" that Uribe has projected.

**The irony**: The same man who received US$2.8 billion from the U.S. for the war on drugs was listed by the DIA as a drug trafficker, his cousin was convicted for parapolitics, his brother for paramilitarism, and his family accumulated 2,100 hectares of land whose origin is questioned.

## The Parapolitics Scandal

The largest scandal of ties between Colombian politicians and paramilitary groups.

### What was it?

Between 2002 and 2013, more than **60 members of Congress** were convicted for alliances with the **United Self-Defense Forces of Colombia (AUC)**. The parapolitics scandal demonstrated that paramilitaries were not an independent armed actor: they were a **political-military project** with support from sectors of Congress, governors' offices, and mayoralties.

### The Pact of Ralito (2001)

Document signed between paramilitary leaders (Salvatore Mancuso, "Don Berna," "Jorge 40," "Diego Vecino") and politicians from the Caribbean Coast. The objective: "refound the fatherland." Signatories included Senators Miguel De la Espriella, Álvaro Araújo, Juan Manuel López Cabrales, among others. It was the written proof of the alliance between paramilitaries and politicians.

### Testimony against Uribe

- **Salvatore Mancuso**: Testified that the AUC supported Uribe's 2002 campaign. He said Uribe knew about the creation of paramilitary groups and that during his government there were meetings between paramilitaries and senior officials.
- **Juan Guillermo Monsalve** (administrator of La Guacharaca, Alberto Uribe's farm): Affirmed that the Uribe Vélez brothers (Álvaro and Santiago) were involved in the founding of the **Metro Bloc of the AUC** at **La Guacharaca**.
- **"Don Berna"** (Diego Fernando Murillo): Mentioned meetings with Uribista politicians and Uribe's knowledge of the Convivir as a paramilitary front.

### The Convivir

During his governorship of Antioquia (1995-1997), Uribe promoted the creation of the **Convivir** (Private Security and Surveillance Cooperatives). 87 were created in Antioquia. At least 13 were directly linked to paramilitary groups. The Convivir allowed paramilitaries to operate legally under the guise of private security.

### The DAS

**Jorge Noguera Cotes**: Director of DAS (2002-2005) during the Uribe government. Sentenced to **25 years in prison** (2011) for homicide, conspiracy to commit a crime, and collaboration with paramilitary groups. Uribe said: "I would put my hands in the fire for him."

**María del Pilar Hurtado**: Director of DAS (2007-2008). Sentenced to **14 years** for the wiretaps: illegal interceptions of Supreme Court magistrates, journalists (Claudia Julieta Duque), opponents (Gustavo Petro, Carlos Gaviria, Piedad Córdoba), and human rights defenders. She sought asylum in Panama (Ricardo Martinelli's government granted her asylum). She turned herself in in 2014.

**Emiro Rojas Granados**: Deputy Director of DAS. Sentenced in 2025 to **14 years** for torture of journalist Claudia Julieta Duque.

The DAS was dissolved by Juan Manuel Santos on October 31, 2011 (Decree 4057).

## The Cartel of the Robe

Scandal uncovered in 2017. A network of Supreme Court magistrates sold rulings to favor politicians —mainly Uribistas— investigated for parapolitics.

### How it operated

The magistrates used privileged information to alter, disappear, redirect, or delay proceedings. The main operator was **Luis Gustavo Moreno** (National Anti-Corruption Director of the Prosecutor's Office), who acted as intermediary between politicians and magistrates.

### Key convicted figures

| Magistrate | Position | Sentence |
| ------------------------ | -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| **José Leonidas Bustos** | Twice president of the Supreme Court | 10 years and 3 months (Feb 2026) |
| **Gustavo Malo** | Criminal Chamber Magistrate | 9 years (Aug 2021) |
| **Francisco Ricaurte** | Former President of the Supreme Court | Arrested |
| **Camilo Tarquino** | Former President of the Supreme Court | Investigated |
| **Luis Gustavo Moreno** | Anti-Corruption Director of the Prosecutor's Office | Convicted in Colombia and the U.S. |

**50+ arrests** in total, including lawyers, intermediaries, and judicial officials.

### The case of Musa Besaile

Uribista senator, investigated for parapolitics. Paid **$2 billion pesos** to the Cartel de la Toga to stop his arrest warrant. Sentenced in July 2024 to 6 years in prison.

### The political connection

The Cartel de la Toga mainly benefited politicians from the Uribista movement and the Caribbean Coast. The magistrates halted parapolitics proceedings in exchange for bribes. The network operated at least between 2014 and 2017 and protected dozens of politicians.

## Judicial Cases Against Uribe

### Case for procedural fraud and bribery

**Origin**: In 2014, Uribe denounced Senator Iván Cepeda for witness tampering. The Supreme Court investigated.

**February 2018**: The Court declines to charge Cepeda and **opens an investigation against Uribe** for procedural fraud and bribery.

**August 2020**: Magistrate César Reyes orders **house arrest** for Uribe. He spent 66 days imprisoned at his El Ubérrimo estate. He resigned from the Senate to lose jurisdiction and have the case transferred to the Prosecutor's Office (where he had more influence).

**October 2020**: Released.

**2022-2023**: Two failed attempts by the Prosecutor's Office to close the case.

**April 2024**: Prosecutor Luz Adriana Camargo calls him to **trial**.

**July 2025**: Judge 44 of the Criminal Circuit, Sandra Heredia, finds him **guilty** of procedural fraud and bribery in criminal proceedings. Sentence: **12 years in prison**. She acquits him of the charge of simple bribery.

**October 2025**: The Bogotá Superior Tribunal **overturns** the conviction and **acquits** him. Decision not unanimous (one dissenting vote). The Prosecutor's Office announces an appeal to the Supreme Court.

**Current status (2026)**: Pending Supreme Court decision. No final conviction.

### Why has he not been definitively convicted?

- The case has gone through multiple challenges to magistrates
- Changes of judge (it moved from the Supreme Court to the Prosecutor's Office due to loss of jurisdiction)
- Two failed preclusion attempts
- A conviction in the first instance overturned in the second
- The appeal can take years
- His defense has used all available legal resources

### Other investigated cases (no formal proceedings)

- **El Aro and La Granja massacres** (1997): while he was governor of Antioquia
- **False positives**: extrajudicial executions during his government (2002-2010). Thousands of young people were killed by the Army and presented as "killed in combat"
- **DAS wiretaps**: illegal interceptions
- **Yidis Politics**: Uribista congressmen who bought votes from Yidis Medina to approve presidential re-election

## Summary of the Pattern

1. **Family linked to the Medellín Cartel** (father partner in Aerofotos Amórtegui whose helicopter ended up at Tranquilandia, brother Jaime paying debts with the same aircraft, sister-in-law Dolly Cifuentes extradited for drug trafficking, cousin Mario Uribe convicted for parapolitics, half-brother Camilo linked to drug traffickers)
2. **Direct kinship with the Ochoa clan** (Laura Vélez, Uribe's mother, was the niece of Roberto Vélez Ochoa, married to Norma Ochoa Restrepo — sister of Fabio Ochoa Restrepo, patriarch of the Ochoa clan, parents of the Medellín Cartel founders)
3. **Public offices used to facilitate interests** (Civil Aviation Authority 1980-1982: licenses processed in 1 day, ~200 licenses linked to the Cartel. Convivir as governor: paramilitary front)
4. **Alliance with paramilitaries** to gain and maintain power (testimony from Mancuso, Monsalve, Zapata Sierra; founding of the Metro Bloc at La Guacharaca)
5. **State capture**: DAS at the service of political persecution (Noguera 25 years, Hurtado 14 years)
6. **Purchase of the judicial system**: Cartel de la Toga to protect allies (Bustos 10 years, Besaile $2 billion)
7. **Systematic impunity**: all judicial proceedings have collapsed, been delayed, or reversed. Convicted in 1st instance (12 years, Jul 2025), acquitted on appeal (Oct 2025), pending appeal
8. **Brothers and relatives imprisoned or convicted**: Santiago 28 years, Dolly extradited, Mario convicted. But he remains free
