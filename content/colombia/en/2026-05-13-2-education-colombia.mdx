# Corruption and Structural Problems in Colombian Education

## PAE — School Feeding Program

The PAE is Colombia's flagship school feeding program, and simultaneously one of the most chronic corruption hotspots in the education system.

**Budget**: ~$4.5 trillion pesos annually (2025). Serves ~5.7 million children.

### Landmark cases

- **La Guajira (2022-2024):** Contracts worth $57 billion pesos for school feeding awarded to inexperienced operators. The Inspector General's Office found children received spoiled food or insufficient rations. Two contractors arrested. The hunger crisis made international headlines (photos of children dead from malnutrition amid multi-million-dollar contracts).

- **Chocó (2023):** The Comptroller's Office detected $12 billion pesos in cost overruns in PAE contracts. Food never reached 23 municipalities for 4 months.

- **PAE Carousel in Bogotá (2023):** The Attorney General's Office is investigating 15 intermediaries who pocketed $48 billion pesos. The modus operandi: signing contracts with shell foundations, invoicing for food never delivered, paying kickbacks to district education officials.

- **"PAE Amazonas" Case (2024):** 7 officials arrested for embezzling $8.5 billion pesos. Children received "yogurt without cold chain" and expired cookies.

### How the money is stolen

The **Comptroller's Office** documented that between 2020-2025 it found 185 fiscal findings worth $56 billion pesos in the PAE — and that's only what it managed to audit. Identified patterns:

1. **Cost overruns (67% of findings):** inflating the meal cost structure
2. **Payments without execution records:** paying for meals never delivered
3. **Poor contract execution**
4. **Non-compliance with guidelines**
5. **Labor fragmentation:** hiring food handlers for ~4-month periods to avoid benefits

**The full pipeline**:

```
National Budget → MinEducation → 96 municipalities → Contractor → Meals
```

Diversion occurs at 3 levels:
- **At the municipality**: tailored bidding terms, direct contracting, fragmentation to avoid competitive bidding
- **At the contractor**: charges for meals not delivered, reduces quality, doesn't pay workers
- **Money laundering**: invoices through shell companies, transfers to front men, invests in property

### Most critical regions

| Region | Amount of fiscal findings |
|--------|---------------------------|
| Nariño | $23.549 billion |
| Central East | $13 billion |
| Caribbean | $11 billion |
| Soacha | $3.55 billion (highest individual case) |
| Boyacá | $2.01 billion |

**Result**: 229,000 children affected in 2024 by program non-delivery. 860,000 children in 2026 started the school year without PAE because 17 municipalities had not contracted in time.

## School Infrastructure Contracting Carousel

The **Educational Infrastructure Financing Fund (FFIE)** managed $3.2 trillion between 2015-2023 to build and improve schools.

### Cases

- **Carousel in Bogotá's Education Department (2022):** Contracts worth $120 billion pesos for maintenance of 200 schools. They were fragmented to avoid public bidding. **Unión Temporal Educativa 2022** billed $28 billion pesos for work never performed.

- **FFIE - Phantom Contracts (2024):** The Comptroller's Office revealed that out of 137 schools contracted under the Duque administration, only 47 were completed. $1.3 trillion spent on "studies and designs" that never moved to construction. Contracting companies were created days before signing contracts.

- **"Escuelas del Caribe" Case (Atlántico, 2023):** 40% cost overrun in the construction of 12 schools. Contractor **Jorge Enrique Pulido** (now convicted) paid $5.2 billion pesos in bribes to Atlántico Governor's Office officials to expedite disbursements.

## Fiscal Crisis of Public Universities

### National University of Colombia
- **2024 Deficit**: $450 billion pesos. The government transferred only 80% of the budget requested under Law 30.
- **2025 Cut**: $385 billion pesos less. 47 construction projects halted, 230 tenured professors lost between 2020 and 2024.

### University of Valle (Univalle)
- **Structural deficit**: $180 billion pesos. Teaching payroll consumes 92% of the budget.
- **2025 Crisis**: on the verge of technical closure. The government transferred $70 billion in "bailout" funds but debt exceeds $95 billion.

### University of Antioquia (UdeA)
- **Operating deficit**: $220 billion pesos. Cut 42 master's programs. $150 billion pesos in debt to the health system.

### General cause
**Law 30 of 1992** indexed the budget to CPI but does not cover enrollment growth or infrastructure replacement. The 33 public universities needed $4.2 trillion additional in 2024. They received $1.8 trillion.

## ICETEX

### How it works
State financial entity that grants educational loans. Interest rates:

| Line | Rate (2024-2025) | Beneficiaries |
|------|------------------|---------------|
| Subsidies (strata 1-2) | 0% CPI + 0% | ~80,000 |
| Tú Eliges Loan | DTF + 2.5% to 8.5% (~12-18% annual) | ~150,000 |
| Medium-term line | DTF + 4% (~16% annual) | ~50,000 |
| Long-term line | DTF + 6.5% (~19% annual) | ~90,000 |

### 2025 Crisis
The budget was reduced from $7.3 trillion (2024) to $4.6 trillion (2025), a **-37%** drop. The Ministry of Finance stopped transferring $402 billion pesos. **332,000 students affected**.

### Abuses against debtors
- **Delinquent portfolio:** $2.1 trillion. 22% of loans are over 60 days past due.
- **Asset seizures:** 12,000 lawsuits per year. Cases of wage garnishment against professionals earning 2 minimum wages.
- **Interest capitalization:** a $15 million loan becomes $45 million in 5 years.
- **Emblematic case**: a teacher from Sincelejo borrowed $8 million in 2015. By 2023 her debt was $34 million despite having paid $16 million. 50% of her salary was garnished.

### Who really benefits
**62%** of ICETEX disbursements go to **private universities** (Andes, Javeriana, Rosario, Sabana). Only 38% goes to public ones. ICETEX functions as an indirect subsidy to private universities using public funds.

## Garage Universities

Colombia has 313 higher education institutions. The Ministry has identified ~50 of "dubious quality."

### Cases

- **San Martín University Foundation (2024):** Closed leaving 12,000 students without degrees. Rector **Jairo Contreras** is a fugitive. Labor debt of $80 billion pesos.

- **"Diplomas without study" (2022):** 17 campuses raided. They issued medical specialization degrees without fulfilling study hours. 23 administrators arrested.

### Mechanism
The Ministry lacks teeth to shut them down quickly. A cancellation process can take 4-5 years, during which the institution keeps collecting tuition.

## SENA — Corruption in Training Contracts

**2024 Budget**: $5.54 trillion. Funded by payroll taxes of 2% from all companies.

### Cases

- **Territorium Platform (2021):** $30 billion peso contract with a US company created 6 months earlier. Massive failures: students couldn't log in, grades lost.

- **SENA Atlántico Region (2023):** Contracts worth $9.2 billion for courses that were never taught. 4,800 "ghost students." 3 officials arrested.

- **Fondo Emprender (2022-2024):** $22 billion transferred to "productive projects" that did not exist. 14 officials under investigation.

- **Parallel payrolls at SENA Bogotá (2024):** 230 instructors with active contracts reporting zero teaching hours. Estimated embezzlement: $6.5 billion.

## Saber Tests: The Public-Private Gap

### 2023-2024 Results

| Factor | Private | Public | Difference |
|--------|---------|--------|------------|
| Saber 11° global score | 298 | 245 | 53 points |
| Critical reading | 62 | 51 | 11 points |
| Mathematics | 64 | 48 | 16 points |
| English | 68 | 44 | 24 points |

### Criticisms of the system
1. Measure basic competencies, not real quality
2. "Teaching to the test" effect: private schools spend up to 3 months training for Saber 11°
3. No consequences for schools with low results
4. **Colombia in PISA (2022):** 52 out of 81 in Mathematics, 49 in Reading, 51 in Science

## Education Reform (Statutory Law 2025)

Petro's government filed a bill in 2024 that seeks to:

- Education as a **fundamental right** from age 3 through undergraduate studies
- Gradual investment from **4.5% to 10% of GDP**
- Eliminate for-profit status in higher education institutions receiving public funds
- Reform ICETEX: 0% interest rate for strata 1-3

**Debates:**
- **FECODE** supports it but demands eliminating "punitive teacher evaluation"
- **Private universities** (ASCUN) oppose the article eliminating for-profit status
- The fiscal cost would be an additional **$15 trillion annually**
- In 2025 it was partially sunk: the Chamber approved 14 of 38 articles

## FECODE — Teacher Unionism

**Basic facts:**
- ~270,000 members (90% unionization rate in public education)
- President: **William Velandia**
- 33 regional unions

### FECODE strikes

**2017 Strike (37 days)** — Government of Juan Manuel Santos
- Demands: reform of the General Participation System (SGP), salary adjustment, progressive bonus, creation of 3 preschool grades, full-day schedule with conditions, school as a territory of peace
- The government said "there's no money" for weeks
- Resolved on June 16, 2017 with an agreement: a bonus reaching 15% of base salary, FECODE on the SGP commission
- What the right doesn't say: the constitutional reforms of Uribe (2007) and Pastrana (2001) had diverted ~$130 trillion from the education budget. It wasn't that "there was no money" — it had been taken away

**2018 Strike (18 days total)** — Government of Iván Duque
- Not a continuous strike but 24-48 hour walkouts throughout the year
- Main cause: the Duque government did not comply with the 2017 agreements
- Duque came to power promising a hard line against unionism

**2019 Strike (28 days)** — Part of the November 21 National Strike
- Not just FECODE: called by the National Strike Committee (CUT, FECODE, students, indigenous groups, labor unions)
- Broad causes: tax reform, financial holding company (privatization of state enterprises), pension and labor reform, criminalization of protest, non-compliance with the Peace Agreement, murder of social leaders
- FECODE demanded: compliance with 2017 agreements, $4.5 trillion for higher education, repeal of the financial holding company law
- Duque ordered border closures, curfew in Cali, dry law in Bogotá. The ESMAD caused the death of **Dilan Cruz** (18 years old) on November 23

**2023 Strike (15 days)** — Government of Gustavo Petro
- Demands: solution to problems with the Teachers' Social Benefits Fund (FOMAG) — failures in medicine delivery
- Petro, being a pro-union government, reached an agreement. A technical committee was created
- Iván Cepeda (Pacto Histórico) criticized FECODE for being "too conciliatory" with an allied government

### Teacher evaluation

**What traditional governments propose:**
- Public evaluation of results
- Performance measured by standardized tests
- Possible dismissal for poor performance
- "Meritocracy"

**What FECODE says:**
- The evaluation ignores socioeconomic conditions: poverty, malnutrition, violence, lack of infrastructure
- If we evaluate student outcomes without evaluating the conditions in which they learn, the blame will always fall on the teacher working in the poorest area
- The real fear: public evaluation is used to justify cuts, concessions, and labor flexibilization

**The concrete fact:** Colombia has two teacher statutes — **Decree 2277 of 1979** (seniority and degree-based career ladder) and **Decree 1278 of 2002** (periodic evaluation, fewer guarantees, more flexible). Decree 1278 was pushed by Uribe to divide the teaching profession and weaken FECODE. It worked: today there are teachers under 2277 and 1278 with different conditions, creating internal tensions.

### Teacher salaries

Colombia pays salaries in the upper average range for Latin American public school teachers (base salary: ~$2.5M - $6.5M depending on rank). But there are problems: gap between statutes, FOMAG deductions, bonuses that don't consolidate into base salary, and much worse conditions in rural areas.

### Internal corruption in FECODE

- **FOMAG**: In 2024-2026, the Superintendence of Health announced audits for alleged irregularities. Fiduprevisora (the administrator) was singled out for **$260 billion in irregular payments**, including transfers to deceased individuals.
- **ADE Bogotá**: The Comptroller's Office investigated the District Teachers' Association for alleged diversion of **$3.2 billion** in union dues.
- **Jairo Rodríguez**: FECODE treasurer arrested in 2023 for embezzlement.
- Reports of union dues being used to finance political campaigns and internal clientelism.

### Criticism of FECODE from the right

The Uribe, Santos, and Duque governments have criticized FECODE for:
- **"Permanent strikes"** affecting millions of students
- **Opposing teacher evaluation** — former Minister María Fernanda Campo said: "FECODE defends mediocrity"
- **Politicization of the union** — its participation in the 2021 National Strike

**What they don't say**: FECODE's strikes are not arbitrary. Every strike has been over non-compliance with signed agreements. The 2017 strike was because the Santos government refused to increase the education budget after Uribe and Pastrana had cut it by $130 trillion. The 2018 strike was because Duque ignored the agreements. The 2019 strike was part of a national strike against reforms that flexibilized labor and privatized public enterprises. The underlying problem is not the protests but the lack of political will to fund public education.

**The solution offered by the right**: it was not to improve education, but to weaken the union. Uribe created a parallel statute (1278) to divide teachers. Duque tried to criminalize protest. The recipe was the same: labor flexibilization, punitive evaluation, progressive privatization (concession schools), and real budget cuts.

### Criticism of FECODE from the left

- **Iván Cepeda** (Pacto Histórico senator, Petro government): criticized FECODE for being **too conciliatory** with the Petro government — they called off the 2023 strike with agreements he considered insufficient, they failed to leverage having an allied government to achieve structural reforms.
- **Abel Rodríguez** (former FECODE president): proposed the union convene a "grand educational mission" to reform the system. He argued FECODE has focused too much on salary issues and neglected the "pedagogical revolution."
- **Alternative sectors**: criticize that FECODE prioritizes defending the statute and salaries over educational quality and pedagogical transformation.

**What they don't say**: FECODE has been the only real check on the privatization of education in Colombia. Without the union, concession schools would have replaced public education long ago. Cepeda's criticism is valid but also reflects the tension between a left-wing government that needs to govern and a union that has historically had to fight for the bare minimum. And the internal corruption (FOMAG, ADE) is real and must be investigated, but it does not invalidate the union's struggle.

## Concession Schools in Bogotá

### What they are
A model implemented in Bogotá since 1999 (mayoralty of Enrique Peñalosa). The District builds public schools but outsources their operation to **private operators** (universities, family compensation funds, foundations) through 15-year concession contracts. Operators receive a per-student payment and must meet quality targets.

**Data:**
- 51 concession schools in Bogotá (2025)
- ~120,000 students
- Annual budget: ~$500 billion
- Operators: Universidad Pedagógica, Colsubsidio, Cafam, Minuto de Dios

### What the discussion should be
Defenders (Peñalosa, Education Department) say the model allowed building schools in areas where the State didn't reach. But that argument is hollow: if the State builds the school and puts up the money, it has already arrived. The real discussion shouldn't be public vs private, but:

- Are students receiving quality education?
- Do teachers have decent working conditions?
- Is there transparency in the use of resources?
- Is the model sustainable without depending on whether the operator wants to make a profit?

That should be the same regardless of school type.

### What really happens
- Operators receive **$1.8 million per student** vs $1.4 million in district public schools
- But Saber 11° scores are only **3% higher** than public schools — 28% more budget for 3% more results
- Concession schools can **select students** through entrance exams — public schools take everyone
- Concession teachers are not part of the public teaching corps: they have private contracts with fewer guarantees, weakening FECODE

The model is not "educational innovation." It is **profit privatization with public risk**: the State builds, the operator manages, the operator keeps the profit, and if something goes wrong, the State is liable.

## Coverage vs Quality: Dropout and Graduation

**Gross higher education coverage:** ~55% (2024). Up from 24.5% in 2002 to ~55%.

**Net coverage** (correct age 17-21): barely **32%** — 2 out of 3 college-age young people are not studying.

**Annual university dropout rate:** ~9.5% in public, ~6.5% in private.

**Graduation rate:** out of every 100 students who enter, only **48 graduate**. Causes: lack of resources (52%), academic problems (22%), pregnancy/motherhood (10%).

| Level | Enrolled | Graduate annually | Rate |
|-------|----------|------------------|------|
| University | 2.5 million | ~350,000 | ~14% |
| Technical/Technological | 800,000 | ~90,000 | ~11% |
| SENA | 9.8 million | ~1.5 million | ~15% |
